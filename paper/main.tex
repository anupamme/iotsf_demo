\begin{abstract}
Modern IoT attackers increasingly use AI to generate stealthy network traffic
that mimics benign behaviour, defeating traditional intrusion detection
systems (IDS) which rely on statistical thresholds, signatures, or shallow
anomaly detectors.  We propose \ours{}, a two-stage defence framework that
fights AI-powered threats with AI-generated defences.  In the first stage,
we use Diffusion-TS, a decomposition-aware diffusion model, to synthesise
\emph{hard-negative} attacks---traffic sequences that are statistically
similar to benign IoT flows yet embed adversarial patterns---while enforcing
protocol compliance constraints (Modbus, MQTT, CoAP).  In the second stage,
we adapt Moirai, a universal time-series forecasting foundation model, for
anomaly detection by fine-tuning it with a combined NLL and supervised
contrastive loss.  The contrastive component, trained on hard negatives,
pushes benign and attack embeddings apart in a shared representation space.
Extensive experiments on the \dataset{} benchmark across four attack types
at three stealth levels (85\%--95\% similarity) demonstrate that \ours{}
substantially outperforms five traditional baselines and four deep-learning
competitors, reducing the false-positive rate while maintaining high
detection rates even against the stealthiest attacks.  We release code,
synthetic datasets, and a reproducible evaluation harness.
\end{abstract}
