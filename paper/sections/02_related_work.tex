\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Traditional IoT IDS.}
Early anomaly detection for IoT relied on hand-engineered thresholds
and signature databases~\cite{snort,zeek}.
Statistical methods (Z-score, IQR) flag individual flow features that
deviate from a learned normal distribution; Isolation
Forest~\cite{liu2012isolation} extends this to multivariate settings.
Ensemble approaches combine multiple detectors to improve
precision~\cite{garcia2014empirical}.  While effective for gross
anomalies, all these methods share a critical weakness: they are
calibrated on known, high-magnitude attacks and fail to detect
stealthy, low-amplitude perturbations.
The \dataset{} dataset~\cite{neto2023ciciot} provides a comprehensive
benchmark that we use throughout this work.

\textbf{Deep-learning anomaly detection.}
Autoencoder-based methods reconstruct benign traffic and flag
samples with high reconstruction error~\cite{an2015variational}.
USAD~\cite{audibert2020usad} extends this with an adversarially
trained dual-autoencoder.  TranAD~\cite{tuli2022tranad} applies
context-aware Transformers with a focus-score mechanism to amplify
anomalous regions.  The Anomaly Transformer~\cite{xu2022anomaly}
introduces association discrepancy: anomalous timesteps attend
differently than their Gaussian neighbourhood prior.
PatchTST~\cite{nie2023patchtst} demonstrates that patch-based
Transformers capture long-range dependencies in time series and
adapts naturally to reconstruction-based anomaly detection.
We include all four methods as competitive baselines.

\textbf{Generative models for data augmentation in security.}
GANs have been applied to synthetic attack
generation~\cite{lin2018idsgan}, but mode collapse and training
instability limit diversity.  TimeGAN~\cite{yoon2019timegan} improves
temporal fidelity via a supervised adversarial framework.
Diffusion-TS~\cite{zhang2024diffusionts}, which we adopt, achieves
state-of-the-art quality on multivariate time-series generation
through a decomposition-aware architecture (trend $+$ seasonality).
To our knowledge, ours is the first work to apply diffusion-based
generation for IoT hard-negative mining with protocol constraint integration.

\textbf{Time-series foundation models.}
Large-scale pre-training on diverse time-series corpora has produced
general-purpose forecasters with strong zero-shot
capabilities~\cite{das2024timeseries,ansari2024chronos}.
Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} is trained on over 27~billion time points
and supports probabilistic multi-variate forecasting via patch-based
Transformers.  MOMENT~\cite{goswami2024moment} and
TimesFM~\cite{das2024timeseries} offer complementary architectures.
We select Moirai because its probabilistic output (confidence intervals)
naturally maps to an anomaly score: timesteps falling outside the
predicted confidence interval are candidates for anomalies.

\textbf{Contrastive learning for anomaly detection.}
Supervised contrastive learning~\cite{khosla2020supcon} has been
applied to tabular anomaly detection~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} and
time-series classification~\cite{eldele2021ts2vec}.
Our CombinedLoss is most closely related to~\cite{reiss2021panda},
which combines reconstruction objectives with contrastive loss for
image anomaly detection.  We adapt this principle to IoT time-series
by pairing NLL-based forecasting with SupCon fine-tuning.
