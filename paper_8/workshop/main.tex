\title{Drift $\neq$ Damage: Representation Change Does Not Reliably Identify When Encoder Adaptation Helps}

\author{
  Anonymous Authors
}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Continual- and transfer-learning practice reads the large representational change that fine-tuning a time-series foundation model (TSFM) induces as forgetting or damage. We show this reflex is misleading, and that the distinction it elides is our thesis: \textbf{drift is observed; whether encoder adaptation \emph{contributed} must be intervened upon}.
We ask what drift cannot answer---did \emph{allowing} encoder adaptation contribute to the gain?---with a \textbf{frozen-encoder intervention} (B$-$D) behind a downstream-only \textbf{pretrained-capability inclusion criterion}. \textbf{Our headline finding is methodological: B$-$D must be scored on \emph{held-out} windows.} Read on the windows model selection already uses---the natural protocol, and the one we previously reported---it systematically flatters the frozen encoder. Re-scored on a disjoint test split, three of the \textbf{19 cells} we previously reported \textbf{reverse sign} (e.g.\ Chronos/ETTh1 $+6.8 \to -39.2$~pp), the criterion reclassifies two others, and a third backbone added here reverses a fourth---all four the same way. Across 23 cells B$-$D spans $-39$ to $+62$~pp. Second, across four TSFMs (Moirai, Chronos, TimesFM, MOMENT), drift \emph{magnitude} (CKA) does not reliably determine the \emph{direction} of that effect---not even after backbone identity is absorbed as a fixed effect rather than by subsetting (App.~\ref{app:crosscell}). Third, the intervention exposes a reproducible regime \textbf{within Moirai} that we name \textbf{pretrained-capability degradation}: in six cells meeting the criterion ($R^2_\text{task}$ $0.50$--$0.83$) full fine-tuning leaves the model \textbf{worse than not fine-tuning at all} ($+8.7\%$ to $+46.1\%$ above zero-shot, in every seed) while a frozen encoder \emph{improves} it ($-3.5\%$ to $-16.0\%$); a criterion fixed before any test window is consulted selects the same six. Those six are among the \textbf{least}-drifted we measure (CKA $0.67$--$0.89$), while the cells adaptation helps most sit at $0.09$--$0.63$: at this resolution the drift reflex would have pointed the wrong way. We establish this regime's \textbf{existence, not its prevalence}---the third backbone contains no such cell. Two analyses from an earlier version are withdrawn and support no claim here (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{abstract}

%% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

A Moirai encoder fine-tuned on ETTh2 can have CKA with its pretrained self fall to ${\sim}0.3$---a ${\sim}70\%$ representational shift---and yet forecast \emph{better} on the very same task.
If drift were a proxy for damage, this should not happen.

Practitioners who fine-tune released TSFM checkpoints inherit a reflex from continual learning: measure drift, read large drift as catastrophic forgetting, and reach for preservation methods (LoRA, EWC, L2-SP)~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc,hu2022lora,li2018l2sp}.
This bundles three implicit claims---drift implies forgetting, forgetting implies harm, drift should be minimized---and the bundle comes apart on TSFMs. Our ``$\neq$'' is insufficiency, not independence: drift does not \emph{reliably imply} harm, because \textbf{downstream utility depends on the functional role of the changed representation}---not \emph{how much} a backbone drifts but \emph{what kind} of change it is, which one scalar cannot distinguish.

We contribute a within-model dissociation (Fig.~\ref{fig:dissociation}), a capability check over four architecturally distinct TSFMs with controlled encoder interventions on the cells that pass it, and a diagnostic chain needing only released weights and target data (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain}). On scope: the check spans four backbones, the 23 intervention cells come from Moirai, Chronos and TimesFM, and MOMENT supports no claim here. Three hypotheses, separated throughout. \textbf{H1} does drift predict utility? \textbf{H2} does drift predict how much \emph{allowing} encoder adaptation contributes? \textbf{H3} does fine-tuning actually destroy a demonstrated pretrained capability? We give evidence against reliable monotonicity for H1 and H2; H3 yields \textbf{pretrained-capability degradation}, a reproducible regime \emph{within Moirai}. Ordering these needs one prior result---\emph{where} the intervention is scored---which is why that comes first below.

Our claims, strongest evidence first. \textbf{(1) B$-$D must be scored out-of-sample}---read on the windows model selection uses, it flatters the frozen encoder, four cells reverse sign, and the gate licensing them is vulnerable the same way. \textbf{(2) CKA drift does not reliably determine the direction of the encoder-adaptation contribution} under our evaluated protocols. \textbf{(3) A downstream-only inclusion criterion} marks where pretrained capability is demonstrated; it is not a validated detector, and we show what it would have selected prospectively. \textbf{(4) The reading is predictable without the frozen control} within Moirai, from condition~B's own held-out forgetting---a partly algebraic rule that does not transfer (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Setup and the Diagnostic Framework}
\label{sec:method}
%% ============================================================

We study four TSFMs: \textbf{Moirai}~\citep{woo2024moirai} (encoder-only, MoE), \textbf{Chronos-T5}~\citep{ansari2024chronos} (encoder--decoder), \textbf{TimesFM}~\citep{das2024timeseries} (decoder-only, $d_\text{model}{=}1280$), and \textbf{MOMENT}~\citep{goswami2024moment} (encoder-only).
Data is univariate ETT~\citep{zhou2021informer}/M4 (lookback 96; $h{=}24$ cross-backbone, $h{=}96/192$ Moirai sweep), plus ILI (weekly influenza, 7 features, lookback 104, $h{=}24$).
\textbf{Each arm trains through its own objective and head.} The 14 Moirai cells---which include all six degradation cells---and ILI train with Moirai's \emph{own} NLL objective through its \emph{own} distribution head (\texttt{param\_proj}) and are scored through its native sampling path (median of 20 samples); no regression head is attached, and the same holds for the 4 TimesFM cells, which train and are scored through TimesFM's \emph{own} point/quantile head (App.~\ref{app:crossbackbone}). Only the 5 Chronos cells attach a common MSE head to encoder states, because its generative decoder admits no matched frozen-versus-adapted comparison otherwise---hence limitation~(iii). What departs from the released recipes is the \emph{data} pipeline (one dataset at a time, fixed lookback, no LOTSA packing), not the objective, head or inference path; the cost is that MSE magnitudes are \emph{not} comparable across arms, so B$-$D too is read within a backbone.
Two conditions run throughout: \textbf{B}~(full fine-tuning) and \textbf{D}~(frozen encoder, head-only). Forgetting\% $= 100 (\text{MSE}_\text{FT} - \text{MSE}_\text{ZS}) / \text{MSE}_\text{ZS}$; negative means improvement over zero-shot.

Three quantities must not be conflated: \textbf{representation drift} (CKA), \textbf{adaptation contribution} (B$-$D) and \textbf{downstream utility} (forecast MSE)---CKA measures the first, B$-$D the second, neither is the third. One inclusion criterion, one detector, one intervention:

\textbf{Pretrained-capability inclusion criterion.}
$R^2_\text{task} = 1 - \text{MSE}_\text{ZS} / \text{MSE}_\text{Linear}$: does the released model beat a lookback-96 linear baseline by ${>}20\%$?
It asks whether there is capability worth preserving---not whether fine-tuning is worthwhile---and needs only released weights and the practitioner's series.
\textbf{Its role is inclusion, not prescription: it decides which cells make an intervention interpretable, and we do \emph{not} claim it is a validated deployment-time decision rule}---it identifies cells with measurable zero-shot capability; we do not show that consulting it improves fine-tuning choices. A gate-fail means no demonstrated zero-shot advantage \emph{under this protocol}, not that the pretrained representation carries no transferable information.

\textbf{CKA drift.}
Linear CKA~\citep{kornblith2019cka} between pretrained and fine-tuned backbone on mean-pooled encoder outputs over identical held-out 192-timestep windows; flagged at $<0.95$ and compared only \emph{within} a backbone, never across architectures. Crucially, \textbf{CKA measures representational change, not functional change}, which is why the chain does not stop here. We are not arguing that CKA is a poor similarity measure: the claim is against \emph{equating} its magnitude with downstream damage, not against the measurement.
Both cutoffs are \emph{operational detectors within this protocol}, not semantic thresholds: pooling moves the CKA magnitude but not the flag (App.~\ref{app:thresholds},~\ref{app:pooling}).

\textbf{Frozen-encoder intervention (B$-$D).}
Condition D measures how much encoder adaptation \emph{contributes} beyond head-only training; B$-$D is the resulting forgetting gap in percentage points, negative meaning adaptation helps, and it is the only step that manipulates the encoder rather than observing it. Freezing applies to encoder weights only---input/output projections stay trainable---so D is not representation-frozen (its CKA runs $0.38$--$1.000$). \textbf{B$-$D therefore measures whether encoder adaptation was \emph{necessary} for the gain---not that the representation change CKA measures carried it.} Freezing also removes optimization degrees of freedom and gradient pathways, so no single mechanism is isolated by this contrast.

\textbf{Detection: where B$-$D is scored decides its sign.}
B$-$D compares two trained models and inherits their model selection: B early-stops on the evaluation windows and a frozen head's regularizer is tuned there, so scoring B$-$D on those windows---the natural protocol, and the one we previously reported---is no generalization estimate. We re-scored all 19 cells on a chronologically disjoint test split, fitting and selecting no model on it (App.~\ref{app:heldout}; dispersions are ${\pm}1$~SEM unless a table states \emph{std}). Three cells \emph{reverse sign}---Chronos/ETTh1 $+6.8{\pm}2.9 \to -39.2{\pm}13.9$, and both Moirai/ETTh2 cells ($+3.8, +7.2 \to -22.3, -20.8$)---while others merely soften. The error runs one way: read on its selection windows, B$-$D \emph{flatters the frozen encoder}. TimesFM's four cells, scored on both splits from the same runs and so never re-scored, reverse a fourth the same way ($+0.2 \to -7.1$): four of 23, all one direction.
\textbf{And so does the gate---which makes cell \emph{inclusion} retrospective.} Recomputed there, two cells that pass on the selection windows fail out of sample (Chronos/Electricity $0.14$, Moirai-Large/ETTh2 $0.13$; ILI fails on both sides, App.~\ref{app:ili}), and the values are no longer bimodal, so the $20\%$ threshold is not margin-free (App.~\ref{app:thresholds}). Applying the criterion to cells whose test split we have seen makes the six below \emph{retrospectively} identified, so we re-ran the definition behind a \textbf{prospective} gate from validation windows alone: it is the more permissive of the two ($22$ of $23$ cells, not $20$) and selects the \textbf{same six} (App.~\ref{app:heldout}).
\textbf{Intervention: pretrained-capability degradation (H3).}
Our headline empirical finding is a reproducible regime \emph{within Moirai} in which full encoder
fine-tuning \emph{destroys} the pretrained advantage the criterion demonstrated while a
frozen-encoder control preserves it. Three clauses must hold, the last two in \emph{every} seed: the
cell gate-passes, B ends up worse than the un-tuned model, and D improves on it.
Six cells qualify. Moirai-Small/Weather is clearest---it beats a lookback-96 linear baseline by
$53\%$/$80\%$ at $h{=}96$/$192$, yet B lands $+37.3\%$/$+46.1\%$ \emph{above} zero-shot error while D
reaches $-15.8\%$/$-16.0\%$---and Moirai-Base and Moirai-\emph{Small} repeat it on ETTh1 at smaller
magnitude (B $+8.7\%$ to $+39.5\%$ vs D $-3.5\%$ to $-14.7\%$, gates $0.50$--$0.72$), so the regime
spans two model sizes (App.~\ref{app:heldout}).

\textbf{Robustness.} Freezing the input projections too (condition~H, CKA exactly $1.0$ by
construction) keeps the sign on all six cells and moves the magnitude by at most $5.8$~pp
(App.~\ref{app:strictfreeze})---``freezing wins'' is not the input projection re-fitting. It does split the second clause: \textbf{6/6} cells beat zero-shot
under D, \textbf{5/6} under the stricter H (Moirai-S/ETTh1 $h{=}192$, forg$_\text{H}{=}{+}1.2$). Chronos/M4 (gate $84.5\%$) anchors the stable end (App.~\ref{app:based}).
\textbf{A third backbone.} TimesFM-2.5 under its \emph{own} recipe---no head attached, so both
forgetting terms are clean---gate-passes on four datasets, and \textbf{none is a degradation cell}: B
improves on zero-shot in all four, B$-$D runs $-7.7$ to $-1.4$. The regime is Moirai's, at horizons where TimesFM
gate-\emph{fails}: scope, not generality. It also gives the measurement claim its sharpest form: condition~D's
transformer stack is \textbf{bit-identical} after training ($\ell_2$ drift exactly $0.00$) and still
reads CKA $0.38$--$0.89$---all of it the input tokenizer---so low CKA cannot by itself mean a damaged
encoder (App.~\ref{app:crossbackbone}).

\textbf{Scope: drift does not flag the degradation cells.}
The six sit at CKA $0.67$--$0.89$ while the cells adaptation helps most (Chronos/ETTh1 $-39.2$, ILI
$-38.6$) sit at $0.17$ and $0.46$---the reflex would have cleared the damaging cells and flagged the
safe ones. Nor does it order the held-out column \emph{within} a
backbone---$\rho{=}+0.495$ over 14 Moirai cells, and the slope stays unpinned when backbone identity
is absorbed as a fixed effect instead ($n{=}23$, App.~\ref{app:crosscell}). Within one model they come apart directly (Fig.~\ref{fig:dissociation},
App.~\ref{app:sweep}).

%% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{What this is and is not.} Two claims, and only one is prospective: the sign reversals are a
generalization result, since no model is fitted or selected on test, while the degradation regime is
\emph{identified} using test data and so establishes existence, not prevalence or detection accuracy
(App.~\ref{app:heldout}). Runs were not blinded, thresholds were set after inspecting results, and two pre-specified
predictions were falsified.

\textbf{A cheaper proxy, where it holds.} B$-$D $\equiv$ forg$_\text{B}-$forg$_\text{D}$ and Moirai's
frozen arm barely moves, so condition~B's own forgetting agrees in sign with B$-$D in 12 of 14 cells
($\rho{=}{+}0.956$)---one run, no control, but partly algebraic and it does not transfer
(App.~\ref{app:predictor}).

\textbf{What follows for practice.} Check that the checkpoint beats a trivial baseline on your series
before worrying about preserving it; do not read drift as damage, because CKA is an observation, not
a verdict; and if you compare a frozen encoder against an adapted one, score it on \emph{held-out}
windows---on selection windows the comparison favours freezing, and four of our cells reverse.

\textbf{Limitations.} Related work: App.~\ref{app:related}. \emph{(i)} Two analyses from an earlier version are \textbf{withdrawn}---the probe axis, and our largest reported effect, whose frozen control had never been trained; B$-$D shares no machinery with either (App.~\ref{app:probes},~\ref{app:pooling},~\ref{app:ili}). \emph{(ii)} Cells are unequally powered (3--10 seeds) and \textbf{not independent}---datasets, horizons and sizes recur---so effective $n$ is far below 23 and cross-cell statistics are descriptive; directional cells are not findings. \emph{(iii)} Chronos carries no per-condition forgetting ($\ddagger$ in Table~\ref{tab:regimes}). \emph{(iv)} The six degradation cells are Moirai-only on two datasets: existence, not prevalence. \emph{(v)} Condition~D leaves input projections trainable (App.~\ref{app:strictfreeze} bounds that cost). \emph{(vi)} Every data pipeline departs from the released recipe (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Within-Model Dissociation Sweep}
\label{app:sweep}
%% ============================================================

Every point shares a backbone, a dataset, a horizon and a protocol; only the fine-tuning sample size
varies. Increasing $n$ moves drift monotonically while the task outcome moves non-monotonically:
CKA falls steadily from $0.949$ at
$n{=}500$ to $0.518$ at $n{=}10$k, while forgetting goes $+14.0$, $+13.4$, $-2.9$, $+6.6$,
$-5.3\%$. The best cell in the sweep is the \emph{most}-drifted one.
The $n{=}5$k variance ($+6.6\%$, std $10.0$, 7 seeds) is bimodal---4 seeds near $-0.1\%$, 3 at
$+15.5\%$ with an epoch-1 overshoot---so we treat that point as unresolved rather than as evidence
for either direction.

This is a \emph{dissociation, not an intervention on drift.} Sample size is an uncontrolled causal
variable: raising it changes supervision, optimization trajectory and overfitting, not drift alone.
The sweep therefore shows that drift and outcome need not move together within one model; it cannot
show that drift itself is causally inert. The frozen-encoder experiment is the intervention, and it
is what the paper's claims rest on.

\section{The Probe Evidence We Retract}
\label{app:probes}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{This appendix records a retracted result.} It reports the probe statistics as we originally
computed them, unchanged, so that they can be audited against the re-analysis in
Appendix~\ref{app:pooling} that withdraws them. Nothing in the main results depends on this
section; we keep it rather than deleting the numbers.

Table~\ref{tab:probestats} gives every probe used in the asymmetry test on Moirai-Small/ETTh2, with dispersion and seed counts.
We classified each $\Delta R^2$ by an effect-size heuristic---whether the mean exceeds one seed-level SD---not by a significance test.
Four auxiliary probes decrease; one is indistinguishable from zero; none increase.
The signature as stated---trained increases \textbf{and} no auxiliary increases---is satisfied without relying on the forecast-48 result in either direction. What it is \emph{not} robust to is the operating point, which is the subject of Appendix~\ref{app:pooling}.

\textbf{Why these five.}
The auxiliary set is chosen to vary along two axes a single probe family would confound: \emph{what} is decoded (low-order statistics vs.\ forecasts) and \emph{how} it is decoded (linear Ridge vs.\ gradient-boosted).
The delta1 probe matters specifically because its Ridge version is misleading: the zero-shot Ridge baseline on next-step deltas is deeply negative ($R^2 \approx -2.3$), so any encoder shift inflates $\Delta R^2$ (Ridge gives $+1.04{\pm}0.22$).
A GBM probe establishes a genuine positive floor ($R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059$) and on that floor decodability \emph{falls}.
We report the GBM result for this reason, and note that this is the only positive-floor untrained readout available to us---a reviewer-visible weakness rather than a strength.
Five probes cannot establish that no specialization occurs along an unmeasured axis (calibration, periodicity, tail behaviour); the asymmetry test is evidence, not proof.

\section{ILI Cell Detail}
\label{app:ili}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{The gate value we previously reported for this cell does not reproduce.}
Earlier versions quoted gate-pass ${=}57\%$ (ZS MSE $1.183$, Linear MSE $2.748$) from a run set that
was not retained. Recomputed from the runs this paper actually reports (10 seeds,
\texttt{results/v40\_ili\_heldout}) with the same definition and the same per-window normalisation,
the gate is $+0.139$ on validation (ZS $1.893$, linear $2.206$) and $\mathbf{-0.033}$ on the test
segment (ZS $2.479$, linear $2.664$): \textbf{ILI gate-fails on held-out windows}, and we withdraw the
$57\%$. The per-feature picture is why the mean lands where it does---five of seven features are
positive but only two clear $0.20$, and \textsc{num.\ of providers} is $-0.86$ because that series is
close to a straight line on the test segment, which linear extrapolation fits almost exactly. ILI is
not a degradation cell and no claim in \S\ref{sec:results} rests on its gate; what changes is that it
can no longer be cited as a moderate-gate cell, and the ``falsified prediction'' below is
correspondingly weaker evidence---a heavily restructuring encoder on a cell with \emph{no}
demonstrated zero-shot advantage is much less surprising than one on a moderate-gate cell.

\textbf{Status of this cell.}
On ILI the encoder restructures heavily (CKA $0.455{\pm}0.008$) and full fine-tuning improves by $-80.0{\pm}0.6\%$ (10/10), falsifying a prediction specified beforehand ($0.90$--$0.95$ CKA expected). We previously reported B$-$D${=}{-}80.7$~pp here, i.e.\ that encoder adaptation supplied \emph{all} of the gain. That is withdrawn. The frozen control it was measured against had never been trained: the script assigned the zero-shot MSE as the fine-tuned MSE and wrote a forgetting of exactly $0.0$, so the ``$0.0\%$ in all 10 seeds'' was one constant, not ten measurements. \section{Full Preservation Spectrum}
\label{app:preservation}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{What this appendix is for.} It is a \emph{secondary}, observational result: it shows that
raising representational similarity does not raise utility, which is evidence against the reflex's
prescriptive half. It is \textbf{not} evidence for this paper's causal claim---that rests entirely on
the frozen-encoder intervention B$-$D in \S\ref{sec:results}, because these methods preserve
representations by different mechanisms and so are not a controlled manipulation of one variable.
The main text quotes $h{=}96$ only.
Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum} gives both horizons and all methods, including the NLL+SupCon variant.
These methods preserve representations by \emph{different mechanisms}---penalty (L2-SP, EWC), reparameterization (LoRA), and complete freezing---so CKA is a common observational axis, not a common intervention, and at $n{=}4$--$7$ the correlations here are descriptive only. We stress that \textbf{LoRA does well}: the paper's claim is that drift magnitude fails to \emph{order} these methods, not that preservation is a bad idea or that fine-tuning should be avoided.
\textbf{A correction to an earlier version.} The figures we previously reported for this spectrum
were not matched to their baseline. The mitigation runs use $n{=}1000$ while the full-fine-tuning
reference we quoted against them was an $n{=}500$, 10-seed run, and the mitigation forgetting
percentages divided by a single shared zero-shot ($0.1259$) rather than each run's own, which sits
between $0.1274$ and $0.1310$; that inflated every one of them by $1.2$--$3.4$~pp. Corrected---all
at $n{=}1000$, $h{=}96$, each run against its own zero-shot---full fine-tuning is CKA $.936$ at
$+13.4\%$, against L2-SP $\lambda{=}0.01$ ($.939$, $+16.0$), L2-SP $\lambda{=}0.1$ ($.965$, $+13.4$),
EWC $\lambda{=}1000$ ($.972$, $+14.9$) and LoRA ($.979$, $-9.2$).

The claim this displaces is the strong one: it is \emph{not} true that every weight-anchoring
configuration forecasts worse. L2-SP $\lambda{=}0.1$ raises CKA substantially ($.936 \to .965$) and
lands on full fine-tuning to within $0.02$~pp. What the corrected spectrum shows is the weaker and
more useful thing: moving up the CKA axis buys nothing predictable---one anchoring setting is worse,
one is a dead heat, a third is worse again, and the reparameterizing method is far better while
being the most CKA-preserving of the four. Representation preservation does not predict utility
here. At $h{=}192$ no $n{=}1000$ full-fine-tuning baseline exists in our runs, so we report that
horizon's methods without a matched reference rather than against the $n{=}500$ one.

the best epoch we \emph{assert} the frozen modules are bit-identical to the released checkpoint; the
run aborts rather than mislabel a condition.

Two design points matter for reading these cells. First, no head is attached: the loss is TimesFM's
own output structure (MSE on the mean channel plus pinball loss on the nine decile channels the
released \texttt{output\_projection\_point} emits), and zero-shot, B and D are all scored through the
same native path. \textbf{Per-condition forgetting is therefore a clean measurement here}, unlike
Chronos (limitation~iii), so this arm re-tests the forg$_\text{B}$ rule on a second backbone.
Second, the released torch API cannot be trained through---\texttt{module.decode()} is wrapped in
\texttt{torch.no\_grad()} and returns detached NumPy---so training uses a differentiable
re-implementation of the prefill path. That path is legitimate only because at $h{=}24$ the
autoregressive loop runs $(\texttt{max\_horizon}-1)//o = 0$ times with $o{=}128$: one forward pass
\emph{is} the whole forecast. \texttt{verify\_native\_path()} asserts agreement with
\texttt{model.forecast()} on real windows before any training (max relative deviation
$6.5\times10^{-7}$ on MPS, $0.0$ on CPU), so a re-implementation error cannot reach the paper
silently. The continuous quantile head ($27.85$M) rewrites only non-median channels at inference and
receives no gradient, so condition B updates $203.44$M of the $231.29$M parameters.

pretrained forecasting head, so the zero-shot term the criterion needs does not exist without
fitting a probe---which would make the ``zero-shot advantage'' an artifact of the probe. MOMENT
therefore supports no claim in this paper.

Chronos cell sits at CKA $0.09$--$0.23$ and every Moirai cell at $0.40$--$0.97$, so pooling those

the honest complication: TimesFM's four cells span $0.25$--$0.56$ and therefore \emph{straddle} the
Chronos and Moirai ranges instead of forming a third stratum, so the pooled correlation is not
purely a between-backbone artifact. It is also the group where drift is largest relative to
outcome---four cells at CKA $0.25$--$0.56$ whose B$-$D all sit within $8$~pp of zero---so adding it
pushes the pooled $\rho$ \emph{up} while adding cells that individually contradict the reflex.
Neither observation licenses reading the pooled row as evidence; both are why we print it.

We report it anyway because a reader can compute it in a minute, and because the honest position is
that our central claim survives on a protocol restriction rather than because no correlation can be
found in the numbers. Within Moirai, the only group with enough cells and enough CKA range to test,
$\rho{=}+0.495$ with a CI that includes zero---but at $p{=}0.072$ this is marginal, and we do not
claim CKA is uninformative, only that it does not reliably order the intervention at this
resolution. Within TimesFM, the one other backbone with a clean forgetting measurement, $\rho$ is
$+0.400$ on 4 cells with a CI spanning almost the whole range---too few cells to test anything, and
reported so that the arm's failure to help is on the record rather than omitted. The $\ell_2$ drift
result is cleaner: it orders nothing even pooled ($\rho{=}-0.301$, CI $[-0.731, +0.249]$), which is
the more robust form of the claim that observational summaries of fine-tuning do not substitute for
running the intervention.

\section{Held-Out Re-Scoring of the Intervention}
\label{app:heldout}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{Why this appendix exists.} Every B$-$D we previously reported was read on the evaluation
windows that model selection already used: condition B early-stops on them, and for the converged
Chronos runs the frozen head's ridge penalty is chosen on them. That makes B$-$D a controlled
within-cell contrast but not an estimate of generalization. Each training script in this work
already carves a chronologically disjoint test split and then discards it; here we score it.

\textbf{Two kinds of evidence, and only one of them is prospective.} These are separate claims and
the paper does not conflate them. \emph{(A)~The protocol finding is genuinely out of sample.} No
model is fitted or selected on the test split, so the sign reversals in \S\ref{sec:results} are a
generalization result. \emph{(B)~The degradation regime is a retrospective discovery.} Its
definition includes a gate that we \emph{recompute} on the test split, so the six cells are
identified using data we have already looked at. That establishes existence; it is not a prospective
validation of the criterion, and it says nothing about how often the regime occurs or how well the
criterion would detect it in advance.

\textbf{What a prospective gate would have selected.} Because (B) is the weaker claim, we measure
it rather than merely caveat it. \texttt{scripts/gate\_all\_cells.py --split val} recomputes
$R^2_\text{task}$ from validation windows alone---each arm's own stored validation zero-shot MSE
over that arm's own validation windows, with no test data touched anywhere---and
\texttt{cell\_matrix.py --gate-split=val} then re-applies the full definition with the forgetting
terms left test-side, so only the \emph{inclusion} decision changes. The prospective gate is the
\emph{more permissive} of the two, admitting $22$ of $23$ cells against the test-side gate's $20$
(the two it additionally admits, Chronos/Electricity $0.30$ and Moirai-Large/ETTh2 $0.60$, both read
\emph{adapt}; ILI fails on both sides at $+0.139$ and $-0.033$). It selects the \textbf{same six
degradation cells}, at validation gates $0.32$--$0.62$ against $0.50$--$0.83$ on test. The regime is
therefore not an artifact of gating on the split it is measured on---though it remains a
retrospective characterisation in every other respect, and we do not claim the criterion is a
validated detector.

\textbf{A correction to the gate itself.} Auditing the above surfaced a window mismatch in the
Moirai arm of \texttt{gate\_all\_cells.py}: the linear denominator was built with an extended
lookback of $2\times96$ for every cell, while \texttt{finetune\_forecasting.py} evaluates with
$\text{lookback}+h$. The two agree at $h{=}96$ and diverge at $h{=}192$, where the numerator and
denominator of $R^2_\text{task}$ were being averaged over different target windows. Corrected, six
$h{=}192$ gate values move (largest, Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $0.72 \to 0.55$; Moirai-Small/Weather
$0.80 \to 0.83$). \textbf{No cell changes gate status and the degradation set is unchanged}; the
values in Table~\ref{tab:regimes}, Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} and App.~\ref{app:thresholds} are
the corrected ones.

\textbf{Protocol.} Train on train, select on validation, report on test. Nothing is selected on the
test windows: condition B's early stopping is untouched, and the ridge penalty is still the
argmin of the \emph{validation} grid, merely re-scored at that $\alpha$ on test. The Chronos runs
share one fixed test subsample across seeds and one seeded zero-shot reference per dataset, since
B$-$D divides by a shared denominator. Condition B was re-trained because only the encoder, not the
head, had been checkpointed; its validation losses reproduce the published values to
$0.008$--$0.039\%$ (MPS run-to-run float noise), and all six frozen-encoder ridge optima reproduce
exactly and remain interior.

\textbf{Which cells count as degradation.} The definition---gate-passing, forg$_\text{B}>0$ in
every seed, forg$_\text{D}<0$ in every seed---selects exactly six of the 23 cells, and
\texttt{cell\_matrix.py} applies it programmatically so the list cannot be curated by hand. The four
TimesFM cells are put through the same test and none passes it. One
further cell (Moirai-Small/ETTh2, $h{=}192$) meets the inequalities on \emph{means} but its harm
term is only $+2.0$ with 5/10 seeds positive, so it is excluded and reported as directional. An
earlier version of the paper listed four, labelling the two Moirai-Small/ETTh1 cells
\emph{preserve} by hand despite their satisfying every clause.

Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} is emitted by \texttt{scripts/cell\_matrix.py}, the same function that
computes the body's numbers, rather than transcribed. An earlier version of this appendix listed
four cells while Table~\ref{tab:regimes} already quoted held-out values for all 19---the appendix had
not been updated when the re-scoring was extended, and the mismatch reads as the body overclaiming.
Generating it is the only fix that stays fixed. All dispersions here and in the body are SEM; the
earlier table mixed SEM with SD, which made two cells look better separated than they were.

appeared to \emph{help} were the ones that did not survive it.

\textbf{A consequence for the closed-form argument.} We previously justified the ridge head as a
closed-form optimum that admits no epoch budget. It is an optimum \emph{on the validation windows}.
Out of sample a weak validation-selected $\alpha$ transfers poorly, and the protocol-matched
early-stopped AdamW head---which agrees with ridge to ${\sim}1$~pp on validation---beats it on test
(Chronos/ETTh1 seed 43: $2.505$ against $1.957$). We pre-committed to reporting the estimator
validation preferred, which is ridge in every seed, with AdamW as a sensitivity; taking the better
of the two on test would reintroduce exactly the fault this appendix removes. Under the AdamW leg
the ETTh1 reversal is smaller but intact.

\textbf{ILI.} This cell also required its condition~D to be re-run from scratch, for a different
reason: the frozen control had never been trained (App.~\ref{app:ili}). With a real D its

held-out windows. Its D reaches CKA $0.76$--$0.90$, not the $1.000$ the earlier version asserted,
because the projections feeding the encoder stay trainable.

\textbf{Scope and denominators.} Every cell in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} is scored on a
chronologically disjoint test split. The zero-shot test denominator matters more than it looks:
B$-$D divides by a shared zero-shot term, and each run stores one measured on \emph{validation}, so
using it against a test-window difference mixes scales---on Moirai-Small/ETTh2 $h{=}96$ the test
zero-shot is $0.492$ against validation's $0.129$, which inflates held-out B$-$D by ${\approx}3.8
\times$. The test references come from three run sets
(\texttt{results/v41\_zs\_test}, \texttt{v43\_moirai\_matrix/*/condition\_A},
\texttt{v39\_moirai\_zs\_test}), 13 in total, and the Chronos cells share one fixed test subsample
and one seeded zero-shot per dataset across seeds. Two rows of Table~\ref{tab:regimes} in an earlier
version---Chronos/M4 and the TimesFM $h{=}96$ pair---have no paired B/D artifacts with test MSEs
retained and are not among the 23; they appear only in App.~\ref{app:crossbackbone}. The four
TimesFM $h{=}24$ cells are a different matter: they were run for this revision and scored on test
windows from the outset, so nothing about them was re-scored.

\section{The Strict-Freeze Control}
\label{app:strictfreeze}
%% ============================================================

Condition~D freezes the encoder's own weights but leaves the input projection
(\texttt{in\_proj}, $247{,}680$ parameters) and \texttt{mask\_encoding} ($384$) trainable alongside
the distribution head (\texttt{param\_proj}, $2{,}956{,}800$). What the encoder \emph{receives}
therefore keeps changing, so its output is not a fixed function of the input---which is why D's
measured CKA is $0.76$--$0.90$ on ILI and $0.97$--$0.99$ on Moirai-Base rather than $1.000$. A
reader can reasonably object that ``freezing wins'' in the degradation cells is partly the input
projection re-fitting rather than the encoder being held still.

Condition~\textbf{H} removes the objection: \texttt{in\_proj} and \texttt{mask\_encoding} are frozen
too, so only \texttt{param\_proj} trains and the encoder's output is pinned by construction
(measured CKA $1.0000$ exactly, as it must be). B$-$H is then a clean read of what allowing
\emph{any} encoder-side change was worth, and B$-$D versus B$-$H separates encoder adaptation from
input re-fitting. We fixed the reading rule before running: the claim is that the sign and reading of
B$-$D survive strict freezing, so B$-$H is reported beside B$-$D on every cell, and divergence on any
cell is reported as that cell's gap being partly input re-fitting rather than being buried.

\textbf{Result.} All six degradation cells and ILI were run; nothing was dropped. Measured CKA is
$1.0000$ everywhere, confirming the construction. \textbf{The reading survives on every cell}: B$-$H
keeps B$-$D's sign in all seven, and the magnitude changes by $-5.8$ to $+0.5$~pp---so at most
$5.8$~pp of a gap as large as $62$~pp is input-projection re-fitting. On ILI, where adaptation helps,
strict freezing makes adaptation look \emph{more} valuable ($-38.6 \to -43.6$), which is the same
direction: holding more of the model still costs more where the encoder was earning its keep.
B$-$D is averaged over the seeds condition H ran, so Moirai-Small/ETTh1's entries here are 3-seed
values against the 5-seed values quoted in Table~\ref{tab:regimes}.

\input{../tables/strictfreeze.tex}

\textbf{One clause does not survive, and it is not the headline one.} ``Degradation'' has two parts:
fine-tuning ends up worse than the un-tuned model, \emph{and} freezing improves on it. The first part
is a property of condition B and is untouched by this control. The second is condition~D's, and on
Moirai-Small/ETTh1 $h{=}192$ the strictly-frozen model does \emph{not} beat the un-tuned model:
forg$_\text{H}={+}1.2{\pm}0.4\%$, positive in all 3 seeds, where forg$_\text{D}$ is
$-4.6{\pm}0.6\%$. On that cell the head alone cannot recover the zero-shot forecast; D's improvement
came partly from re-fitting the input projection. The other five cells keep both parts
(forg$_\text{H}$ from $-0.6\%$ to $-12.8\%$), and B$-$H stays positive on all six, so
``freezing beats full fine-tuning'' holds throughout while ``freezing also beats not fine-tuning''
holds strictly on five of six. We report the cell rather than re-defining the term around it.

\section{CKA Pooling Robustness, and the Re-Analysis that Retracts the Probe Axis}
\label{app:pooling}
%% ============================================================

A consequence worth stating plainly: because the magnitude of CKA depends on how the
representation is constructed, this paper's claims are strongest where they are
\emph{ordinal and interventional}---which cells drift, and what freezing costs on each---and
weakest if read as statements about absolute CKA values. The drift \emph{flag} is stable
under pooling; the number attached to it is not.

\textbf{Pooling robustness.}
CKA is reported on mean-pooled encoder outputs throughout. Because mean-pooling discards
token-level structure, we recomputed whole-encoder CKA on the same ten fine-tuned ETTh2
encoders ($n{=}10$k, $h{=}96$, condition~B) both mean-pooled and token-flattened, over 500
held-out 192-timestep windows.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Representation & CKA (mean$\pm$SD, 10 seeds) & Seed range \\
\midrule
Mean-pooled (as reported) & $0.561{\pm}0.157$ & $0.328$--$0.840$ \\
Token-flattened & $0.732{\pm}0.036$ & $0.685$--$0.797$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two things follow. The \emph{qualitative} drift flag is robust: both values sit far below the
0.95 threshold, so every cell keeps its assignment. The \emph{magnitude} is not: token-level
similarity is substantially higher and far less seed-variable ($\pm0.036$ vs $\pm0.157$), so
part of the drift reported as ${\sim}0.52$ is an artifact of pooling. We regard this as
consistent with, rather than damaging to, the thesis---it is a further reason not to read a
single scalar as a measure of damage---but CKA magnitudes should be read as pooling-dependent.

\textbf{Probe protocol, recovered and verified.}
The probe pipeline was not documented in enough detail to re-run, so we reconstructed it from
source and verified it against the published numbers. The protocol is: the encoder input window
is \textbf{192 steps} (extended lookback $=$ lookback $+$ horizon), the probe \textbf{trains on
the first 300 validation windows and is evaluated on the first 200 test windows}, the target is
the next 96 steps across \textbf{all seven features} flattened, and the probe is
Ridge($\alpha{=}1.0$) on mean-pooled encoder outputs. Under exactly this protocol the trained
probe reproduces:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Trained 96-step probe & $R^2(\text{PT})$ & $R^2(\text{FT})$ & $\Delta R^2$ (10 seeds) \\
\midrule
Published & $-6.90$ & $-6.23$ & $+0.67{\pm}0.23$, 0/10 neg \\
This reproduction & $-6.9043$ & $-6.2364$ & $+0.6679{\pm}0.2262$, 0/10 neg \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The headline $\Delta R^2$ therefore reproduces to four significant figures from the released
checkpoints. Three protocol details each move the result by an order of magnitude and are easy
to get wrong: using the 192-step window rather than a 96-step context, evaluating across the
validation$\rightarrow$test boundary rather than within one split, and using all seven features
as the target rather than the target channel alone. Substituting a within-validation split, for
instance, moves $R^2(\text{PT})$ from $-6.90$ to $-0.13$.

\textbf{A transparent re-analysis, and what it costs us.}
Rather than keep searching for the target normalization behind the published auxiliary floors,
we specified a protocol with every knob fixed in advance and report what it gives
(\texttt{scripts/probe\_transparent.py}). Windows: the same 500. Features: mean-pooled encoder
output, standardized on probe-train. Probe: Ridge with $\alpha$ chosen by 5-fold CV on the
\emph{pretrained} encoder's probe-train over $\log_{10}\alpha \in [-3, 6]$, then that same
$\alpha$ reused for all ten fine-tuned encoders so the two are never tuned apart. Statistic:
$\Delta R^2$ per seed with a percentile bootstrap 95\% CI over the ten seeds (10{,}000
resamples). Two additions decide the outcome:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Two splits.} $T$ is the published temporal split (train on validation, test on
test). $R$ randomizes the same 500 windows into 300/200. The pair separates decodability from
the validation$\rightarrow$test level shift.
\item \textbf{A permuted-target noise floor.} The identical pipeline run on 20 independently
shuffled copies of each target. Any real $\Delta R^2$ must exceed the spread the pipeline
produces when the target carries no information at all.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The published $\Delta R^2$ is a regularization artifact.} Holding the split at $T$ and
sweeping $\alpha$, the trained probe's $\Delta R^2$ decays monotonically---$+0.668$, $+0.271$,
$+0.066$, $+0.034$, $+0.005$, $+0.000$ at $\alpha = 1, 10, 10^2, 10^3, 10^4, 10^5$---while
$R^2(\text{PT})$ stays between $-6.90$ and $-5.23$ throughout. The effect is largest exactly
where the probe extrapolates worst, and CV on the training split selects $\alpha{=}10^6$, at
which it vanishes. Under split $R$, where the trained probe attains $R^2(\text{PT}) = +0.68$,
$\Delta R^2$ is at most $+0.017$ at any $\alpha$ and is negative at the CV-selected value.
There is no configuration in which the trained probe both generalizes and increases by
anything near $+0.67$. (Standardizing the targets is provably \emph{not} a knob: with an
intercept, Ridge is equivariant under affine target scaling, and the sweep confirms the rows
are identical to the last digit.)

\textbf{This contradicts our own stated criterion.} Section~\ref{app:probes} rejects the
Ridge version of the delta1 probe precisely because ``the zero-shot Ridge baseline is deeply
negative ($R^2 \approx -2.3$), so any encoder shift inflates $\Delta R^2$.'' The headline
trained probe sits at $R^2(\text{PT}) = -6.90$---further into that regime than the probe we
disqualified---and we did not apply the criterion to it. Applied consistently, it disqualifies
the published trained/auxiliary comparison as well.

\textbf{What survives.} Under the one split where all seven probes generalize, every
$|\Delta R^2|$ is $\le 0.011$: the trained probe decreases ($-0.0042$, 10/10 seeds), mean and
trend decrease, lag-1 autocorrelation \emph{increases}, and variance, lag-24 and spectral
centroid are indistinguishable from zero. That is not the claimed asymmetry; it is closer to no
detectable change in any direction. We therefore \textbf{retract the probe axis} as unsupported by
a protocol we are willing to defend, and we have left the original numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:probestats} rather than deleting them so the two can be compared. The
frozen-encoder intervention is unaffected: it shares no machinery with the probes, manipulates the
encoder rather than fitting a readout on it, and is measured in forecast MSE rather than $R^2$.
It is where the paper's weight now rests, and the two inversions it supports
(\S\ref{sec:results}) were never probe-dependent.

\section{Predicting the Reading Without the Frozen Control}
\label{app:predictor}
%% ============================================================

An earlier version listed this as open; within Moirai it is not. Condition~B's \emph{own} held-out
forgetting agrees in sign with B$-$D in 12 of the 14 Moirai cells (both exceptions directional), and
the matched within-checkpoint contrast is sharper: Moirai-Base forgets under B on ETTh1 at both

It fails on Chronos ($\rho{=}{-}0.100$, limitation~iii), and on TimesFM, the other arm where
forg$_\text{B}$ is a clean measurement, the sign agrees 4 of 4 while forg$_\text{D}$ varies nearly
as much as forg$_\text{B}$ (SD $19.2$ vs $22.0$): the mechanism does not transfer even where the rule
does. It is reported here, not in the body, because it is a fourth result competing with the three
the paper is actually about, and because a rule that holds on one backbone for a reason that does not
generalise is not a contribution we want weighed alongside them.

\section{Related Work}
\label{app:related}
%% ============================================================

Catastrophic-forgetting mitigations---EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc},
LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}, LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora}, L2-SP~\citep{li2018l2sp}---are designed to limit
how far a fine-tuned model moves from its pretrained state, but they are typically applied
without first asking whether the pretrained state is worth preserving on the target task. Our
pretrained-capability inclusion criterion is a downstream-only check for exactly that question, and the
preservation spectrum (Appendix~\ref{app:preservation}) shows why it matters: methods that raise
representational similarity do not reliably improve utility, and the ordering reverses with
horizon. The reflex itself is well founded where it originated: \citet{luo2023forgetting} document
substantial forgetting in LLMs under continual fine-tuning. Our claim is not that the phenomenon is
absent but that on TSFMs the drift measurement used to detect it does not order downstream harm.

Closest to this work, \citet{wilinski2025representations} study TSFM representations directly:
they measure layer self-similarity within and across model sizes, find block-like redundancy that
supports informed pruning, and steer the latent space to inject concepts such as periodicity and
trend. Their interventions act on a \emph{trained model's activations} to characterize and control
what the representation encodes; ours acts on the \emph{learning process}---freezing the encoder
and re-running fine-tuning---to ask whether representation change was necessary for the downstream
gain. The two are complementary, and their redundancy finding is a plausible mechanism for what we
observe: if TSFM representations carry substantial redundancy, a large measured drift can be
reorganization within that slack rather than loss of task-relevant structure, which is exactly the
gain-independent restructuring our intervention isolates. Neither their analysis nor ours predicts
a cell's B$-$D without running both conditions.

\citet{kumar2022finetuning} show that fine-tuning can distort pretrained features and that
linear probing followed by fine-tuning mitigates it; \citet{neyshabur2020transferred} study what
transfers between pretrained and fine-tuned networks. Neither provides a pre-fine-tuning screen
for when preservation is warranted, which is the gap this paper addresses. On the measurement
side we use linear CKA~\citep{kornblith2019cka}; our pooling analysis
(Appendix~\ref{app:pooling}) is a reminder that its magnitude is representation-dependent and
should be read as an operational drift detector rather than a measure of damage.

Pretraining overlap for the backbones we test is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:crossbackbone}.

drift anywhere in the study. The first three seeds we ran (42, 101, 123) averaged $-49.8$\% and
gave a $4.4$~pp gap, reported in an earlier version; extending to the full ten seeds moved D up to
$-45.3$\% and roughly doubled the gap, so that earlier estimate rested on a favourable subset. The
remaining caveat is hardware: the published B ran on CUDA and this D on MPS. At ten seeds each the
standard deviations still overlap, but the means no longer do (Welch $t{=}2.8$ on the $8.9$~pp
difference), so this cell is better read as a small-but-real adaptation contribution than as
B${\approx}$D.

%% ============================================================
\section{Threshold Sensitivity}
\label{app:thresholds}
%% ============================================================

Both thresholds are operational decision boundaries for this study, not universal constants, and neither was tuned to produce the reported signatures.

\textbf{Preservation-priority gate (20\%), and a margin claim we withdraw.}
Earlier versions reported the observed gate values as bimodal with a wide margin---``no cell falls in
$[0,28)\%$, so any threshold in $10$--$30\%$ produces identical assignments''. That was read on the
windows model selection uses. Recomputed on the test windows for all 23 cells from one code path
(\texttt{scripts/gate\_all\_cells.py}, \S\ref{sec:results}), the values are
$-3.3$, $12.9$, $13.8$, $28.7$, $33.1$, $38.9$, $40.1$, $45.6$, $47.2$, $49.4$, $50.5$, $50.6$,
$52.6$, $55.4$, $56.8$, $57.8$, $65.1$, $67.0$, $68.2$, $70.3$, $71.9$, $72.2$, $82.5\%$
(the four TimesFM cells contribute $40.1$, $47.2$, $50.6$ and $70.3$). Three cells fall below the threshold
($-3.3$, $12.9$, $13.8$) and one sits just above it at $28.7\%$, so the distribution is not bimodal
out of sample and the threshold is \emph{not}
margin-free: moving it to $30\%$ would additionally reclassify Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}96$, and moving
it to $10\%$ would restore Chronos/Electricity and Moirai-Large/ETTh2 to gate-pass. The six
degradation cells are unaffected across the whole $10$--$50\%$ range (they sit at $50.5$--$82.5\%$),
which is the assignment the paper's claims depend on; the reclassified cells all read
\emph{adapt}, where the gate is not what licenses the reading.

\textbf{CKA threshold reclassification.}
The gate analysis above shows wide margins; the CKA cutoff deserves the same treatment. We
reclassify every cell at four thresholds using the observed CKA values (ranges span seeds, not
datasets).

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccc@{}}
\toprule
Thresh. & Moirai-B & Moirai/ILI & Chronos/ETT & TimesFM ($h{\scriptstyle=}24$) & Chronos/M4 & TimesFM ($h{\scriptstyle=}96$) \\
& (0.25--0.33) & (0.456) & (0.087--0.179) & (0.19--0.64) & (0.98--0.999) & (0.995--0.999) \\
\midrule
$0.900$ & drift & drift & drift & drift & stable & stable \\
$0.925$ & drift & drift & drift & drift & stable & stable \\
$0.950$ & drift & drift & drift & drift & stable & stable \\
$0.975$ & drift & drift & drift & drift & stable & stable \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every assignment is identical at all four thresholds, and no cell splits. Note that TimesFM appears
twice and on opposite sides: its $h{=}96$ cells are the most stable in the study and its $h{=}24$
cells among the most drifted, so ``stable'' is not a property of the backbone. The margin is not
symmetric, and it is worth being explicit about which end is tight: the drifting cells sit
at least $0.26$ below the lowest cutoff considered, whereas the stable cells clear the highest one
by only $0.005$ (Chronos/M4, whose lowest seed is $0.98$) and $0.020$ (TimesFM at $h{=}96$). So it is
the stable end, not the drifting end, that would become threshold-sensitive if the cutoff were pushed
past $0.975$. We state the operating range explicitly rather than claiming
threshold-independence: the conclusions hold for cutoffs in $[0.90, 0.975]$.

Earlier versions of this table listed the Chronos/ETT cells at $0.886$--$0.949$ and therefore
reported them as \emph{split} below $0.95$. That range was misattributed---it belongs to the
Moirai-Small sample-size sweep, not to Chronos---and the measured Chronos values are an order of
magnitude lower, which removes the split.

\textbf{CKA (0.95).}
Observed CKA clusters away from the cutoff at the drifting end: drifting cells sit at
$0.087$--$0.179$, $0.19$--$0.64$, $0.25$--$0.33$, and $0.456$; stable cells at $0.98$--$0.999$.
A random-initialization baseline gives CKA $0.000{\pm}0.000$ (5 seeds), fixing the floor of the
scale, so the Chronos/ETT cells at $0.087$--$0.179$ are much nearer that floor than the threshold.
This is also why the Chronos/ETTh1-vs-ETTh2 comparison cannot be resolved by CKA at any cutoff:
both cells are unambiguously drifted on every reading, yet the less-drifted of the two is the one
that loses more to freezing.
