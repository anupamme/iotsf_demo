\section{Cross-Domain Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Per-seed cost--benefit invariant (protocol-robust).}
At $n{=}10$k, the CKA-probe gap (CKA$\downarrow$, Ridge and MLP
$\Delta R^2{>}0$) holds in all seeds individually on the 96-step
trained head under both early-stopped and final-epoch protocols.
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ is a diagnostic of encoder shift \emph{toward the
trained objective} (not absolute quality: $R^2(\text{FT})$ remains
negative), verified by two controls: (i)~frozen-encoder gives
$\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.048$ (14$\times$ smaller), ruling out
data-distribution effects; (ii)~random-init gives
$\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.199$ (4/5 negative) vs.\ pre-trained $+0.668$
(10/10 positive), ruling out ``any encoder fine-tuned on this objective''.
Task-specificity confirmed: $\Delta R^2{>}0$ on trained head,
${\leq}0$ on untrained heads (delta1 GBM: $-$0.056).
Population statistics: Ridge $\Delta R^2$ rises from $+$0.12 ($n{=}500$)
to $+$0.67$\pm$0.23 ($n{=}10$k, \textbf{10/10} positive);
MLP $k{=}5$ gives $+$0.70$\pm$0.32 (10/10 positive).

\textbf{Task forgetting is protocol-dependent.}
Forgetting is non-monotonic in $n$ ($+$14\% at 500, $-$2.9\% at 2k,
$-$5.3$\pm$6.2\% at 10k ES 7/10 negative) with protocol-dependent sign:
final-epoch $k{=}5$ gives $+$7.5$\pm$8.2\%, ES $-$5.3\% (difference
from late-epoch overshoot in 3 seeds, all near-zero).
We do not lead on forgetting sign; it is a task-outcome consequence,
not the primary signal.
Absolute $R^2(\text{FT})$ on the 96-step head remains negative
throughout ($R^2_{\text{ZS}}{=}-6.90$, $k{=}10$ $R^2(\text{FT})$
$-6.37{\pm}0.36$ at $n{=}10$k);
$\Delta R^2$ is a \emph{relative linear-decodability} signal.  A
next-step-delta head with a gradient-boosted probe gives
$R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059{>}0$ (positive-floor head) but
$\Delta R^2 {<} 0$ there, revealing the decodability shift is
objective-specific: it holds on the 96-step target fine-tuning
optimises, not on every linear readout
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:probehead}, Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
A CUDA replication at $k{\geq}10$ on matched seeds with
deterministic training has been completed (see Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).

\textbf{Second cell: ETTm2 $n{=}10$k shows the same deep-floor $\Delta R^2{>}0$ pattern.}
On ETTm2 ($h{=}96$, $n{=}10$k, CUDA $k{=}10$), the CKA-probe gap
holds with the \emph{opposite} task outcome from ETTh2: forgetting is
uniformly negative ($-28.2{\pm}2.2\%$, 10/10) while CKA falls to
$0.491{\pm}0.143$ and Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}5.43{\pm}2.57$ (10/10 positive).
The raw $\Delta R^2$ magnitude is larger than ETTh2 ($+$0.67) because
the ZS Ridge floor on ETTm2 is deeper ($R^2(\text{ZS}){=}-24.52$
vs.\ $-6.90$ on ETTh2, a 3.5$\times$ difference that proportionally
inflates raw $\Delta R^2$); the cross-dataset comparable statistic is
the sign test, which gives \textbf{10/10} positive in both cells
(Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
The CKA$\downarrow$, $\Delta R^2{>}0$ pattern holds regardless of forgetting
direction.
Both ETTh2 and ETTm2 operate in a deeply-negative-$R^2$ regime; the
delta1/GBM reprobe on ETTm2 finds no positive-floor reference
(Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
Probe noise-floor analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:noisefloor}) confirms the
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ direction is robust across $\alpha$, pooling, and probe seeds.

\textbf{ETTh1 $n{=}10$k: task resolves but per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ does not hold.}
On ETTh1 ($h{=}96$, $n{=}10$k, CUDA $k{=}10$), forgetting resolves
($-6.1{\pm}3.6$\%, 10/10 negative) and CKA falls to $0.727{\pm}0.059$.
Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{-}4.23{\pm}4.47$ (2/10 positive), substantively
different from ETTh2/ETTm2: the encoder drifts, the task improves, but
linear decodability \emph{decreases} in 8/10 seeds.
We interpret this as \emph{task-improvement-without-decodability-gain}:
encoder restructuring benefits forecasting loss but does not increase
linear separability of the 96-step target.
This narrows the per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ invariant to ETTh2/ETTm2.
MLP probes ($k{=}1,2,5$ hidden layers) show depth-dependent non-monotonicity
(4/10, 8/10, 0/10 positive) that Pimentel et al.~\cite{pimentel2020information}
warn does not constitute robust evidence; we report for completeness but
do not use as primary evidence.
A post-hoc spectral entropy pilot (Appendix~\ref{app:etth1_extended})
suggests ETTh1 is more trend-dominated (SE$=$3.61 bits) than cyclical
ETTh2/ETTm2 (SE$=$2.25--3.46 bits), consistent with non-linear temporal
features improving MSE without linear separability.
We treat the three-regime characterisation as a \emph{descriptive taxonomy},
not a predictive theory.

(CKA 0.81--0.94) causes forgetting (10--16\%) at $n{=}500$; ETTh2

(CKA 0.46--0.63); IoT is a negative control
(sub-random ZS, CKA 0.31--0.45).

\textbf{Model-size replication.}
Moirai-Base (91M) shows aggressive drift
(CKA$=$0.460$\pm$0.200) with strong task improvement
(unfrozen $-$44.1$\pm$5.7\%; frozen control $-$47.4\%).  The
3-point unfrozen-vs-frozen gap sits on a larger
capacity-driven gain and is within seed variance, so we decline to
treat Base as a second dissociation instance at $n{=}500$:
it is consistent with Base being \emph{value-gated differently
than Small} (the larger pre-trained capacity plausibly compensates
for drift, and $n{=}500$ fine-tuning contributes little over
freezing), not with the dissociation failing at Base.  A full
$n$-sweep on Moirai-Base is future work.
Moirai-Large (311M) at $n{=}500$ mirrors Small
($+$10.5$\pm$9.5\% forg., 3 seeds); LoRA-Large fails at default LR
and is rescued only by $10\times$ LR reduction
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:mitigation}, Appendix~\ref{app:lora_rank}).

\textbf{IoT negative control.}
On CICIoT2023~\cite{neto2023ciciot} anomaly detection, Moirai's
zero-shot AUC ($=0.481$) is sub-random; fine-tuning drives aggressive
drift (CKA 0.31--0.45) but a from-scratch CNN-NLL
(AUC $0.598{\pm}0.041$, 10 seeds) matches fine-tuned Moirai at IoT's
200-window scale.  IoT validates the value-gate framework as a
negative control, \emph{not} a second dissociation instance
(Appendix~\ref{app:cnn_detail}).
