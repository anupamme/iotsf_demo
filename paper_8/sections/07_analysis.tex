\section{Cross-Domain Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Per-seed cost--benefit regularity.}
At $n{=}10$k on ETTh2, 10/10 CUDA seeds show $\Delta R^2{>}0$ (Ridge
$+0.67{\pm}0.23$; MLP $k{=}5$: $+0.70{\pm}0.32$; 95\% Wilson CI [0.72,\,1.00]).
At $n{=}500$, the point estimate $+0.12$ ($k{=}2$) sits within the random-init
range $[-0.405,\,+0.149]$; no distinguishability claim is made at $n{=}500$.
The regularity is established at $n{=}10$k: condition~B range $[+0.21,\,+0.94]$
does not overlap the random-init range (Appendix~\ref{app:randominit}).
Task-specificity: $\Delta R^2{>}0$ on the trained 96-step head, ${\leq}0$ on
untrained heads (forecast48 ${\approx}0$, delta1 GBM $-0.056$;
Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).

\textbf{Task forgetting is protocol-dependent.}
Forgetting is non-monotonic in $n$ ($+$14\% at 500, $-$2.9\% at 2k,
$-$5.3$\pm$6.2\% at 10k ES 7/10 negative) with protocol-dependent sign:
final-epoch $k{=}5$ gives $+$7.5$\pm$8.2\%, ES $-$5.3\% (difference
from late-epoch overshoot in 3 seeds, all near-zero).
Per-epoch trajectories (10 CUDA seeds, $n{=}10$k): 7 converge monotonically
(best val-MSE epochs 3--5); 3 overshoot in epochs 12--18 --- source of the
final-epoch vs.\ early-stopped sign difference (Appendix~\ref{app:n5k_trajectories}).
Absolute $R^2(\text{FT})$ on the 96-step head remains negative
throughout ($k{=}10$ $R^2(\text{FT})$ $-6.37{\pm}0.36$ at $n{=}10$k);
$\Delta R^2$ is a \emph{relative linear-decodability} signal.  A
next-step-delta head with a gradient-boosted probe gives
$R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059{>}0$ (positive-floor head) but
$\Delta R^2 {<} 0$ there, revealing the decodability shift is
objective-specific: it holds on the 96-step target fine-tuning
optimises, not on every linear readout
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:probehead}, Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
\textbf{Second cell: ETTm2 $n{=}10$k replicates CKA$\downarrow$, $\Delta R^2{>}0$.}
ETTm2 ($h{=}96$, $n{=}10$k, CUDA $k{=}10$) replicates the frozen-probe pattern
with the \emph{opposite} task outcome: forgetting $=-29.2{\pm}2.1\%$ (10/10 negative,
improvement), Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}5.92{\pm}1.97$ (10/10 positive).
The larger raw $\Delta R^2$ reflects ETTm2's deeper ZS Ridge floor
($R^2(\text{ZS}){=}{-}24.52$ vs.\ $-6.90$ on ETTh2);
the cross-dataset comparable statistic is the sign test: \textbf{10/10}
positive in both cells (95\% Wilson CI [0.72,\,1.00]; see \S\ref{sec:forecasting:replication} and
Appendix~\ref{app:probing} for absolute per-seed values).
Probe noise-floor analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:noisefloor}) confirms the
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ direction is robust across $\alpha$, pooling, and probe seeds.
The probe asymmetry on ETTm2 (gate-fail) has a different diagnostic meaning than
on ETTh2 (gate-pass): it confirms the same encoder restructuring mechanism
operates regardless of pre-training value, but the \emph{consequence} differs---on
ETTm2, restructuring overwrites features that were not outperforming Linear, so
drift is benign.
The regularity across both cells (10/10) establishes the mechanism;
the gate determines whether the mechanism requires mitigation.

\textbf{ETTh1 $n{=}10$k: task resolves but per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ does not hold.}
On ETTh1 ($h{=}96$, $n{=}10$k, CUDA $k{=}10$), forgetting resolves
($-6.1{\pm}3.6$\%, 10/10 negative) and CKA falls to $0.727{\pm}0.059$.
Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{-}4.23{\pm}4.47$ (2/10 positive), substantively
different from ETTh2/ETTm2: the encoder drifts, the task improves, but
linear decodability \emph{decreases} in 8/10 seeds.
We interpret this as \emph{task-improvement-without-decodability-gain}:
encoder restructuring benefits forecasting loss but does not increase
linear separability of the 96-step target.
This narrows the per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ regularity to ETTh2/ETTm2.
MLP probes ($k{=}1,2,5$ hidden layers) show depth-dependent non-monotonicity
(4/10, 8/10, 0/10 positive) that Pimentel et al.~\cite{pimentel2020information}
warn does not constitute robust evidence; we report for completeness but
do not use as primary evidence.
We have no confirmed theory for the ETTh1 anomaly; a post-hoc spectral
entropy pilot (Appendix~\ref{app:etth1_extended}) notes ETTh1's higher
SE ($=$3.61 bits vs.\ 2.25--3.46 for ETTh2/ETTm2) but the hypothesis
is unverified and a pre-registered falsification test is specified there.
We treat the three-regime characterisation as a \emph{descriptive taxonomy},
not a predictive theory.
We treat the per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ regularity as \emph{ETTh2/ETTm2-specific}
pending replication on a gate-passing dataset with a different spectral profile.
\textbf{Pre-registered falsification criterion.}
We declare the spectral hypothesis \emph{confirmed} if: (i)~a dataset with
SE $>3.5$~bits (trend-dominated; ETTh1's SE$=$3.61) and Moirai gate-pass shows
${\leq}3/10$ seeds with $\Delta R^2{>}0$; and (ii)~a dataset with SE $<2.5$~bits
(cyclical; ETTm2's SE$=$2.25) and Moirai gate-pass shows ${\geq}7/10$ seeds with
$\Delta R^2{>}0$.
Traffic and Exchange were run as candidates; both gate-fail
($-$38\% and $-$21\% respectively; \S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}),
so the spectral hypothesis remains unfalsified pending a gate-passing
dataset with SE $>3.5$ bits.
If Moirai does not gate-pass a chosen dataset ($R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT})$
$<$20\% advantage over Linear), we report $\Delta R^2$ and CKA
descriptively without claiming a falsification result
(Appendix~\ref{app:etth1_extended}).

\textbf{Cross-domain envelope.}
Table~\ref{tab:cross_domain} spans six settings.
ETTh2 (gate-pass, $R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.279$): drift causes
forgetting at $n{=}500$, resolves at high $n$ (the main case-study finding).
ETTh1 and ETTm2 gate-fail at the standard evaluation protocol
($R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}){<}0$; Table~\ref{tab:r2task}): they are included
as cross-domain comparisons where the ZS baseline itself underperforms linear
regression, so fine-tuning \emph{can} improve on ZS even while CKA falls.
ETTh2-Base/Large at larger capacity improve despite drift (CKA 0.46--0.63);
IoT is a negative control (sub-random ZS, CKA 0.31--0.45).
Drift occurs in all six cells --- consequences depend on
pre-training value at the specific $(n, \text{horizon})$ point.

\textbf{Model-size replication.}
Moirai-Base (91M) shows aggressive drift (CKA$=$0.460$\pm$0.200) with
strong task improvement ($-$44.1$\pm$5.7\%); the 3-point unfrozen-vs-frozen
gap is within seed variance, so we treat Base as \emph{value-gated differently}
from Small, not as a second case-study instance at $n{=}500$.
Full $n$-sweep on Base is future work.
Moirai-Large (311M) mirrors Small at $n{=}500$ ($+$10.5$\pm$9.5\%, 3 seeds);
LoRA-Large is rescued by $10\times$ LR reduction (Appendix~\ref{app:lora_rank}).

\textbf{IoT negative control.}
Moirai ZS AUC$=$0.481 (sub-random); fine-tuning drives drift (CKA
0.31--0.45) but from-scratch CNN-NLL (AUC $0.598{\pm}0.041$) matches
fine-tuned Moirai.
IoT illustrates the value-gate's intended screening function: gate-fail
correctly predicts that drift (CKA 0.31--0.45) has no harmful consequence
(from-scratch CNN matches fine-tuned Moirai), confirming the gate's
utility as a practitioner screening tool (Appendix~\ref{app:cnn_detail}).

\subsection{Non-Moirai Backbones: All Nine Cells Gate-Fail}
\label{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}
% ============================================================

Matched-protocol ZS across nine non-Moirai backbone${\times}$ETT cells
(Chronos-T5-Small/Base/Large, TimesFM-2.5-200M, MOMENT-1-base $\times$ ETT):
all gate-fail ($-$14\% to $-$148\% vs.\ Linear); the CKA-probe gap is
established on Moirai only (full table Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
A plausible explanation is pre-training corpus coverage: Moirai's LOTSA
dataset~\cite{woo2024moirai} explicitly includes ETT benchmarks; Chronos
(\cite{ansari2024chronos}: KernelSynth + public data), TimesFM-2.5
(\cite{das2024timeseries}: Google Trends-based), and MOMENT
(\cite{goswami2024moment}: MIMIC, M4, and others) use corpora without
documented ETT inclusion---suggesting corpus composition, not model size,
drives gate-pass status.

We do not rule out architecture (T5 vs.\ patch-based), tokenization
(vocabulary vs.\ continuous), or pre-training objective (next-token vs.\
masked reconstruction) as alternative explanations.
Corpus composition is the most parsimonious account given the
size-invariance evidence (Chronos-Small/Base/Large all gate-fail on ETT)
and a direct empirical test: \textbf{Chronos-T5-Small gate-passes on
M4-Monthly} (ZS MSE 0.322 vs.\ Linear 2.077; 84.5\% improvement),
a dataset documented in Chronos's training corpus.
The same backbone gate-fails on ETT (not in its corpus) by $-$43\%.
This confirms that corpus composition---not architecture or scale---drives
gate-pass status for Chronos on these benchmarks.

\textbf{Moirai cross-dataset gate check.}
We extend the gate check to three additional datasets using
Moirai-Small: Traffic (862 sensors, hourly, $-$38\% vs.\ Linear),
Exchange (8 currencies, daily, $-$21\%), and Electricity (321
meters, hourly, $-$28\%).
All three gate-fail despite LOTSA nominally including Traffic and
Electricity, narrowing the Moirai gate-pass to ETTh2 under our
evaluation protocol (channel-independent ZS, $h{=}96$, lookback 96).
The gate-fail despite nominal corpus inclusion suggests that
\emph{pre-training emphasis} (dataset weight during training) rather than
mere inclusion determines the gate-pass signal---consistent with LOTSA's
documented emphasis on ETT benchmarks during
Moirai's development~\cite{woo2024moirai}.
