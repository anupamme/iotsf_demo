\section{Fine-Tuning Protocol}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================

This section describes the Moirai fine-tuning protocol shared by both
the forecasting (Section~\ref{sec:forecasting}) and IoT
(Section~\ref{sec:iot}) experiments.
The IoT-specific hard-negative generation pipeline is described
in Section~\ref{sec:experiments:setup}.

\textbf{Moirai for anomaly scoring.}
Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} predicts a distribution over
$\mathbf{X}_{97:128}$ given context $\mathbf{X}_{1:96}$.
The anomaly score is the fraction of timesteps falling outside the
$1{-}\alpha$ CI ($\alpha{=}0.05$):
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}(\mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{T'}\sum_{t=97}^{128}
    \mathbf{1}\!\left[x_t \notin \text{CI}_{0.95}(\hat{X}_t)\right],
\end{equation}
where $T' = 128 - 96 = 32$ is the scored-horizon length.
A sample is classified as an attack if $\hat{s}(\mathbf{X}) > \delta$,
where $\delta$ is calibrated on a benign validation set
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments:setup}).

\textbf{Combined training objective.}
We fine-tune with:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:combined_loss}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) =
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\theta) +
    \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}}(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{y};\, \tau),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = 0.5$ and $\tau = 0.07$ (sensitivity in
Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}; stable within $\pm$0.02 AUC).
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}}$~\cite{khosla2020supcon} is applied to
L2-normalised embeddings from a projection head
$h_\phi: \R^{384} \to \R^{256} \to \text{ReLU} \to \R^{128}$.

\textbf{Training details.}
AdamW ($\eta = 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$),
batch size 32, up to 10 epochs (5 in the main ablation,
10--20 in scaling experiments), early stopping (patience 3),
gradient clipping at 1.0.
All model sizes (Small 13.8M, Base 91M, Large 311M) use the same
protocol.

\textbf{uni2ts PackedStdScaler patch.}
We identified and patched an in-place tensor mutation in \texttt{uni2ts}'s
\texttt{PackedStdScaler} that silently detaches autograd on losses
depending on scaled statistics; a differentiable \texttt{torch.where}
replaces the in-place write.  All fine-tuning results here use the
patched scaler (released with our code); unpatched \texttt{uni2ts}
runs may converge differently.

\textbf{Representation diagnostics.}
We track representation drift with three complementary metrics:
(1)~CKA (Centered Kernel Alignment)~\cite{kornblith2019cka} between
pre-trained and fine-tuned encoder representations---a geometric
similarity measure;
(2)~linear probing R$^2$ (Ridge regression on frozen representations
predicting the forecasting target)---a \emph{functional} measure of
feature preservation; and
(3)~weight drift ($\ell_2$ distance from pre-trained parameters).
A random-initialisation CKA baseline ($<$0.001;
Appendix~\ref{app:cka_calibration}) confirms our measurements are
meaningful.
