\section{Experiment 1: Is There a Capability Worth Preserving?}
\label{sec:forecasting}
\label{sec:forecasting:gate}
% ============================================================

To ask whether fine-tuning damages valuable pre-trained features we first have
to find a cell where such features are demonstrably present.  This section
reports that search, and it comes out negative: across \textbf{32 screened
cells}---21 Moirai (three capacities, five datasets, two horizons), 5
Chronos-T5, 5 TimesFM-2.5 and 1 Moirai/ILI---\textbf{seven} clear the value gate,
and all seven are Moirai: five on ETTh2 and two on ETTm2.  The gate is scored on
selection windows, which exist for 31 of the 32---TimesFM/Electricity has no
paired frozen-encoder run, so it is screened but carries neither a gate nor an
intervention row---and every denominator below is 31.
Figure~\ref{fig:screen}a is this screen emptying; the figure itself is in
\S\ref{sec:intro}, because panel~(b) is the paper's claim rather than this
section's.

\textbf{Setup} follows \S\ref{sec:method}: $h{\in}\{96,192\}$ for Moirai and $h{=}24$
elsewhere, $n{=}500$ or $n{=}1{,}000$ samples, 3--10 seeds per cell, and every gate
value scored on \emph{selection} windows against a baseline fit on the cell's own
training split.

\textbf{Moirai: 7 of 21} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:r2task}).  ETTh2 clears at
every capacity---Small $h{=}96$ ($+0.874$) and $h{=}192$ ($+0.932$), Base
$h{=}96$ ($+0.777$) and $h{=}192$ ($+0.925$), Large $h{=}96$ ($+0.875$)---and
ETTm2 clears at two of four, Small $h{=}192$ ($+0.352$) and Base $h{=}192$
($+0.211$).  Every other dataset fails at every capacity, and every failing cell
is below the baseline rather than short of the threshold (Table~\ref{tab:r2task}).
Capacity does not help: Moirai-Base is 6.3$\times$ larger than
Moirai-Small and \emph{further} behind the baseline on both ETTh1 and Weather.

\textbf{Chronos-T5-Small: 0 of 5} and \textbf{TimesFM-2.5: 0 of 4}
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:crossbackbone}).
All nine cells fall in a narrow band about zero, $-0.275$ to $+0.082$, and none
reaches the threshold.  Within a band that narrow the dataset \emph{ordering} is
not stable across splits, and we withdraw a claim an earlier version built on it
(Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}).  What holds on both splits is the dataset
contrast: ETTh2, where Moirai clears the gate at every capacity, is where
Chronos is \emph{below} the fitted baseline ($-0.184$ on selection, $-0.325$ on
held-out), so the advantage is not a property of the dataset alone.

\textbf{One rung of eight.}  The screen's emptiness is not one estimator's
artefact.  Scoring the same numerator against a \textbf{ladder} of eight
baselines---on the same selection windows, with the training mean as an
admissibility floor---and measuring each cell against the \emph{strongest}
admissible rung it faces, \textbf{no cell of the 31 clears $0.20$}, and none does
on either of two further window sets.  \textbf{20 of the 31 clear it against at
least one admissible rung---14 Moirai, 3 Chronos, 3 TimesFM}: that union, not the
seven, is the scope every drift analysis below runs on, so those analyses are not
confined to one backbone.  Where this paper says seven cells clear the gate it
means seven clear \emph{this} rung of the eight; the per-rung counts, the other
two window sets, and the four cells a near-constant rung would admit are in
Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}.
