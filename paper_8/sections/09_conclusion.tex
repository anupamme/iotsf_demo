\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

% One paragraph rather than two: the body ends flush with the bottom of page 9, and a paragraph
% break here costs a printed line that the page limit does not have to give.
We set out to measure how much fine-tuning damages a time-series foundation
model's pre-trained capability, and found that on these benchmarks it is mostly not
\emph{measurably} there to damage: 5 of 32 cells beat a lookback-96 regression fit
on the target data, all five on one dataset, and none beats every admissible rung
of an eight-baseline ladder.  On three of those five, unconstrained fine-tuning
improves MSE by 31--42\%, and no cell meets a definition of degradation we fixed in
advance.  So check the features beat a fitted baseline before spending effort on
LoRA, EWC or L2-SP; if they do, settle the question against a frozen encoder scored
on windows you did not select on.  What survives as a positive
finding is the dissociation: the outcome spans $-51.8\%$ to $+46.1\%$ against
zero-shot across the 31 cells, and the magnitude of CKA-measured
change is not sufficient to predict $\denc$'s sign (within-backbone
$\rho{=}{+}0.168$, clustered CI $[-0.469,{+}0.717]$).  The cheap rule we had hoped
to report rests on a premise these benchmarks do not supply.
