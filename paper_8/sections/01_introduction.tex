\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} is a foundation time-series model trained
on billions of time points that achieves strong zero-shot performance
on forecasting benchmarks.  A natural next step is to fine-tune it on
domain-specific tasks: anomaly detection, classification, or
short-horizon forecasting with limited labelled data.  We present
a Moirai case study that characterises what happens to pre-trained
representations during this fine-tuning.

\textbf{Representation drift during fine-tuning.}
We provide systematic empirical characterization of \emph{representation
drift}---progressive overwriting of pre-trained features---during
fine-tuning of Moirai.  The drift--utility dissociation is established
on the Moirai family (Small 13.8M, Base 91M, Large 311M).
Matched-protocol attempts to replicate on three non-Moirai backbones
(Chronos-T5-Small~\cite{ansari2024chronos},
TimesFM-2.5-200M~\cite{das2024timeseries},
MOMENT-1-base~\cite{goswami2024moment}) gate-fail on all nine
ETT$\times$backbone cells tested (7 original + 2 new Chronos
capacity cells); we report this as a substantive finding about
backbone-specific ETT pre-training coverage
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting}, Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
Catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning is well-studied in continual
learning~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc, li2017lwf} and
NLP~\cite{luo2024forgetting}; our contribution is a systematic
case-study diagnosis for Moirai, with controlled experiments isolating
drift from overfitting and a spectrum of mitigations.
We use ``catastrophic forgetting'' to mean degraded performance on
distributions the model originally handled well---consistent with
continual learning usage where the ``previous task'' is the pre-training
objective.

\textbf{Distinguishing drift from overfitting.}
Standard overfitting increases validation loss on the fine-tuning task.
Representation drift instead degrades zero-shot performance on tasks
the model previously handled well, while training loss continues to
decrease monotonically.  Our frozen-encoder control (same data, same
epochs, CKA$=$1.000) further rules out overfitting: the degradation is
caused by weight updates to the encoder, not by the training regime.

On ETT forecasting benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm2), where zero-shot
Moirai outperforms Linear baselines by 28--45\%, fine-tuning with NLL
loss degrades MSE by 10--16\% after 20 epochs at $n{=}500$ and resolves
by $n{=}2{,}000$.  On IoT (sub-random ZS AUC), CKA drops to 0.31--0.45
but task performance improves from a near-null baseline: the drift is
irrelevant because there were no valuable features to forget.

\textbf{Why this matters.}
Unlike language models, where drift manifests as degraded generation
quality, time-series drift directly harms the distributional predictions
that underpin anomaly scoring and probabilistic forecasting.
Our study isolates drift's task-level consequences on Moirai and their
dependence on the value of pre-trained features: harmful on ETT in
the low-data regime, benign once $n$ is large enough for Moirai to
learn replacements, and irrelevant on IoT where ZS features have no
value to begin with.

\textbf{Experimental approach.}
We design a controlled diagnosis with fine-tuning conditions
(B:~NLL-only, C:~NLL$+$SupCon) and standard mitigations
(D:~frozen encoder, E:~LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora},
F:~L2-SP~\cite{li2018l2sp}, G:~EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc})
across three Moirai model sizes (Small 13.8M, Base 91M, Large 311M).
We deploy this on two settings:
(1)~ETT forecasting benchmarks~\cite{zhou2021informer} (ETTh1, ETTh2,
ETTm2), chosen because zero-shot Moirai outperforms Linear baselines
by 28--45\%, ensuring pre-trained features are demonstrably useful; and
(2)~IoT anomaly detection as a \emph{negative control} (Moirai ZS
AUC sub-random; we do not claim IoT as a contribution, only as
validation that the value-gate correctly suppresses the dissociation
test when ZS features have no value).
Both settings use CKA, linear probing, and weight drift as
representation-change diagnostics.
We report forgetting under two protocols (CUDA 10-seed deterministic
early-stopping and final-epoch $k{=}5$); the sign of the $n{=}10$k
headline differs between them ($+7.5\%$ vs.\ $-5.3\%$), so we lead on
the more robust $\Delta R^2{>}0$ majority and treat forgetting sign as
a secondary, protocol-dependent outcome.

We frame the contribution as a \emph{characterisation of three observed fine-tuning regimes}
on Moirai--ETT rather than a universal law: the three observed regimes are
empirical descriptions of what happens in specific (backbone, dataset,
$n$) cells, not a predictive theory.

\textbf{Contributions.}  We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A controlled case study of encoder representation dynamics
    during Moirai fine-tuning on ETT, using the \emph{CKA-probe gap}.}
    We document three sample-size-dependent outcomes on Moirai-Small$+$ETTh2
    and characterise boundary conditions across ETTh1 and ETTm2.
    Fine-tuning on the 96-step NLL objective imposes a \emph{geometric
    cost} (CKA$\downarrow$: from 0.949 at $n{=}500$ to $\approx$0.52
    at $n{=}10$k) whose task-level consequence takes three forms:
    \textbf{(i)}~on ETTh2 and ETTm2 at $n{=}10$k, CKA decay is
    accompanied by consistent $\Delta R^2{>}0$ movement on the trained
    head (\textbf{10/10 CUDA seeds per dataset}; $\Delta R^2{\leq}0$
    on untrained heads); both $R^2(\text{ZS})$ and $R^2(\text{FT})$
    remain negative throughout, so this is a relative-improvement
    signal whose direction is robust across probe-design perturbations
    (Appendix~\ref{app:noisefloor});
    \textbf{(ii)}~on ETTh1 at $n{=}10$k (CUDA $k{=}10$), forgetting
    resolves (10/10 negative, $-6.1{\pm}3.6$\%) but $\Delta R^2$ is
    \textbf{2/10 positive}---a \emph{task-improvement-without-decodability-gain}
    case that limits the scope of regime~(i); and
    \textbf{(iii)}~in the low-$n$ regime, drift causes forgetting
    ($+$14\% at $n{=}500$, resolving by $n{=}2$k).
    The $\Delta R^2{>}0$ invariant thus applies on ETTh2 and ETTm2
    but not universally: ETTh1 $n{=}10$k is an explicit counterexample.
    Ridge $\Delta R^2$ climbs from $+$0.12 at $n{=}500$ to
    $+$0.67$\pm$0.23 at $n{=}10$k; a 5-hidden-layer MLP probe
    gives $+$0.70$\pm$0.32 (10-seed CUDA).
    On an untrained next-step-delta head with
    $R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059{>}0$, GBM gives
    $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.056$, confirming the benefit is task-specific.
    The frozen-encoder control (5 seeds, CKA$=$0.9993,
    $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.048{\pm}0.008$, 14$\times$ smaller than
    condition~B) pins the effect to encoder weight updates.
    A \emph{trajectory signature} at the $n{=}5{,}000$ crossover
    (high-forgetting seeds overshoot in epoch~1) yields a concrete
    early-stopping recipe (\S\ref{sec:forecasting:trajectory}).

  \item \textbf{Mitigation spectrum and LoRA-Large LR rescue.}
    We evaluate standard mitigations---LoRA, EWC, L2-SP, and encoder
    freezing---alongside unconstrained fine-tuning.  LoRA on
    Moirai-Small achieves the best trade-off (CKA$\approx$0.98); the
    same recipe fails on Moirai-Large at default LR, and rank
    escalation does not rescue.  A $10{\times}$ LR reduction to
    $10^{-5}$ \emph{does} rescue LoRA-Large (forg.$=-8.5{\pm}0.7$\%,
    5~seeds) --- a concrete practitioner recipe not previously reported.

  \item \textbf{Nine-of-nine backbone$\times$dataset gate-failure
    pattern.}  Chronos-T5-Small/Base/Large, TimesFM-2.5-200M, and
    MOMENT-1-base gate-fail on every ETT$\times$backbone cell we ran
    (9 total, $-$14\% to $-$148\% vs.\ Linear); the Chronos
    capacity sweep (Small/Base/Large) confirms the gap is
    size-invariant.  A substantive finding about ETT-family
    distributions lying outside the pre-training coverage of these
    backbones, and a methodological caution for cross-backbone
    replication studies in this regime
    (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
\end{enumerate}
Code and datasets will be released upon acceptance.  We also
release a reproducibility-critical \texttt{uni2ts} patch
(\S\ref{sec:method}): without it, Moirai fine-tuning silently
detaches autograd through \texttt{PackedStdScaler}, so any
reproduction of this paper's numbers requires the patched scaler.
