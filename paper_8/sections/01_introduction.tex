% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Fine-tuning a time-series foundation model reshapes its internal
representations, and a large literature treats that reshaping as damage to be
prevented---by freezing the encoder, by regularising toward the pre-trained
weights, by confining updates to low-rank adapters.  Every one of those
prescriptions presupposes that the pre-trained representation was worth
preserving \emph{on the target task}.  \textbf{We test that presupposition on
the benchmarks the field actually uses, and it mostly fails.}

Our test is deliberately weak.  We ask only whether the pre-trained model,
zero-shot (ZS), beats a lookback-96 linear regression \emph{fit on the
practitioner's own target data}: not a tuned supervised forecaster, not an
ensemble---a single ridge-OLS map estimated once on the training split and
applied unchanged.  We write the margin as
$R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}) = 1 - \text{MSE}_{\text{ZS}}/\text{MSE}_{\text{Linear}}$
and screen \textbf{32 held-out cells} spanning three backbones---Moirai
(Small 13.8M, Base 91M, Large 311M), Chronos-T5~\cite{ansari2024chronos} and
TimesFM-2.5~\cite{das2024timeseries}, with
MOMENT~\cite{goswami2024moment} screened and excluded---six datasets and two
forecast horizons (\S\ref{sec:forecasting}).  \textbf{Five cells clear it},
and all five are the same dataset on the same backbone family: ETTh2 on
Moirai.  On the five that do clear it, full fine-tuning \emph{improves} MSE by 31--42\% in
three; the two whose mean shows harm carry only 4/10 and 5/10 seeds agreeing
on the sign.

\textbf{Terminology.}  \emph{Zero-shot} (ZS) is the frozen pre-trained model
evaluated without fine-tuning; condition~B is full fine-tuning and
condition~D a frozen encoder trained on the same data for the same epochs, so
$\denc \equiv \text{B}{-}\text{D}$ is the incremental value of allowing
encoder-side adaptation, and is the quantity this design manipulates. We measure
representational change with \emph{centred kernel alignment} (CKA, geometric
similarity between pre-trained and fine-tuned encoders; CKA${=}1$ means no
change) and \emph{weight drift} ($\ell_2$ parameter distance), both defined in
\S\ref{sec:method}; neither is manipulated, so both enter only as candidate
predictors of $\denc$. EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc},
L2-SP and LoRA appear as mitigation conditions; LR is learning rate, $n$
sample size, ILI the influenza-like-illness benchmark.

\textbf{The definition no cell meets.}
We fixed, in advance, what it would take for a cell to count as damage to a
demonstrated pre-trained capability: the cell must clear the gate, full
fine-tuning must end worse than not fine-tuning at all in \emph{every} seed,
and the frozen encoder must end better in \emph{every} seed.  \textbf{No cell
in the matrix satisfies it} (\S\ref{sec:analysis:degradation}).  The single
candidate, Moirai-Small on ETTh2 at $h{=}192$, splits 5/10 on the sign of the
harm.  The prospective test
we pre-registered therefore has no positive instance to predict: scored on
eight held-out cells the gate returns TP~0, FP~8, and precision $0.00$ at
every threshold from $0.10$ to $0.60$.  Within Moirai it is worse than
uninformative---higher pre-trained value predicts encoder adaptation helping
\emph{more} (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.518$ over 22 cells from six series).

\textbf{Contributions.}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{A negative result on the premise of forgetting mitigation.}
    Screened against a linear regression fit on the target data, only 5 of 32
    held-out cells show a pre-trained advantage to preserve, and all five are
    ETTh2 on Moirai; three of those five are \emph{improved} 31--42\% by full
    fine-tuning (\S\ref{sec:forecasting}).

  \item \textbf{Zero cells meet a pre-registered definition of degradation},
    so the value-gate we proposed has no positive instance: TP~0, FP~8,
    precision $0.00$ at every threshold in $[0.10,0.60]$, and
    $\rho{=}{-}0.518$ against the intervention outcome within Moirai---a
    direction, not an interval claim (\S\ref{sec:analysis:degradation},
    \S\ref{sec:analysis:prospective}).

  \item \textbf{Two measurement preconditions for getting this right.}
    Held-out scoring of the frozen-encoder comparison (4 of 31 cells reverse
    sign, all one-directional), and a fitted rather than per-window linear
    baseline (17 of 21 Moirai cells flip). Both corrections move results
    against the conclusion we originally drew
    (\S\ref{sec:analysis:heldout}, Appendix~\ref{app:gatecorrection}).

  \item \textbf{The drift--utility dissociation, on three backbones.}
    Outcomes from $-51.8\%$ to $+46.1\%$ with no reliable drift signature
    (within-backbone $\rho{=}{+}0.168$, clustered CI $[-0.469,{+}0.717]$); four of
    Chronos-T5's five cells sit at CKA${\in}[0.092,0.169]$---indistinguishable
    drift---and the outcome across all five spans 62 points, from $-41.4\%$ to
    $+20.6\%$ (\S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}). A reproducibility bundle (configs,
    seed lists, metric scripts, uni2ts patch, both linear baselines)
    accompanies this paper.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What is a claim here, and what is a control.}  Those four are the
paper's claims, argued in \S\S\ref{sec:forecasting}--\ref{sec:exp4}; we
do not claim that foundation models are useless for forecasting, nor that
fine-tuning is safe.  Everything else is a control or a boundary case, labelled
as such where it
appears: ILI and an IoT anomaly detector are external cells that gate-fail,
reported as case studies rather than evidence (\S\ref{sec:analysis:ili}); the
mitigation spectrum, a strict-freeze condition and the LoRA sweeps ask only
whether the frozen-encoder comparison depends on how it is implemented
(Appendices~\ref{app:full_tables},~\ref{app:strictfreeze},~\ref{app:lora_rank});
and a linear-probe $\Delta R^2$ axis from an earlier version is retracted
outright (Appendix~\ref{sec:retraction}).  No claim above rests on any of them.
