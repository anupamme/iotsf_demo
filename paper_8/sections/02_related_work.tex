\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\textbf{IoT IDS and DL anomaly detection.}
Traditional methods (Isolation Forest~\cite{liu2012isolation},
ensembles~\cite{garcia2014empirical}) fail on stealthy perturbations.
DL approaches---USAD~\cite{audibert2020usad},
TranAD~\cite{tuli2022tranad}, Anomaly
Transformer~\cite{xu2022anomaly}, PatchTST~\cite{nie2023patchtst}---serve
as our baselines on \dataset{}~\cite{neto2023ciciot}.

\textbf{Generative augmentation and adversarial training.}
GANs~\cite{lin2022idsgan} suffer mode collapse;
Diffusion-TS~\cite{zhang2024diffusionts} achieves better quality.
Unlike FGSM/PGD~\cite{goodfellow2015adversarial, madry2018pgd}
test-time perturbations, we generate adversarial \emph{training data}
via closed-form perturbations with protocol constraints.

\textbf{Time-series foundation models.}
Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} (27B+ pre-training time points) provides
probabilistic forecasting; its CI output maps to anomaly scoring.

\textbf{Catastrophic forgetting and mitigation.}
Catastrophic forgetting is extensively studied in continual
learning~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc, li2017lwf} and NLP fine-tuning~\cite{luo2024forgetting}.
The mitigation toolkit---EWC, L2-SP~\cite{li2018l2sp},
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}, encoder freezing---is well-established.

\textbf{Fine-tuning and representation dynamics.}
Kumar et al.~\cite{kumar2022finetuning} show that full fine-tuning can
distort pre-trained features relative to linear probing on the frozen
encoder, especially out-of-distribution; our positive forgetting\% at
$n{\leq}1$k on Moirai+ETT is a time-series analogue.
Andreassen et al.~\cite{andreassen2023evolution} characterize OOD
robustness decay throughout fine-tuning; our monotone CKA decay is a
geometric trajectory consistent with their findings, but we additionally
show that functional probe utility ($\Delta R^2$) moves in the
\emph{opposite} direction, so geometric loss does not imply functional
loss on the target.
Neyshabur et al.~\cite{neyshabur2020transferred} dissect what is
transferred during transfer learning; the CKA-vs-$\Delta R^2$
dissociation we report extends that question to within-fine-tuning
dynamics on a time-series foundation model.

\textbf{Probing and representational similarity.}
Probing classifiers~\cite{hewitt2019structural} diagnose what a
frozen encoder encodes; probe accuracy depends on probe capacity as
well as the encoder~\cite{pimentel2020information}, so relative
$\Delta$R$^2$ is the interpretable statistic, not absolute R$^2$.
CKA~\cite{kornblith2019cka} captures geometric rather than functional
similarity, motivating the CKA-vs.-probe dissociation in
\S\ref{sec:forecasting:replication}.
We do not claim novelty in the \emph{mechanisms} of drift or in
individual mitigations.
Our contribution is a systematic cross-domain \emph{characterization}
specific to time-series foundation models: CKA, linear probing,
weight drift, and frozen-encoder controls across two domains
with contrasting pre-training value, establishing when drift
helps vs.\ harms.

\textbf{Contrastive learning.}
SupCon~\cite{khosla2020supcon} has been applied to time-series
anomaly detection~\cite{eldele2021ts2vec}; we use it as the
condition-C objective in both the ETT forecasting fine-tuning
protocol (temporal-cluster labels) and the IoT negative control
(benign vs.\ attack labels).
