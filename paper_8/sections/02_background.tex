\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Catastrophic forgetting in fine-tuning.}
Catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning is extensively studied in continual
learning~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc, li2017lwf} and
NLP~\cite{luo2023forgetting}. Kumar et al.~\cite{kumar2022finetuning} show
full fine-tuning can distort pre-trained features relative to linear probing;
Andreassen et al.~\cite{andreassen2023evolution} characterize OOD robustness
decay throughout fine-tuning. Our contribution is a systematic
\emph{characterization} for time-series foundation models: CKA, linear
probing, weight drift, and frozen-encoder controls across domains with
contrasting pre-training value.

\textbf{Time-series foundation model adaptation.}
Recent work on adapting pre-trained time-series models includes
probabilistic fine-tuning (Lag-Llama~\cite{rasul2024lagllama}),
generative pre-training with task-specific heads
(Timer~\cite{liu2024timer}), LLM reprogramming
(Time-LLM~\cite{jin2024timellm}), and cross-task transfer
(One Fits All~\cite{zhou2023onefitsall}).
These works develop \emph{adaptation recipes}; our contribution is
orthogonal: a \emph{diagnostic methodology} for characterising what
happens to pre-trained representations during any such adaptation,
applicable when the practitioner needs to decide whether drift is
harmful or benign.

\textbf{Analysing time-series foundation model representations.}
A parallel line of work probes the internal structure of TSFMs directly.
Wili{\'n}ski et al.~\cite{wilinski2025representations} characterise and
\emph{steer} representations of \emph{frozen} TSFMs, uncovering
block-like redundancy and interpretable concepts (periodicity, trend)
that can be added by latent-space intervention.  Our study is
complementary along the time axis: rather than intervening on a fixed
model, we ask how those representations \emph{change under fine-tuning}
and whether the change is harmful.  Our diagnostics build on
representation-similarity (CKA~\cite{kornblith2019cka}) and probing
methodology~\cite{hewitt2019structural, pimentel2020information}, applied
here to the pre-trained-vs-fine-tuned encoder pair rather than to a
single frozen model.

\textbf{Setting.}
Given a pre-trained time-series foundation model with encoder
$f_\theta: \R^{T \times F} \to \R^d$ and downstream dataset
$\mathcal{D}$ of size $n$, fine-tuning produces updated parameters
$\theta'$ by minimising a task loss (NLL for forecasting).  We measure
encoder changes using two diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:method}):
(1)~CKA between $f_\theta$ and $f_{\theta'}$ (geometric similarity) and
(2)~weight drift $\|\theta' - \theta\|_2$.  A linear-probe
$\Delta R^2$ diagnostic used in an earlier version is retracted
(\S\ref{sec:retraction}).

\textbf{Pre-training value gate.}
Studying whether fine-tuning destroys valuable features requires a domain
where such features are demonstrably present.  We set the gate at zero-shot
MSE 20\% below a Linear baseline; ETTh2 at $h{=}96$ passes with
Moirai-Small ($R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.279$).
The gate is deliberately cheap and \emph{downstream-only}: the
practitioner---defined here as someone adapting a \emph{released}
foundation-model checkpoint to a small target dataset---runs the frozen
model zero-shot on the target set and compares to a lookback-96 linear
regression fit on that same set.  Neither pre-training data nor
pre-training compute is required, so the screen is available exactly in
the small-sample adaptation regime where fine-tuning is normally
performed.
ETTh1 and ETTm2 gate-fail at the standard evaluation protocol
($R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}){=}{-}0.41$ and ${-}0.56$ respectively;
Table~\ref{tab:r2task}); they are included as cross-domain comparisons
to characterise drift behaviour outside the gate-pass regime.
Domains that gate-fail serve as negative controls.
The 20\% threshold is calibrated to ETT short-horizon benchmarks where
linear baselines are surprisingly competitive~\cite{zeng2023transformers}:
a model must substantially outperform a lookback-96 linear regression before
drift can be \emph{harmful} (rather than irrelevant).
The threshold is a practical screen, not a universal constant;
we report $R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT})$ values for each cell
(Table~\ref{tab:r2task}) so readers can apply alternative thresholds.

\textbf{Task-level consequence: forgetting.}
We define forgetting as signed relative change in zero-shot-canonical
task metric on held-out test:
$\text{forg.\%} = 100 \cdot (M_{\text{FT}} - M_{\text{ZS}}) /
|M_{\text{ZS}}|$ (MSE for forecasting, so positive = worse).
Forg.$>0$ indicates catastrophic forgetting (degraded performance vs.\
pre-training baseline).

\textbf{Decoupling harmful forgetting from benign specialisation.}
A reviewer-critical distinction: representational change that redirects
features toward the target (\emph{benign specialisation}) must be
separated from change that destroys generally useful structure
(\emph{harmful forgetting}).  An earlier version of this work operationalised the distinction with a
\emph{probe-sign asymmetry} criterion---trained-objective decodability up,
task-orthogonal decodability flat or down.  That criterion is retracted
(\S\ref{sec:retraction}): the asymmetry appears only where the probes fail
to generalise and vanishes once they do, so it cannot separate benign
specialisation from harmful forgetting.
What remains is \emph{value-gate conditioning}.  Representational change is
harmful only when the structure it discards was valuable, i.e.\ in a
gate-pass cell ($R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}){>}0$, ZS beats Linear); in a
gate-fail cell the same change is benign because no valuable features
existed to forget.  Thus forgetting$>0$ \emph{in a gate-pass cell} is the
operational definition of harm, and the question of whether the encoder's
change \emph{carried} the gain is answered by intervention---comparing full
fine-tuning against a frozen encoder---not by observing the representation.  This is the mechanism the diagnostics in
\S\ref{sec:method} (notation in Table~\ref{tab:notation}) are designed to
resolve.
