All nine backbone$\times$dataset cells gate-fail (ZS under-performs
the Linear baseline by 14\% to 148\%), consistent with ETT-family
distributions being outside the pre-training coverage of these
backbones at these sizes.  The Chronos capacity sweep
(Chronos-T5-Small/Base/Large on ETTh2) confirms the gap is
size-invariant: scaling from Small (210M) to Large (710M)
yields no improvement ($-$42.6\% $\to$ $-$34.3\% $\to$ $-$35.2\%).
TimesFM-2.5-200M and MOMENT-1-base gate-fail across all tested datasets.
We interpret this pattern as a substantive finding about
\emph{backbone-specific ETT coverage} rather than evidence against
generality of the dissociation: matched-protocol ZS under-performance
on a value-gate design means fine-tuning these backbones on ETT
cannot meaningfully distinguish drift-as-forgetting from
drift-as-adaptation (there is no valuable feature to forget).  A
gate-passing second backbone on a different dataset (Traffic, ILI,
Exchange, or a model with explicit ETT pre-training coverage)
remains the most direct path to a multi-backbone replication and
is the highest-priority future-work direction.

\textbf{(2) Sample-sweep depth and seed count.}
The central sample-size sweep (Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep}) covers
ETTh2 at $n \in \{500, 1\text{k}, 2\text{k}, 5\text{k}, 10\text{k}\}$.
At $n{=}10$k we report the CUDA 10-seed deterministic early-stopped result:
$k{=}10$ seeds (42, 101, 123, 202, 303, 456, 777, 789, 888, 999),
all completed (no NaN); \texttt{--deterministic} flag
(\texttt{CUBLAS\_WORKSPACE\_CONFIG=:4096:8}, \texttt{cudnn.deterministic=True},
seeded DataLoader, PYTHONHASHSEED).
Forg.\ $-$5.3$\pm$6.6\%, 7/10 negative; mean best epoch $4.2{\pm}1.3$.
\emph{Dispersions in this item are standard deviations over seeds
($\text{ddof}{=}1$), matching Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} rather than the body's
SEM convention (\S\ref{sec:method}), because the four protocols compared below
differ in seed count and the comparison is about their spread.}
The Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}$0.67$\pm$0.24 (\textbf{10/10} positive) that an
earlier version reported alongside these is \textbf{retracted}
(\S\ref{sec:retraction}) and is quoted here only as a record of the withdrawn
computation; the forgetting and CKA columns are unaffected.
The three near-zero positive-forgetting seeds (123: $+$1.8\%, 456: $+$4.4\%, 789: $+$0.4\%)
follow the $n{=}5$k overshoot pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:dissociation}, right panel).
For comparison, MPS early-stopped $k{=}10$ gave $-$5.7$\pm$10.4\% (8/10 neg,
\texttt{v18\_mps\_deterministic\_n10k}); a second early-stopped run ($k{=}5$) gave
$-$11.7$\pm$5.3\% (all 5 negative, \texttt{v17\_etth2\_n10k\_es}); the
final-epoch runs gave $+$10.6$\pm$9.8\% on $k{=}3$
(\texttt{v16\_etth2\_n10k}), which is the sign fragility
\S\ref{sec:forecasting:replication} reports.  \emph{That last record does not
isolate early stopping as the cause, and we do not read it that way}: it differs
from the reported reading in four respects at once: its three seeds
(101, 456, 789) are a subset of the ten that happens to contain two of the three
most positive; it trains for 20 epochs rather than 10; it takes its percentage
against a different zero-shot reference ($0.1325$ against $0.1266$); and it reads
the final epoch rather than the best.  Choosing a different early-stopped baseline
reverses which factor looks dominant (seed-matched, the CUDA record reads
$-$2.0\% and the MPS one $+$4.1\%), so the pair bounds how far this cell's sign
moves across protocols and attributes it to nothing.
Moirai-Base is now evaluated at both $n{=}500$ and $n{=}10$k (10 seeds each);
full $n$-sweep on Moirai-Large is future work.

\textbf{(3) Probe-head capacity.}
Ridge and MLP absolute $R^2(\text{FT})$ remains negative on the
96-step-ahead head at all sampled $n$, which is exactly why the
$\Delta R^2$ axis is \textbf{retracted} (\S\ref{sec:retraction}): relative
movement between two fits that never decode the target is not evidence about
the encoder. The probe-design sweep of Appendix~\ref{app:noisefloor} shows
only that the direction is stable across $\alpha$, pooling and probe seed
\emph{within} that failed-fit regime; it does not rescue the statistic, and no
claim in this paper rests on it. Probe capacity is therefore no longer a
limitation of a live result but a diagnosis of a withdrawn one.

\textbf{(4) Sample-sweep on other sizes.}
Moirai-Large is evaluated at $n{=}500$ only; full $n$-sweep is future work.
\textbf{(5) IoT scope.}
IoT is a negative control (ZS AUC 0.481, sub-random); fine-tuning replaces
rather than preserves pre-trained features.
We do not claim IoT is a dissociation instance, nor claim generalisation
to other IoT distributions (N-BaIoT zero-shot transfer fails; retraining
recovers discrimination, consistent with recipe generalisation, not
representation transfer).

\textbf{(6) Three residual unsettled items.}  These closed the body's limitations
list until the page limit took the recap; each is stated where it arises and none
is withdrawn.  \emph{(a)}~The forgetting sign at $n{=}10$k is protocol-dependent:
item~(2) above gives the four protocols and their four signs on the same cell.
\emph{(b)}~The ILI cell is small, ${\approx}450$ windows, which is why
Appendix~\ref{app:ili_gate} calls it a case study and not a pillar.  \emph{(c)}~Two
pre-registered predictions were falsified rather than confirmed: ILI's CKA
prediction of $0.90$--$0.95$, against the measured value
Appendix~\ref{app:ili_gate} reports, and the prospective value-gate predictor, which
scored precision $0.00$ at every threshold in $[0.10, 0.60]$
(\S\ref{sec:analysis:prospective}).  We report both as failures of our own
diagnostic, which is what the pre-registration was for.  Values are not repeated
here: a recap that retypes them is how the two of them drift apart.

\textbf{(7) Why similarity is not mechanism, in two directions.}  A representation
can drift far and still carry the task information in a transformed basis, so a
low CKA need not mean a capability was lost; and a small drift can destroy one
critical direction, so a high CKA need not mean one was kept.  Both directions are
consistent with the positive control of Appendix~\ref{app:poscontrol}, where CKA
on task~A's inputs falls to $0.063$ while task~A itself \emph{improves}.  Our
negative claim is about \emph{identifying} damage from similarity alone
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}, item~6), not about CKA's validity as a measure of
similarity, which we do not dispute.

\section{uni2ts PackedStdScaler Patch Details}
\label{app:scaler_patch}
% ============================================================

We identified and patched an in-place tensor mutation in
\texttt{uni2ts}'s \texttt{PackedStdScaler} that silently detaches
autograd on losses depending on scaled statistics.  The issue arises
when \texttt{PackedStdScaler.transform} performs an in-place
\texttt{.copy\_()} that breaks the gradient tape.  Our patch replaces
the in-place write with a differentiable \texttt{torch.where}
operation.  \emph{All} Moirai fine-tuning results in this paper use
the patched scaler (released with our code); unpatched
\texttt{uni2ts} runs may converge differently and we have observed
silent non-convergence on \texttt{uni2ts@main}.  We recommend
upstreaming this patch to \texttt{uni2ts}.

\section{Moirai Median Forecast Selection}
\label{app:moirai_median}
% ============================================================

Moirai generates probabilistic forecasts as a mixture of
distributions (StudentT, NormalFixedScale, NegativeBinomial,
LogNormal).  The heavy-tailed components (NegBin, LogNormal) produce
extreme right-tail outliers when using the \emph{mean} of forecast
samples as the point prediction; for ETTh2 at $h{=}192$, sample
means reached 21{,}431 against a target maximum of 45.5, inflating
MSE from 0.30 to 8.58.

We use the \emph{median} of 20 forecast samples as the robust point
prediction, consistent with the Moirai paper's evaluation protocol.
This eliminates sensitivity to the heavy-tailed mixture components
while preserving the distributional forecast's central tendency.

\label{app:etth2_details}

ETTh2 (Electricity Transformer Temperature, Hourly, dataset 2)
contains 17{,}420 timesteps of 7 features (HUFL, HULL, MUFL, MULL,

\textbf{Window construction, and exactly which values Moirai is given.}
An earlier version of this appendix described the evaluation windows as
``extended lookback sequences (context\_length $+$ prediction\_length
timesteps)'', which reads as though the forecast target were part of the
model input.  It is not, and because the training and evaluation window
sets differ the arithmetic is worth writing out.  Let $x_1,\dots,x_T$ be
the series and $h$ the horizon.  Both window sets are built by two
functions in \texttt{scripts/finetune\_forecasting.py}, named here so that
this paragraph and the code can be checked against each other.

\emph{Training} (\texttt{make\_train\_sequences}): window $i$ has context
$x_i,\dots,x_{i+95}$ and target $x_{i+96},\dots,x_{i+95+h}$.  The two are
concatenated into one $96{+}h$ tensor and passed to Moirai's
\texttt{\_val\_loss} with fixed patch size 32, which is the interface's
required calling convention: \texttt{\_val\_loss} splits the tensor at
\texttt{context\_length} and marks the latter part with
\texttt{prediction\_mask}, and the module then substitutes its learned
\texttt{mask\_encoding} at every masked position and zeroes the
corresponding observed flags.  The target values are therefore scored by
the likelihood and never conditioned on.

\emph{Evaluation} (\texttt{make\_eval\_sequences}): window $i$ has input
$x_i,\dots,x_{i+95+h}$ (that is, $96{+}h$ steps of \emph{history}),
and target $x_{i+96+h},\dots,x_{i+95+2h}$.  The input's last index is
$i+95+h$ and the target's first is $i+96+h$, so the two windows are
disjoint and consecutive and no target value appears anywhere in the model
input.  Only the input is passed, as \texttt{past\_target} with
\texttt{past\_observed\_target} all ones; the point prediction is the
median of 20 forecast samples with \texttt{patch\_size='auto'}.
``Extended lookback'' thus means a longer \emph{history}, not
history-plus-target.  Figure~\ref{fig:windowlayout} draws both layouts
from the index arrays those two functions actually return rather than from
this description, and asserts the disjointness at draw time, so the figure
cannot drift from the implementation.

The one asymmetry this does introduce is between models: Moirai is given
$96{+}h$ steps of history where the fitted baseline of
Appendix~\ref{app:gatecorrection} is given 96.  That direction favours the
pre-trained model (more context lowers $\text{MSE}_{\text{ZS}}$, raises
$\Vb{b}$ and so admits \emph{more} cells), and
Appendix~\ref{app:baselines:matchedlb} measures it rather than arguing it, by
giving the baseline the same $96{+}h$ history.

All data is fed to Moirai in raw scale (Moirai's internal
PackedStdScaler handles instance normalization); predictions and targets
are then normalized using training-set mean and standard deviation
for MSE/MAE computation, matching the standard time-series forecasting
evaluation protocol.

\textbf{Condition C's objective and its contrastive labels.}
Condition~B fine-tunes on NLL only; condition~C adds a supervised contrastive
term~\cite{khosla2020supcon},
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\theta) +
\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}}(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{y};\, \tau)$, with
$\lambda = 0.5$ and $\tau = 0.07$.  Condition~C appears in this paper only as a
protocol variant of~B and carries no claim; the comparison every claim turns on
is B against~D.
For the contrastive labels we divide training sequences into
8 temporal clusters based on their position in the time series.
Sequences within the same cluster are positive pairs; sequences from
different clusters are negatives.
This encourages the encoder to maintain temporal-regime awareness.

\textbf{Horizon selection.}
We evaluate $h{=}96$ and $h{=}192$, the two horizons at which Moirai-Small on
ETTh2 clears the value gate: measured against the fitted lookback-96 baseline
the gate is defined with (Appendix~\ref{app:gatecorrection}),
$\Vb{\text{ridge}}{=}{+}0.400$ at $h{=}96$ and ${+}0.464$ at
$h{=}192$ on held-out windows.
Horizons $h{=}336$ and $h{=}720$ exceed Moirai-Small's
\texttt{max\_seq\_len}$=$512 constraint when combined with the
required extended lookback and are omitted.

\paragraph{Why the four reverse, and all in one direction.}
The reversals of \S\ref{sec:analysis:heldout} are driven by the frozen arm, whose
held-out penalty is much larger than its selection-window penalty ($\Delta$ frozen
$+53.9$ against $\Delta$ adapted $+8.0$ on Chronos/ETTh1, the largest of the four),
and they concentrate where the two splits are least exchangeable: the
\emph{untrained} checkpoint's held-out-to-selection error ratio spans $0.98$ to
$5.07$ across these four cells, a quantity that involves no fine-tuning at all.  A
frozen encoder cannot adapt to that shift and a fine-tuned one partly can, which is
exactly the asymmetry that makes same-split scoring flatter freezing, so the
direction of the four reversals is not a coincidence to be noted but a consequence
to be expected, and it is why scoring $\denc$ on selection windows is not a
defensible option rather than merely a less careful one.

\paragraph{The mitigation spectrum, cell by cell.}

\section{The Pre-Registered Prospective Test, in Full}
\label{app:prospective2}
% ============================================================
% The complete record of the arm S7 now states in one paragraph. Moved here 21 Sep 2026 because four
% body paragraphs on an arm with ZERO positive instances invited exactly the reading a reviewer took
% from it -- that the arm refutes the gate. It does not. It measures the gate's false-positive
% behaviour, and about the gate's sensitivity it is silent, because sensitivity has an empty positive
% class in its denominator. The prose below is the body's own, moved rather than rewritten: this file

\textbf{What was registered, and when.} Predictions were written to
\texttt{results/v47\_prospective/preregistration.json} and committed to git at
a named revision before any outcome run for those cells existed; the scoring
script reads that file and the finished runs and decides nothing.
Two rules were registered against each other: the \textbf{gate rule},
gate$_\text{pre}\!\geq\!0.20 \Rightarrow$ at risk, which is the paper's own
criterion frozen \emph{before} the baseline correction and therefore graded on
trend-denominator scores rather than re-derived after seeing the outcomes; and a
\textbf{dataset rule}, dataset ${\in}$ \{ETTh1, Weather\} $\Rightarrow$
degradation, a \emph{post hoc} pattern read off the original Moirai cells and
registered as a competitor so the gate is graded against something.
Eight cells were then run (Weather, ETTm2, Electricity7, ETTh1
$\times$ Small/Base/Large, 3 seeds each) and scored by the pre-registered
outcome definition, unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:prospective}).

\textbf{Result: the outcome the gate was registered to predict never occurs.}
None of the eight cells degraded.  The gate flagged all eight, giving
\textbf{TP~0, FP~8, FN~0}: precision $0.00$, while \textbf{sensitivity and
specificity are both unestimable}: sensitivity has an empty positive class in its
denominator, and specificity is $0/8$ only because every prediction was positive.
The competing dataset rule scores no better, for the same reason.  \textbf{What
``prospective'' does and does not mean here:} the predictions were frozen before
these cells were \emph{trained}, so they are prospective with respect to the
intervention, but the datasets and their splits already existed, so this is not a
test on newly collected data.  Nor is the empty positive class an artefact of the
corrected gate: only one of the eight clears $0.20$ on either corrected split
(base/ETTm2 $h{=}192$, $+0.21$ on selection windows) and it fails clause~(ii) in 0
of 3 seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}).  \textbf{Nor is the $0.20$ threshold
what fails}: re-scoring the same predictor against the same outcome at thresholds
from $0.10$ to $0.60$, moving nothing else, gives precision $0.00$ at every one,
with 8, 8, 6, 5, 3 and 2 cells flagged (Table~\ref{tab:gate_sensitivity}); the
problem is not the cutoff but that the predicted class is empty.

\textbf{What a zero-prevalence test licenses.} With no positive instance the arm
is informative in one direction only. It establishes that the gate's positive
predictions are wrong here, at every threshold swept, and that the error is not an
artefact of the operating point. It establishes \emph{nothing} about whether the
gate would detect degradation if degradation existed: that is a claim about
sensitivity, and $0/0$ is not a rate. A reader who takes this arm as a refutation
of the gate is reading past the prevalence. The regime the arm cannot reach is
one a positive control has to construct deliberately
(\S\ref{sec:exp4:poscontrol}, Appendix~\ref{app:poscontrol}), and which, on
these benchmarks, we could not construct.

\textbf{The gate does not provide a reliable ordering, and there is no actionable
threshold.}
Within Moirai ($n{=}22$ cells from six series, the only arm with enough cells to
ask), the gate's continuous score correlates \emph{negatively} with held-out
$\denc$: Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.293$, and neither cells nor series exclude zero, so
we report the direction and not the interval (Appendix~\ref{app:clustered}).
The sign is the wrong one (higher measured pre-trained value goes with encoder
adaptation helping \emph{more}, not less, the opposite of what a preservation
heuristic needs), but the sharper point is that the magnitude is not stable:
scoring the same gate on the held-out windows instead gives $\rho{=}{-}0.518$
on the same cells.
An ordering that nearly doubles when the gate changes split is not one a
practitioner can set a threshold on, and no other quantity we measured orders
$\denc$ either (Appendix~\ref{app:predictor}).

\label{app:strictfreeze}
% ============================================================

Condition~D freezes the encoder's own weights but leaves the input projection
(\texttt{in\_proj}, $247{,}680$ parameters) and \texttt{mask\_encoding} ($384$)
trainable alongside the distribution head (\texttt{param\_proj},
$2{,}956{,}800$). What the encoder \emph{receives} therefore keeps changing, so
its output is not a fixed function of the input, which is why D's measured CKA
is $0.76$--$0.90$ on ILI and $0.97$--$0.99$ on Moirai-Base rather than
$1.000$. A reader can reasonably object that wherever ``freezing wins'', it
wins partly by re-fitting the input projection rather than by holding the
encoder still.

Condition~\textbf{H} removes the objection: \texttt{in\_proj} and
\texttt{mask\_encoding} are frozen too, so only \texttt{param\_proj} trains and
the encoder's output is pinned by construction (measured CKA $1.0000$ exactly,
as it must be). B$-$H is then a clean read of what allowing \emph{any}
encoder-side change was worth, and B$-$D versus B$-$H separates encoder
adaptation from input re-fitting. We fixed the reading rule before running: the
claim is that the sign and reading of B$-$D survive strict freezing, so B$-$H
is reported beside B$-$D on every cell, and divergence on any cell is reported
as that cell's gap being partly input re-fitting rather than being buried.

\textbf{Result} (Table~\ref{tab:strictfreeze}). The sixteen cells fall into
three groups. Seven were chosen before the gate was corrected: they are the six
that met the degradation
definition under the superseded trend baseline
(Appendix~\ref{app:gatecorrection}), plus ILI. Nothing was dropped; two cells
the prospective arm later added (Moirai-Large on ETTh1 and on Weather) postdate
this control and carry no H run. That selection no longer picks out a privileged
class (against the fitted baseline no cell meets the definition), but it
remains the set chosen to be \emph{most} favourable to the degradation story,
which is the useful property here. Five are the gate-passing
survivors of \S\ref{sec:forecasting:results}, run afterwards precisely because
the first seven did not reach them: until they existed, the survivors' readings
rested on condition~D alone. The last four are the Moirai/ETTm2 cells that clear
the screen against a rung other than the fitted line
(Appendix~\ref{app:valueaxis}), added so that the control covers value-cells
outside ETTh2: all four of them, at three seeds each, so ETTm2 is covered
completely rather than partially. Measured CKA is
$1.0000$ everywhere, confirming the construction.

\textbf{On twelve of the sixteen the reading survives; on four it reverses, and
the four are the ones we added.} B$-$H keeps B$-$D's sign on the original seven
and on the five survivors. There the magnitude
changes by $-13.1$ to $+28.9$~pp, and on the original seven by $-5.8$ to
$+0.5$~pp, so at most $5.8$~pp of a gap as large as
$62$~pp is input-projection re-fitting. On ILI, where adaptation helps, strict
freezing makes adaptation look \emph{more} valuable ($-38.6 \to -43.6$), which
is the same direction: holding more of the model still costs more where the
encoder was earning its keep. B$-$D is averaged over the seeds condition H
ran, so Moirai-Small/ETTh1's entries here are 3-seed values against the 5-seed
values quoted in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all}.

\textbf{All four ETTm2 cells diverge, and we said in advance how that would be
read.} On every one of them $\denc$ changes sign between the two controls:
Moirai-Small $h{=}96$ goes from B$-$D $+7.3{\pm}2.9$ to B$-$H $-6.8{\pm}2.0$,
Moirai-Base $h{=}96$ from $+7.0{\pm}2.4$ to $-9.2{\pm}3.1$, Moirai-Small
$h{=}192$ from $+4.2{\pm}5.8$ to $-2.7{\pm}6.1$ and Moirai-Base $h{=}192$ from
$+1.3{\pm}3.4$ to $-2.4{\pm}2.9$: shifts of $-14.2$, $-16.2$, $-6.9$ and
$-3.7$~pp. The last of those is the weakest of the four and we do not lean on it:
both of its intervals straddle zero, so what that cell shows is a shift in the
mean and not a resolved reversal. The pattern is carried by the two $h{=}96$
cells, where the shift is four to five times the SEM. The consistency of the
\emph{direction} across all four is what the reading rests on: four cells, one
dataset, four shifts, all negative. The rule fixed before the runs was that divergence means part of
that cell's gap is input re-fitting, and that is what these say: on ETTm2,
condition~D's apparent ``freezing wins'' was carried \emph{entirely} by the
input projection adapting to the target, and once it is held fixed too,
allowing the encoder to adapt is the better choice. Two readings follow, and
they point the same way as the rest of the paper rather than against it. First,
the direction of the correction is towards adaptation on every diverging cell,
so nothing here recovers a degradation case; ETTm2 joins ETTh2 in the column
where full fine-tuning wins under the strict control. Second, and more
important for \S\ref{sec:exp2}, it is direct evidence that
\textbf{condition~D does not measure encoder adaptation alone}: on four of the
sixteen cells where we can separate the two, the loose control's sign is set by
the projections rather than by the encoder. That is a limit on B$-$D as an
estimand, and it is why \S\ref{sec:method} defines $\denc$ against the control
actually used and reports H beside it rather than substituting one for the
other.

\textbf{On the five survivors the control cuts against freezing, not for it.}
These are the cells the objection mattered most for, and on the three where full
fine-tuning already won, strict freezing \emph{widens} its margin rather than
narrowing it: B$-$D of $-22.3$, $-20.2$ and $-20.8$ becomes B$-$H of $-28.0$,
$-31.8$ and $-33.9$ on Moirai-Base $h{=}96$, Base $h{=}192$ and Large $h{=}96$,
a move of $-5.6$ to $-13.1$~pp. Part of what condition~D was scoring on these
cells was therefore the input projection re-fitting to the target data, and
removing it makes encoder adaptation look \emph{more} valuable, not less. On
Moirai-Small $h{=}192$ (the cell that comes closest to degradation), B$-$H is
$+1.2{\pm}8.5$ against B$-$D's $+4.1{\pm}8.6$: freezing still reads as better,
by less, with a standard error several times the mean either way. Condition~H
also improves on the un-tuned model in every seed of all five
(forg$_\text{H}$ from $-0.2\%$ to $-8.5\%$), so on the survivors both parts of
the degradation definition behave the same way under the strict control as
under the loose one.

\textbf{The one large change is the diverged seed, and it corroborates the
earlier reading.} Moirai-Small/ETTh2 $h{=}96$ moves from B$-$D $+13.7$ to
B$-$H $+42.6{\pm}29.5$, the only cell whose magnitude shifts by more than
$13$~pp. It is seed 42, which \S\ref{sec:forecasting:results} reports as a
training failure rather than an encoder-drift effect: that seed diverges under
full fine-tuning ($+98.9\%$) \emph{and} under the frozen encoder ($+91.1\%$),
but it cannot diverge under condition~H, where only \texttt{param\_proj} trains
and the run therefore sits at zero-shot ($-0.1\%$). Over the other two seeds
B$-$H is $+14.4$ against B$-$D's $+16.7$. The strict control thus supplies
independent support for the seed-42 diagnosis: what fails on that seed is
optimisation, and holding the encoder's \emph{inputs} fixed as well as its
weights is what prevents it.

\section{LoRA on the Value-Cells: the Constrained Middle of the Adaptation Axis}
\label{app:loravaluecells}
% ============================================================

Conditions~B and~D bracket the adaptation axis: everything trains, or the
encoder body does not. Neither tells a practitioner whether a \emph{cheap
constrained} adaptation would have beaten the extreme their cell happened to
run. Condition~\textbf{E} (LoRA, $r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$) is that middle point,
and until this revision it existed on one cell, which is why every ``LoRA is
the cheap win'' sentence in earlier versions generalised from
Moirai-Small/ETTh2. We ran E on the eight Moirai value-cells that lacked it,
seeds $42/123/456$, each at its own arm's $n$ and epoch budget
(Table~\ref{tab:lora_valuecells}).

\textbf{What E can and cannot measure, fixed before the runs.} LoRA adds
trainable low-rank updates to the encoder's attention projections, so the
encoder's \emph{function} changes while its base weights do not: $\ell_2$ weight
drift is exactly $0.0$ on all 24 records by construction, and it is omitted from
the table rather than printed as a column of zeros that would invite the reading
that the encoder was held still. CKA is the measure that still reads on E. $\denc$
remains B$-$D; E is not a second estimate of it.

\textbf{The one-cell reading does not survive.} CKA$_\text{E}$ spans
$0.5482$ to $0.9674$ across these eight cells, against the ${\approx}0.98$ the
single-cell spectrum reports, so LoRA is not a representation-preserving method
in general; it is one on some cells. Full fine-tuning finishes ahead of LoRA
(B$-$E${}<0$) on \textbf{4 of the 8}. LoRA is itself worse than not fine-tuning
at all on two cells (forg$_\text{E}={+}11.9\%$, 2 of 3 seeds, on
Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}96$; ${+}11.2\%$, 3 of 3, on Moirai-Base/ETTm2
$h{=}192$).

\textbf{Against the frozen encoder, read as a paired contrast.} D$-$E is paired
per seed rather than taken as the difference of two means, which matters: on
Moirai-Small/ETTh1 the B$-$E and B$-$D means sit $0.2$~pp apart with a SEM of
$4.9$, and a difference of means there would read as ``LoRA beat freezing'' on
a cell that cannot separate them. Paired and requiring a unanimous sign,
\textbf{LoRA beats freezing on 3 of 8 cells, freezing beats LoRA on 2, and 3 are
inseparable}. Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}192$ is the one cell that orders all three
points cleanly (full fine-tuning, then LoRA, then frozen), and it is a
gate-passing cell, so the ordering is on a cell with something to preserve.

\textbf{Why this belongs in a paper about drift as a diagnostic.} It is a third
condition on which the paper's central claim holds. Across these eight cells
CKA$_\text{E}$ does not order the outcome of constraining adaptation
($\rho{=}{-}0.167$ against B$-$E; ${-}0.333$ against B$-$D on the same cells):
the highest-CKA cell here, Moirai-Large/ETTh2 at $0.9674$, is one where the
constrained option \emph{loses} to full fine-tuning by $27.8$~pp, and the
lowest, Moirai-Base/ETTm2 $h{=}96$ at $0.5482$, is one where it wins. As with
B$-$D, how much the representation moved does not tell you whether constraining
the move helped.

\textbf{Two limits of this arm.} The two Moirai-Small/ETTh2 cells already
carrying E are excluded here: their E arm ran at $n{=}1{,}000$ against B/D at
$n{=}500$, a mismatch disclosed in Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum}'s caption
and not improved by adding a third $n$. And $\alpha$ is not written into the run
records (only $r$ is), so $\alpha{=}16$ is traceable to
\texttt{scripts/run\_lora\_valuecells.sh} and its committed command line rather
than to the per-run JSONs, the same limitation the learning rate carries.

Why a naive re-initialisation reads zero is worth recording, because it is the
likely origin of the earlier number: ``Xavier for weight tensors, zeros for
biases'' also zeroes LayerNorm's scale parameter, which annihilates the signal at
the first normalisation and drives the encoder to a \emph{constant} output.  CKA
is then $0$ because one side has no variance, not because the two share no
structure.  Our script asserts that the randomised encoder's output variance is
non-trivial, and reports $0.215$ once normalisation parameters are left at
$\gamma{=}1$; initialising every layer with its library default instead gives the
same answer to within one standard deviation.

\textbf{What this changes.}  Two of the earlier reading's conclusions do not
survive.  The IoT arm at CKA $0.423{\pm}0.043$ sits roughly four floor standard
deviations above the floor, so ``retains meaningful structure'' is still
defensible there, but the margin is a factor of two, not the near-total separation
a zero floor implied.  And ``ETT fine-tuning preserves most pre-trained
structure'' was read off small-$n$ runs: the same cell at $n{=}10$k reads $0.465$,
and the range across all 31 cells is $0.092$ to $0.970$.  The condition-D row also
needs a scope: D is uniform only within Moirai, and its 131 runs exclude the
partial-unfreeze arm of Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}, whose runs are stored
under the same \texttt{condition\_D} filenames and reach down to CKA $0.317$
while three of six layers train.  Because D leaves \texttt{in\_proj}
and \texttt{mask\_encoding} trainable (\S\ref{sec:method}), TimesFM's D falls as
low as $0.376$, while Chronos's D and every condition-H run are exactly $1.0000$;
only H is $1.0000$ by construction.

\textbf{What it adds.}  Comparing the floor against all 31 cells,
\textbf{four fall below it} outright (the Chronos cells at
CKA $0.092$, $0.094$, $0.112$ and $0.169$), and two more (Chronos/Electricity
$0.232$, TimesFM/ETTh2 $0.246$) sit inside the floor's own seed-to-seed range
($0.132$ to $0.288$).  This is a measured basis for a claim
\S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai} otherwise has to assert: on those cells CKA is
at or beneath what an \emph{untrained} encoder scores, so it cannot order them,
and their outcomes span $-39.2$ to $-2.7$ in held-out $\text{B}{-}\text{D}$
regardless.  The remaining 25 cells, including all five gate-passing survivors and
both arms of the layer-unfreeze comparison ($0.716$ and $0.465$,
Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}), sit clear of the floor, so CKA differences
among them are readable.

\section{Linear Probing: Functional Representation Diagnostic}
\label{app:probing}

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\textbf{Retracted.} The analyses in this
appendix rest on the linear-probe $\Delta R^2$ statistic, which is retracted
(\S\ref{sec:retraction}): it is non-zero only where the probe fails to
generalise. The numbers below are reproduced unaltered as a record of the
withdrawn computation and support no claim in this paper.}}
\vspace{1em}
% ============================================================

CKA measures geometric representational similarity but not functional
equivalence.  We therefore supplement CKA with a \emph{linear probe}:
a Ridge regression head ($\alpha{=}1.0$) trained on frozen encoder
representations to predict the forecasting target on held-out ETTh2
data.

\textbf{Protocol.}
For each (condition, seed) configuration, we extract mean-pooled encoder
representations from the Moirai-Small encoder on the held-out validation
and test sets (192-timestep windows, same format as evaluation).
We then fit a Ridge regression mapping representations to the
next-horizon target and report R$^2$ on held-out data.
The probe training set uses $n_{\text{probe}}{=}300$ held-out examples;
the remainder forms the test split used for MSE evaluation.
$\Delta R^2$ denotes fine-tuned minus pre-trained probe R$^2$:
positive values indicate that fine-tuned representations encode
more forecasting-relevant information than pre-trained ones.

\textbf{Interpretation.}
Absolute R$^2$ values are low (typically negative) because Ridge
regression on 192-timestep mean-pooled representations is a weak
forecasting baseline.  The \emph{relative} change ($\Delta R^2$)
is the meaningful signal: it isolates whether fine-tuning added
or removed task-relevant information from the encoder's
representational basis.

\textbf{Key result.}
Across the sample-size sweep (the transcript in \S\ref{sec:retraction};
Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} no longer prints these columns)
$\Delta R^2$ increased monotonically with training data
(Ridge: $+$0.12 at 500 samples $\rightarrow$ $+$0.67$\pm$0.24
at 10{,}000 on CUDA 10-seed deterministic early-stopped encoders,
\texttt{results/v19\_cuda\_etth2\_n10k/}; for comparison, MPS early-stopped
$k{=}5$ gave $+$0.56$\pm$0.14 (\texttt{v17\_etth2\_n10k\_es}) and the
final-epoch runs, $k{=}3$, gave $+$0.84$\pm$0.15
(\texttt{v16\_etth2\_n10k})), while CKA decreases monotonically.
This directly dissociates \emph{geometric} drift from
\emph{relative linear-decodability}: at low $n$, drift destroys
task-relevant features faster than it builds replacements;
at higher $n$, the fine-tuned representation becomes less
linearly misaligned with the forecasting target than the
pre-trained one.
We emphasise that $R^2(\text{FT})$ remains
\emph{absolutely negative} at every sampled
$n \in \{500, 1\text{k}, 2\text{k}, 5\text{k}, 10\text{k}\}$
(ZS: $R^2{=}-6.90$; CUDA $k{=}10$ early-stopped: $-6.79$ at $n{=}500 \to -6.24{\pm}0.24$
at $n{=}10$k).  $\Delta R^2 {>} 0$ is therefore a
\emph{relative-improvement} signal, not a claim that
fine-tuned representations linearly encode the target well
in absolute terms; the primary task-outcome signal remains
forg.$<0$ at $n{\geq}2$k.
\textbf{Cross-dataset $\Delta R^2$ comparability.}
Raw $\Delta R^2$ is not directly comparable across datasets because
it depends on the ZS Ridge floor depth.
On ETTm2 ($n{=}10$k, CUDA, $k{=}5$), the ZS Ridge floor is
$R^2(\text{ZS}){=}-24.52$, 3.5$\times$ deeper than ETTh2's
$-6.90$, which proportionally inflates the raw $\Delta R^2$
($+$6.35 on ETTm2 vs.\ $+$0.67 on ETTh2).
The cross-dataset comparable statistic is the \emph{sign test}: ETTh2 gives
\textbf{10/10} positive $\Delta R^2$ and ETTm2 \textbf{5/5}, on the five CUDA
seeds that exist for that cell.  Every dispersion in this appendix is a standard
deviation over seeds at $\text{ddof}{=}1$.

\textbf{Horizon-sweep probe: head-specificity of the $\Delta R^2$ gain.}
Table~\ref{tab:probe_horizon} reports Ridge $\Delta R^2$ across three
probe heads on four early-stopped encoders at $n{=}10$k (the
same encoders as the main-text MLP sweep).  The gain is concentrated
on the \emph{trained} 96-step head: $\Delta R^2{=}{+}0.116{\pm}0.153$
($k{=}4$).  On the untrained \emph{forecast48} head (first 48 steps
of the 96-step target, a sub-horizon the encoder was not directly
optimised for), $\Delta R^2 {=} {-}0.001{\pm}0.163$: the gain
vanishes at the horizon boundary.  On the untrained \emph{delta1}
head, a GBM probe gives $R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059{>}0$, establishing
a positive-floor reference, with $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.056{\pm}0.032$
(decodability \emph{falls} on the untrained readout).
The head-specificity pattern is consistent with the encoder drifting
toward the fine-tuning objective rather than developing generally

\section{IoT Negative-Control Details}
\label{app:cnn_detail}
% ============================================================

The IoT negative-control evaluation uses N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018n}
with 12 network-flow features and 128-timestep windows
(\texttt{data/nbaiot\_benign\_windows.npy}, shape $1096{\times}128{\times}12$);
the anomaly
score is the fraction of timesteps falling outside the 95\% CI of
Moirai's forecast over a 32-timestep scored horizon.  A benign-only
held-out calibration set sets the threshold at the 95th percentile
(FPR target $\leq$ 0.05).  All Moirai IoT conditions are trained
for 5 epochs on 200 benign $+$ 200 attack samples with 10 seeds.

\textbf{CNN-NLL from-scratch control.}
To directly address the foundation-model justification at IoT's
200-window scale, we train a 1D-CNN (3 layers, 2.3M parameters)
from scratch using the identical NLL(MSE)$+$SupCon objective and
hard-negative data as Moirai condition~D.  At 10 seeds,
CNN-NLL (AUC$=$0.598$\pm$0.041) matches fine-tuned Moirai B/D
(AUC$=$0.556$\pm$0.021) and is close to the best Moirai condition
C (AUC$=$0.629$\pm$0.022).  A CNN-MSE variant (AUC$=$0.580$\pm$0.023)
rules out the loss-function confound.  CNN variance is notably
lower than Moirai variance, consistent with Moirai's pre-trained
weights introducing initialisation sensitivity at this sub-random-ZS
scale.  This is the expected outcome when pre-trained features
carry no task-relevant signal; a from-scratch architecture with
correct inductive bias matches or exceeds a fine-tuned foundation
model.

\section{Layer-Wise Unfreeze Localization}
\label{app:layerunfreeze}
% ============================================================

Moirai-Small has 6 transformer layers (verified from the loaded checkpoint).
We added an \texttt{--unfreeze-top-n-layers} flag to
\texttt{finetune\_forecasting.py} and swept $N$, the number of top layers left
trainable, on ETTh2 $n{=}10$k, $h{=}96$.  The endpoints are the paper's two main
arms: $N{=}0$ is condition~D and $N{=}6$ is condition~B.

\textbf{Provenance, and why both arms were re-run.}  The $N{=}3$ and $N{=}6$ rows
of Table~\ref{tab:layerunfreeze} were re-run from scratch for this version, 10
seeds each in one environment
(\texttt{results/v49\_layerunfreeze\_10seed}).  The values previously printed
here traced to no run record on \emph{either} arm, and the surviving three-seed
$N{=}3$ runs had early stopping disabled, which reverses the sign of the
forgetting reading.  Since the claim is a \emph{comparison} between two unfreeze
depths, it is only traceable if both depths share one environment, so both were
re-run rather than only the arm that was missing.  Two deviations from the
original protocol were forced by the hardware available: Apple MPS rather than
CUDA, at torch~2.14, which is above \texttt{uni2ts}'s declared \texttt{<2.5} pin
but below which the NegativeBinomial component of Moirai's output mixture
collapses to \texttt{total\_count}${=}0$ on MPS and training dies in the first
NLL step; and \texttt{PYTORCH\_ENABLE\_MPS\_FALLBACK=1}, because
\texttt{aten::\_cummax\_helper} has no MPS kernel.  The $N{=}0$, $N{=}4$ and
$N{=}5$ rows are the older records, are \emph{not} matched to the re-run pair,
and read the final epoch rather than the best one; $N{=}0$ is 5 seeds, not 10.
They are context, not part of the comparison.

Because $N{=}6$ \emph{is} condition~B, re-running it also produced an unplanned
cross-device replication of the cell that
Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} reports at $n{=}10$k: the same 10 seeds, the same
configuration, CUDA/A10G against Apple MPS at a different torch version.  The two
agree: CKA $0.518$ against $0.465$ ($0.72$ standard errors of the difference) and
forgetting $-5.3$\% against $-7.9$\% ($0.90$~SE), with 7/10 and 8/10 seeds
negative.  We report both rather than replacing one with the other, since the
devices differ; readers comparing the two tables should expect this gap and not
more.

\textbf{$N{=}1$ instability.}

default LR $=10^{-4}$, despite gradient clipping at norm~1.  The 11 epochs that
completed show CKA stabilising at $\approx$0.72; we do not extract probe results
from a crashed run.  This instability is qualitatively consistent with the
LoRA-Large behaviour documented in Appendix~\ref{app:full_tables}: extreme
partial freeze concentrates gradient pressure on a small number of parameters,
requiring LR reduction to stabilise.

\textbf{Finding: the task outcome does not track the drift across unfreeze
depth.}  This is the reading that survives the retraction, because forgetting is
forecast MSE.  Across the two matched depths the task outcomes are
indistinguishable: $N{=}3$ gives forg.\ $-5.9$\% (SEM $2.0$, 9/10 seeds
negative) against $N{=}6$'s $-7.9$\% (SEM $2.1$, 8/10 negative), a 2.0-point gap
on a 2.9-point standard error of the difference, so $0.70$~SE, while the
representations they arrive at are not: CKA $0.716$ against $0.465$ is a gap of
$0.251$ at $3.5$~SE, and $\ell_2$ weight drift separates them the same way
($9.42{\pm}1.25$ against $13.47{\pm}0.75$, SEM).  Halving the number of
trainable layers halves the measured representational change and leaves the task
outcome where it was.  This is the dissociation of \S\ref{sec:exp3} localised
inside a single encoder rather than read across cells, and it is the only claim we
draw from this sweep.

\textbf{What we no longer claim.}  The earlier version of this appendix read the
same sweep through $\Delta R^2$ and concluded that the top 3 of 6 layers suffice
to reorient the encoder toward the trained objective while the lower 3 are
required for task recovery.  That account rests entirely on the withdrawn probe
quantity (\S\ref{sec:retraction}) and we do not assert it.  The $\Delta R^2$
column is retained so the withdrawn computation stays auditable; for the record
the re-run gives $+0.329{\pm}0.197$ at $N{=}3$ and $+0.534{\pm}0.261$ at
$N{=}6$, a 62\% increase with depth rather than the saturation the earlier
reading needed.

\section{Chronos-T5-Small on M4-Monthly: Full Diagnostic Results}
\label{app:chronos_detail}
% ============================================================

This appendix reports Chronos-T5-Small (T5 seq2seq, tokenised cross-entropy) on
M4-Monthly, at $200$ series, lookback $96$ and $h{=}18$, re-run from the data
upwards.  It is the only place in this paper where an appendix was deleted and
replaced rather than corrected, so what was wrong with it is worth stating
plainly.  Earlier versions reported ten seeds at two sample sizes, a final-epoch
comparison, a frozen-encoder arm, a random-init control, a $\Delta R^2$ probe and
an $84.5\%$ advantage over a linear baseline.  \emph{No run record for any of it
exists under} \texttt{results/}: the series were never retained, the selection
rule was never written down, and the gate was measured with the superseded
per-window trend denominator on the held-out split.  Under this paper's own rule
(every number traces to an executed run with an emitter and a prose check),
none of that was reportable, and none of it survives below.  What replaces it is
six runs, conditions B and D at three seeds each, whose records are in the
release, and a gate computed with the estimator \S\ref{sec:method} defines,
on the split it defines it on.  The re-run was registered
(\texttt{results/chronos\_m4/preregistration.json}) and committed before the gate
was computed, because the gate estimator was one of the things being corrected
and seeing the number first would have let the estimator be chosen to clear the
threshold.

\textbf{Data and splits.}  The cell takes the first $200$ series in file order
with at least $228$ observations of history and $18$ test values; there is no RNG
in the selection, so the set is a property of the two released CSVs, whose hashes
are in \texttt{results/data\_manifest.json}.  Each series is cut three ways:
sliding training windows over all but the last $114$ observations
($57{,}327$ usable, $27$ dropped for a constant context), one selection window
per series, and one held-out window per series whose target is the official
M4-Monthly test values.  The held-out \emph{context} does contain the $18$ values
used as the selection target, because M4 defines its test set as the $18$ steps
following the history; no held-out target is ever a training or selection target.
This replaces a split that was not one: \texttt{finetune\_chronos\_m4.py} cut a
randomly subsampled window pool at the $80$th percentile of its own index, so
windows overlapping in $95$ of $96$ steps sat on both sides of the boundary; it
early-stopped on the same windows it reported its forgetting number on; and it
initialised the early-stopping best score to the zero-shot score with the
pre-trained weights already saved as the best state
(\texttt{finetune\_chronos\_m4.py:505--507}), so the pre-trained checkpoint was
itself an early-stopping candidate, and any seed that selected it reported a CKA
of exactly $1$ by definition rather than by measurement.  How many of its seeds
did is not recoverable (that is what having no run record means), so no count
is stated here.  What the mechanism explains is available without one: an
appendix reading ``this backbone does not drift'' is the expected output of a
selection rule that can return the weights it started from.  Here the best epoch
is required to be at least $1$.

\section{Eight Denominators, One Numerator: Is the Screen a Property of the Estimator?}
\label{app:baselines}
% ============================================================

The appendix above shows the gate's verdict barely moving when the $0.20$
threshold is swept but moving for 17 of 21 Moirai cells when the baseline
estimator is swapped.  So the sensitivity analysis this paper already had varied
the parameter that does not matter, and the parameter that does had been varied
exactly once, between a wrong estimator and a right one.  This appendix varies
it across a family, and on \emph{both} splits: the selection windows every
admission decision in the paper is made on (Table~\ref{tab:baselinefamilyval}, the
31 cells with a paired frozen-encoder run) and the held-out windows the
retrospective variant scored (Table~\ref{tab:baselinefamily}, all 32 screened
cells).  The body's counts are the selection-split ones.

\textbf{What is held identical.}  $\Vb{b}{=}1{-}\text{MSE}_\text{ZS} /
\text{MSE}_\text{base}$, and only the denominator depends on the estimator, so
the numerator is read from the published gate for that split
(\texttt{results/gate\_val\_side.json} or \texttt{gate\_test\_side.json}) and never
recomputed: each column differs from the published one in exactly one respect.
Before anything is reported, \texttt{scripts/gate\_baseline\_sensitivity.py}
recomputes the \texttt{fitted} column through the alternative-baseline code path
and checks it against both the published denominator \emph{and} the published
$\Vb{b}$; all 32 cells agree to a relative tolerance of $10^{-6}$, and
the script refuses to emit a table if any does not.  The two checks are not
redundant, and the ILI cell is why: its denominator matched while its
$\Vb{b}$ did not, because that cell aggregates as a mean over features
of per-feature ratios where every other cell is a ratio of means
(\texttt{gate\_all\_cells.ili\_gate}, matching \texttt{finetune\_ili}).  A
denominator check alone would have published one row of this table on a
convention no other number in the paper uses.

\textbf{The estimators.}  \emph{Persistence} repeats the last observed value for
all $h$ steps and is the bottom of the ladder.  \emph{Seasonal naive} repeats the
last seasonal period
and fits nothing; periods are declared per dataset (24 steps for the hourly ETT,
Weather and Electricity series, 96 for 15-minute ETTm2, 52 for weekly ILI) rather
than inferred, because inferring one from an autocorrelation peak returns 1 on
the flatter series and quietly substitutes a persistence baseline.
\emph{Per-feature AR} is the paper's map with the cross-channel terms removed, so
it isolates how much of the gate rests on multivariate information.
\emph{DLinear} is a single linear map on the trend/seasonal decomposition of the
lookback, shared across channels.  \emph{MLP} is one two-layer network per channel
(hidden 128) with the penalty selected per channel on a chronological holdout;
the channels that hit the 200-iteration budget are recorded per cell.  \emph{Tuned
ridge} is the paper's own estimator with $\lambda$ selected on a chronological
20\% holdout of the training windows rather than fixed at $10^{-4}$; the selected
values span $10^{-6}$ to $10^{4}$, and on all five ETTh2 cells the selection lands
at $10^{3}$ or $10^{4}$, so the fixed $10^{-4}$ the paper uses is badly
under-regularised there.  \emph{Boosted trees} is one
\texttt{HistGradientBoostingRegressor} per feature over that feature's lags plus
the horizon step as an input feature; one model per (feature, step) would be 672
fits on a 7-feature $h{=}96$ cell.  Its rows are windows${\times}$steps, capped at
$200{,}000$ per feature, which on the Moirai cells uses 1{,}041--2{,}083 of the
10{,}165--48{,}777 available windows.  Every cap and every selected penalty is
recorded per cell in \texttt{results/gate\_baselines.json} rather than only in the
source.

\textbf{Four columns coincide on the single-channel arms, and that is arithmetic
rather than a copy.}  On the Chronos and TimesFM cells, which score one target
channel, the fitted map, per-feature AR and DLinear are the \emph{same estimator}:
with $F{=}1$ there are no cross-channel terms for AR to drop, and DLinear's
trend/seasonal split is an invertible linear reparameterisation of the lookback, so
a shared linear head on it spans exactly the maps a lookback regression spans.
Their columns therefore agree to three decimals by construction, and tuned ridge
differs from them only through $\lambda$.  A reader who sees four identical entries
on those rows is seeing the ladder collapse where the data has one dimension, not
a transcription error.

loses Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}192$ at $+0.048$.  The

TimesFM/Weather and Moirai-Small/ETTh1 $h{=}96$.  The instability of the passing

clause~(i) taken from each estimator, the definition returns \textbf{zero cells

Moirai-Base/ETTh1 at both, Moirai-Large/ETTh1 $h{=}96$ and Moirai-Large/Weather

confirmed from the other side: the degradation instances the earlier version
reported needed a denominator no stronger than a constant.

\textbf{The ladder read as a graded axis, and why the headline is a minimum.}  A
pass count per rung still treats the gate as binary.  The graded reading assigns
each cell one number, $\Vbest$, measured against the
\emph{strongest} admissible rival it faces.  That is a \textbf{minimum} over
rungs, not a maximum: a weaker denominator inflates $\Vb{b}$, so the
strongest rival is the one that leaves the smallest score.  Read that way,
\textbf{no cell of the 32 clears $0.20$ against every admissible rung}, and 15
clear it against at least one.  The two counts move in opposite directions as the
ladder grows (the first can only fall, the second can only rise), so only the
first is a claim, and adding a ninth rung could not undo it.  The five ETTh2
survivors have $\Vbest$ between $-0.738$ and $-1.460$, every
one of them against DLinear.  Both readings agree with the selection-split ones
above (no cell clears every admissible rung on either split), so the graded
conclusion does not depend on which split supplies the axis, and the value axis
\S\ref{sec:exp3} plots is the selection-split one
(\texttt{results/gate\_baselines\_val.json}) because its $x$ and $y$ must not
share windows.  Each file stores $\Vbest$ per cell alongside
which rung supplied it.

\subsection{A third window set: the ladder on the train tail}

The selection-split gate meets the protocol a reviewer would ask for (the gate's
windows are disjoint from the outcome's), but not its residual: the selection windows
also drove early stopping and checkpoint choice, so the outcome depends on them
indirectly.  So we scored the same nine denominators a third time, on the last
$20\%$ of each cell's \emph{training} region, held back by index rather than inferred

\subsection{Matching the lookback: is the verdict an artifact of the context asymmetry?}
\label{app:baselines:matchedlb}

Moirai is evaluated with $96{+}h$ steps of history (Figure~\ref{fig:windowlayout})
while the baseline is given the last $96$, and \S\ref{sec:forecasting} argues that
this favours the pre-trained model.  An argument is not a measurement, so here is
the measurement: the same ridge map, the same windows, the same fitting region,
the same normalisation and the same $\lambda$, given all $96{+}h$ available steps
instead of the last $96$.  Only the integer passed to the fit changes, so the two
columns of Tables~\ref{tab:matchedlbval} and~\ref{tab:matchedlb} differ in the
baseline's information set and in nothing else; the numerator is the same stored
$\text{MSE}_\text{ZS}$, and the \texttt{fitted} column reproduces the published
denominator on all 21 Moirai cells of each split to a relative tolerance of
$10^{-6}$ before anything is reported.

\textbf{The effect is small and its sign depends on the split, because a matched
map is not automatically a stronger one.}  On the selection windows (the split
every admission decision is made on), the matched map is the stronger of the two
on \textbf{12 of 21} cells, and the count clearing $0.20$ falls from
\textbf{7 to 6}: \textbf{Moirai-Base/ETTm2 $h{=}192$} is the one cell that flips,
from $+0.211$ to $+0.125$.  On the held-out windows the direction reverses.  There
the matched map is stronger on only \textbf{7 of 21}, the count rises from
\textbf{5 to 7}, and the two cells that flip do so \emph{into} the survivor set
(Moirai-Small and Moirai-Base on ETTm2 at $h{=}192$).  The reason is visible in
the two MSE columns: the matched map estimates $(96{+}h){\times}F$ inputs from the
same training windows, which fits the training region better and generalises to
the held-out region worse.  So giving the baseline the model's full history moves
the count by one or two cells in whichever direction the extra parameters happen
to pay off, and no reading in this paper turns on either move.

\textbf{Why this is reported as its own axis rather than as a ninth rung, and what
the separation does not hide.}  The ladder above varies the estimator
\emph{family} at a fixed lookback, and its rungs feed the graded
$\Vbest$ that \S\ref{sec:exp3} uses as a covariate; a rung that
varies the \emph{information set} instead would move that minimum for reasons
belonging to a different question.  The separation protects no count, and we
checked rather than asserted that: folding the matched column into the graded
minimum leaves the number of cells clearing $0.20$ against every admissible rung
at \textbf{0 of 21 on both splits}, changes \textbf{no} cell's graded verdict, and
makes the matched map the strongest admissible rival on \textbf{0} cells on the
selection split and \textbf{3} on the held-out split.

\subsection{The numerator is not deterministic either: the evaluator's sampling noise}
\label{app:zsnoise}

Every denominator above is a fitted estimator, and the eight rungs ask whether the
screen is a property of that choice.  The \emph{numerator} deserves the same
question.  Moirai's forecast is the median of $20$ sampled paths, so a cell's
zero-shot MSE is a measurement, not a
constant: two runs of one cell on the same deterministic windows record two
different values (Appendix~\ref{app:moirai_median}).  Each gate value in this paper
is therefore one draw, and the width of that draw sets the resolution at which a
$0.20$ operating point can be read at all.

We can measure it, because the records contain repeats.  Across the $26$ Moirai
cell-scorings (the $21$-cell retrospective grid and batch 3's five) there are
$222$ independent zero-shot measurements, every one of them the same frozen
checkpoint on the same windows.  Recomputing $R^2_\text{task}$ from each gives a
per-cell spread of at most $0.0244$, on Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}96$; substituting
the all-replicate mean for the registered denominator moves $R^2_\text{task}$ by at
most $0.0037$ on batch 1, $0.0035$ on batch 2 and $0.0011$ on batch 3.
% "batch 2" appears exactly once in this paper, here, and a reader meeting a numbered batch that is
% reported nowhere is entitled to wonder what was in it. Glossed rather than dropped: its zero-shot
% records are a genuine INPUT to batch 3's registered denominator (cell_matrix.new_moirai_cells reads
% them through extra_a_dirs), so the number above depends on them and the arm's provenance does not.
(Batch 2 was abandoned before any outcome run: it contributed six zero-shot
measurements, which enter batch 3's registered denominator, and no condition-B or
condition-D record anywhere in this paper.)

\textbf{The comparison that matters is per cell, not global.}  That largest spread,
$0.0244$, is bigger than the tightest margin to the operating point anywhere
($0.0109$, Moirai-Base/ETTm2 $h{=}192$ at $R^2_\text{task}{=}{+}0.2109$), and
pairing the two would say the screen sits inside its own noise.  It does not: they
are different cells, and the cell carrying the $0.0244$ sits $0.577$ from $0.20$,
where noise of any size cannot change a call.  Sampling noise on a cell can only
threaten that cell's own call, so margin and noise must be taken together per cell.
The minimum of that ratio over all $26$ cell-scorings is $9.2\times$, at the
tightest cell; and directly, \textbf{no cell's $0.20$ call flips under any single
replicate substitution}, which the emitter asserts rather than reports, so a
future record that broke it would fail the re-derivation instead of shipping.

Two consequences we accept.  First, the screen's resolution is roughly $10^{-2}$ in
$R^2_\text{task}$, so Moirai-Base/ETTm2 $h{=}192$ is a cell whose admission we would
not defend on a remeasurement at a $0.21$ threshold, which is why
Table~\ref{tab:gate_sensitivity} sweeps the threshold on the pre-registered cells
rather than defending one number.  Second, this noise is why the pre-registered
roots are read \texttt{condition\_A} only: averaging the outcome runs'
re-measurements into a registered denominator moves the predictor after its
outcomes exist, and at ${\sim}10^{-3}$ it does so far too quietly to be noticed
downstream.  An earlier revision of our own code did exactly that, and
item~(g) of Appendix~\ref{app:corrections} records the repair; had it shipped,
\textbf{$0$ of the $26$} admissions would have differed, which is precisely why
nothing downstream caught it.

\section{Is the Zero a Property of the Runs or of the Definition?}
\label{app:defsensitivity}
% ============================================================

The paper's headline is that no cell meets its definition of damaging a
demonstrated pre-trained capability, and the first fair objection is that the
definition does the work. It requires condition-B forgetting positive in
\emph{every} seed and condition-D forgetting negative in \emph{every} seed;
unanimity over ten seeds is a demanding bar, and if a simple majority produced
five degradation cells the negative result would be a property of the rule rather
than of the runs. That objection is answerable from the stored per-seed sign
counts with no new runs.

Table~\ref{tab:defsensitivity} walks a ladder of definitions from the weakest
defensible one (the two means alone, with seed signs unchecked) up to ours,
crossed with both gate thresholds so the sensitivity to \emph{both} free
parameters is one table rather than two. Two exclusions apply identically at every
rung, exactly as \texttt{degradation\_cells()} applies them: cells with no
zero-shot test reference cannot be scored at all (there are none), and the five
Chronos cells are excluded because a head/decoder mismatch makes their
per-condition forgetting non-comparable (limitation~iv). Admitting them at the
relaxed rungs would make loosening the rule appear to find degradation cells when
what it found is a measurement already known to be invalid. Both exclusions are
properties of the measurement rather than of the outcome, so applying them
uniformly is not a selection on the result. That leaves 26 scorable cells, of
which 7 clear a gate of $0.10$ and 7 clear $0.20$: under the fitted baseline the
two thresholds select the same set, so the gate column is a sensitivity check that
happens to move nothing rather than a second free parameter.

\textbf{Unanimity is not what produces the zero.} At the paper's gate threshold
the largest count anywhere on the ladder is \textbf{two}, and it is produced by a
single rung (seed counts only, half the seeds agreeing), which is the one rung
that drops the means clause entirely. Of the six rungs that keep it, the
\emph{weakest}, the two means with seed signs unchecked, admits one, and requiring
70\% of seeds, 80\%, unanimity, or a one-sided $t$-test on forgetting$_\text{B}$
all give zero. Relaxing the gate to $0.10$ changes none of these counts, so 5 of
the 8 rungs yield zero at both. There is no setting of these two parameters at which the paper's
conclusion becomes ``several cells degrade''.

cells, three are strongly \emph{improved} by full fine-tuning rather than
marginally harmed (forgetting$_\text{B}$ $-31.44{\pm}3.65$, $-36.35{\pm}2.80$
and $-42.42{\pm}0.47$~SEM, with every seed agreeing), and two more improve on the
mean, both ETTm2 at $h{=}192$ ($-1.10{\pm}3.37$ and $-8.45{\pm}3.23$). The
seventh, Moirai-Small/ETTh2 at $h{=}96$, does have a positive mean
forgetting$_\text{B}$ of $+9.71{\pm}10.58$, but fails the means clause on the
\emph{other} side, for the reason the next paragraph gives.

\textbf{Means and seed counts can disagree in direction, and we report both
families rather than absorb the disagreement.} Moirai-Small/ETTh2 at $h{=}96$ has
a mean forgetting$_\text{D}$ of $+6.91$ while 9 of its 10 seeds are negative: a
single outlier drags the mean across zero. A ladder that silently required the
means clause at every rung would hide that, and ``majority seed-consistency'' is
what the objection proposes as a \emph{replacement} for unanimity, not as an
extra hurdle on top of it. The last two rows therefore drop the means clause
entirely, and the first of them is the only row on the whole ladder that admits
two cells, which is how Moirai-Small/ETTm2 at $h{=}192$ becomes visible at all.

\textbf{Which definition the paper uses, and why.} We keep unanimity. The reason
is not that it yields the fewest cells (at gate $0.20$ 5 of the 8 rungs
also yield zero), but that a degradation claim is a claim that fine-tuning
\emph{reliably} destroys something, and a cell whose seeds disagree on the sign
of the harm is not evidence of that. The cost of the choice is that it can
discard a real but noisy effect, which is exactly what the $h{=}192$ cell would
be if its true forgetting$_\text{B}$ were small and positive; that cell is
therefore reported by name in the body rather than dropped silently.

\section{Paired Inference on Encoder Adaptation, and What These Seed Counts Can
Resolve}
\label{app:pairedinference}
% ============================================================

A unanimity count is not inference.  This appendix reports $\denc$ per cell as a
paired estimate with an interval, an effect size, two $p$-values and a
multiplicity-adjusted $q$; states the three-way call the body is built on; and
then says plainly what the matrix's seed counts can and cannot resolve.

\input{tables/paired_inference}

\textbf{The call, and why it has three outcomes.}  Thirty-one cells are tested,
so we adjust: Benjamini--Hochberg across the cells within this contrast gives the
$q$ column, and a cell is called decisive only at $q{<}0.05$.  That yields
\textbf{2 cells where freezing is decisively better, 4 where adapting is
decisively better, and 25 inconclusive}.  The third category is the substantive
addition.  Our published criterion recorded a cell with no measurable effect and a
cell with a large effect its seeds disagreed about identically, as ``not
degrading''; under the interval rule the second says \emph{inconclusive}, which is
what it is, and the minimum detectable effect below is what separates the two.

\textbf{Where the decisive cells sit relative to the screen.}  Both cells where
freezing is decisively better, Moirai-Small/ETTh1 at $h{=}96$ ($\denc{=}{+}22.1$,
$q{=}0.011$) and $h{=}192$ ($+13.5$, $q{=}0.014$), \textbf{fail} the screen, at
$\Vb{\text{ridge}}$ of $-0.177$ and $-0.347$.  Two of the four cells where adapting
is decisively better, Moirai-Base/ETTh2 $h{=}192$ ($-20.2$, $q{=}0.018$) and
Moirai-Large/ETTh2 $h{=}96$ ($-20.8$, $q{=}0.022$), \textbf{pass} it.  So on this
matrix the screen admits cells where adaptation decisively wins and rejects the
only cells where preservation decisively wins.  That is a sharper statement against
the screen than the unanimity tally could make, and it is stated here because it
counts against our own instrument.

\textbf{Both degradation criteria are reported, and both return zero.}  Clause (i)
of the definition in \S\ref{sec:method} is unchanged.  Replacing the unanimity
clauses (ii) and (iii) with interval clauses (the CI on
$\Delta_\text{FT}{=}\text{B}{-}\text{ZS}$ entirely above zero, the CI on
$\Delta_\text{frz}{=}\text{D}{-}\text{ZS}$ entirely below) selects
\textbf{zero cells}, exactly as the pre-registered unanimity version does.  The two
rules can disagree in principle, in either direction, so the agreement is a result
and not a tautology; \texttt{scripts/paired\_inference.py} computes both and
cross-checks the unanimity list against \texttt{cell\_matrix.degradation\_cells},
which is the definition of record, rather than trusting two spellings of one rule
to stay in step.

\textbf{The zero-shot reference's own error is propagated, and only where it
belongs.}  On the 21 Moirai cells the zero-shot denominator is a per-cell mean over
condition-A seeds rather than each seed's own measurement, so the B$-$ZS and D$-$ZS
intervals above are widened by that mean's SEM,
$\text{se}{=}\sqrt{\text{se}_\text{within}^2+\text{se}_\text{ZS}^2}$.  $\denc$ is
\emph{not} widened and must not be: the reference cancels from its numerator and
survives only as a common scale factor.  That asymmetry is one of the reasons
$\denc$ is the estimand.  The widening is material on 13 cells and is nominally
zero on 8, where the reference ran a single condition-A seed; on those the
reference's error is \emph{unestimated}, not zero, and we say so rather than let a
$0.00$ read as a check that passed.

\input{tables/power_mde}

\textbf{What these seed counts could have detected.}  Table~\ref{tab:power_mde}
turns ``the interval is wide'' into a number for the seven cells the screen admits,
which are the only cells about which we make a preservation recommendation.  At the
multiplicity-adjusted level the call actually uses, \textbf{4 of the 7 are
unresolvable within our feasibility cap of 20 paired seeds}: they would need 29,
36, 182 and 212.  Two of the remaining three are already decisive.  So on the cells
where a practitioner would consult this screen, the honest summary is that our
design settles two of seven and cannot settle four of seven at any budget we could
run, which is a limitation of this matrix, not evidence that the effects are
absent, and it is the reason \S\ref{sec:method} reports an interval rather than a
sign count.

\textbf{The exact test's resolution is set by $N$ alone.} A sign-flip permutation
test on $N$ paired differences has $2^N$ assignments, so its smallest attainable
two-sided $p$ is $2/2^N$: $0.25$ at $N{=}3$, $0.0625$ at $N{=}5$, $0.002$ at
$N{=}10$.  The paired-$N$ histogram across the 31 cells is $\{3{:}24,\ 5{:}4,\
10{:}3\}$, so \textbf{only 3 of 31 cells can reach $p{<}0.05$ without a
distributional assumption}, and only one does (ILI, $p{=}0.002$).  Thirteen cells
have a $t$-interval excluding zero where the exact test cannot reject at any
effect size.  We report both columns rather than the more favourable one: the
$t$-test's rejections are real inferences under its normality assumption, and the
exact column is the price of not making that assumption.

\textbf{Why the reported interval is the $t$-interval, not the bootstrap.} At
$N{=}3$ every percentile-bootstrap resample mean lies inside $[\min, \max]$ of the
three differences, so a same-sign triple excludes zero \emph{by construction}.
Such an interval reports the sign of the data, not the uncertainty of the mean.
The bootstrap is computed and stored in \texttt{results/paired\_inference.json};
the table prints the paired $t$-interval beside the exact $p$.

\textbf{The aggregate straddles zero.} Averaging cell means and resampling
$(\text{backbone}, \text{dataset})$ clusters, the 20 value-cells give
$\denc{=}{+}0.7$ with CI $[-11.1, {+}14.1]$ and 10 of 20 positive; all 31 give
$+2.9$ with CI $[-12.7, {+}15.1]$ and 16 of 31 positive.  Neither excludes zero in
either direction.  This is the honest aggregate statement of the paper's central
contrast: across these benchmarks we cannot establish that allowing encoder
adaptation systematically helps \emph{or} hurts, which is a different and weaker
claim than either regime law the literature offers.

\section{The Graded Value Axis: Is the Dissociation Confined to Cells With Nothing
to Lose?}
\label{app:valueaxis}
% ============================================================

The screen in the body is binary: a cell either clears $\Vb{b}{=}0.20$ or
it does not.  That discards the only interesting covariate, and it leaves open the
single strongest objection to this paper's negative result: that drift fails to
predict $\denc$ only \emph{because} 16 of our 31 cells have no measured advantage
to lose, so we are reading noise where there is nothing to read.  If that were
right, the drift-to-outcome relation would strengthen with pre-trained value, and
our finding would be a statement about weak benchmarks rather than about the
diagnostic.  This appendix estimates that dependence two ways and reports an
interval rather than a verdict.

\textbf{The axis.} Value here is the graded score of
Appendix~\ref{app:baselines}: $\Vbest$ against the \emph{strongest
admissible} rung of the eight-baseline ladder, which is a \textbf{minimum} over
rungs, since $\Vb{b}{=}1-\text{MSE}_\text{ZS}/\text{MSE}_\text{base}$ and a
weaker rival inflates it.  It runs from $-1.638$ (Moirai-Small/ILI) to $+0.058$
(Moirai-Small/ETTh2 $h{=}96$) with mean $-0.610$, and its ceiling below $0.20$ is
the same fact as ``no cell clears the gate against every admissible rung''.  A
cell with no admissible rung at all is \emph{dropped}, never scored as zero
value: a missing denominator and a denominator of zero are different facts, and
the second would place the cell at the origin of this very axis.

\textbf{The rank answer: the relation is not stronger where there is more to

restriction the objection asks for does not sharpen the relation, and the
value-cell figure is not evidence that drift orders the outcome: it pools all three

statistic is $\rho{=}{+}0.168$ with an interval spanning zero.  We do not test the
difference of those two $\rho$s: a difference of rank statistics on 20 and 11 cells
from 11 and 7 clusters has no usable sampling distribution, and reporting one would

$+39.8$ with CI $[-55.1, {+}179.4]$.  Two things to say about it and no more.  The
point estimate has the \emph{opposite} sign to the objection (drift orders the
outcome slightly \emph{less} well where there is more value), and the interval
spans zero so widely that only an interaction several times larger than the CKA
slope itself would have been visible at 15 clusters.  \textbf{So this is not a
refutation of the objection; it is the statement that these cells cannot separate
it from no dependence at all.}  That is a weaker claim than we would like to make,
and it is the one the data support.

\textbf{And value does not order the outcome by itself.}  The graded score against
$\denc$ gives $\rho{=}{-}0.270$, CI $[-0.592, {+}0.392]$ across all 31 cells and
$\rho{=}{-}0.229$, CI $[-0.755, {+}0.284]$ across the 20 value-cells, the same
non-result as CKA, on the covariate that was supposed to rescue it.  The right
panel of Figure~\ref{fig:valueaxis} is that row: every point lies left of the
$0.20$ line, and the cells that improve most under encoder adaptation are not the
ones with the least to lose.

\section{Cross-Cell Statistics, and a Pooled Analysis We Do Not License}
\label{app:crosscell}
% ============================================================

Across the 31 intervention cells of Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} we ask whether
either observational diagnostic (CKA, or $\ell_2$ weight drift) orders the
held-out intervention contrast B$-$D. Two predictors rather than one is
deliberate: if \emph{both} fail, the statement is about observational measures
of fine-tuning in general and not about CKA specifically. The statistic is
Spearman $\rho$ with a $10{,}000$-resample bootstrap CI (seed~$0$), reported at
two resampling levels: over cells, and over the $(\text{backbone},
\text{dataset})$ clusters that Appendix~\ref{app:clustered} argues is the correct
level, and emitted by \texttt{scripts/clustered\_inference.py}. Pooled over all
31 cells:

\input{tables/clustered_pooled}

\noindent The $\ell_2$ row covers 25 of the 31 cells because the five Chronos
cells and ILI retain no weight-drift measurement in their run records. No
$p$-values are reported in this appendix or the next: they were previously
computed from the cell-level resampling, which mis-states the dependence between
cells, and this analysis is descriptive (Appendix~\ref{app:clustered}).

\textbf{The pooled CKA row is not a licensed analysis, and it is the row that
would help us.} The three backbones occupy almost disjoint CKA ranges (Moirai
$0.36$--$0.97$, Chronos $0.09$--$0.23$, TimesFM $0.25$--$0.56$), \emph{and}
differ systematically in outcome: every Chronos and TimesFM cell favours
adaptation, and every large positive B$-$D is Moirai's. A rank
correlation pooled over 31 cells drawn from three such groups therefore
measures which backbone a cell came from at least as much as how far its
encoder moved: it is a textbook setting for a group-level artefact of the
Simpson's-paradox kind, and CKA magnitude is representation-dependent, which is
why this paper compares CKA only within a backbone
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}). We stress the direction of the incentive.
The pooled figure is the \emph{favourable} one (it is the only reading in this
appendix under which a diagnostic we introduced orders the intervention with an
interval excluding zero), and the body nevertheless leads with the
within-backbone figure. We decline to make the positive claim the pooled number
would support, because the restriction that forbids it was fixed before these
cells were run rather than chosen after seeing them.

\textbf{Chronos and TimesFM cannot be asked at all.} With $n{=}5$ and $n{=}4$
the bootstrap resamples only a handful of distinct rank permutations and the
interval spans nearly the entire admissible range, $[-1.000, +1.000]$ and

statistic can take on four cells, and its interval covers almost everything.

\textbf{$\ell_2$ weight drift does no better, and it is the second of the two
diagnostics we defined.} Pooled (already the reading most likely to manufacture
a correlation), weight drift gives $\rho{=}{+}0.118$ with a CI that includes
zero at both resampling levels, widening from $[-0.300, +0.518]$ over cells to
$[-0.489, +0.545]$ over its nine clusters. Since \S\ref{sec:method} defines
exactly these two representation
diagnostics, their joint failure is the more useful statement: it is not that we
picked the wrong summary of the encoder's movement, but that neither summary we
defined orders the outcome. The two are also not redundant and can move
independently: a stack whose weight drift is \emph{exactly} zero can read CKA
well below one when the projections feeding it are trainable, which is what
condition~D does on TimesFM, where $\ell_2$ drift over the transformer stack is
$0.00$ by construction while its measured CKA runs $0.38$--$0.89$
(\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}; the analogous Moirai
control is Appendix~\ref{app:strictfreeze}). A geometric battery (RBF CKA,
SVCCA, orthogonal Procrustes, mean cosine and effective-rank change, on the 59
encoders that retained checkpoints) is Appendix~\ref{app:driftmetrics}. None of
those measures orders the outcome either, and they do not order the
\emph{encoders} alike, so the failure is not a property of the summary we
happened to pick.

\textbf{A backbone fixed-effects reading, and its limits.} The natural
alternative to subsetting is to absorb backbone identity and let CKA enter as a
continuous regressor, which answers the objection that we adopted the split that
removes the significant relationship. We do fit it
(Table~\ref{tab:clustered_fe}, \texttt{scripts/cka\_fixed\_effects.py}): the
within-backbone slope of held-out B$-$D on CKA is $+39.1$~pp per unit CKA with
backbone fixed effects and $+39.9$ once CKA${\times}$backbone interactions are
added, against $+47.7$ with no backbone term at all. Both adjusted intervals
include zero once clusters rather than cells are resampled. So the reduction from
$+47.7$ to $+39.1$ shows that absorbing backbone identity does \emph{not} by
itself destroy the pooled slope (most of it survives adjustment), while the
interval shows that 31 cells drawn from 15 clusters cannot pin the surviving
slope down. What we do \emph{not} do is fit variance components: with $22$, $5$
and $4$ cells in the three groups there is no repeated-measures structure to
estimate them from, so a mixed model over three groups of that size is not
identified and any interval it produced would be an artefact of the
specification. Cells here are unequally powered and not independent, so every
cross-cell number in this appendix is descriptive.

\section{Is the Reading Specific to CKA? Six Measures on 59 Encoders}
\label{app:driftmetrics}
% ============================================================

The body reads drift through linear CKA, so a reader is entitled to ask whether
``drift does not order the outcome'' is a fact about representations or a fact
about CKA. Appendix~\ref{app:crosscell} gives the answer that covers the whole
matrix: $\ell_2$ weight drift, a geometrically unrelated quantity defined in
\S\ref{sec:method} alongside CKA, fails on the same 31 cells. This appendix
gives the second answer, on geometry, and states up front what it cannot do.

\textbf{What it cannot do.} Only the $n{=}10$k runs retained encoder
checkpoints. The 22-cell Moirai matrix is $n{=}500/1{,}000$ and kept none, so
this battery \emph{cannot} recompute the within-Moirai $\rho$ under a different
measure; that would require retraining the matrix, on different hardware, which
would perturb every number in the paper. What it can do is test
measure-specificity on the 59 encoders that do exist: Moirai-Small at $h{=}96$,
$n{=}10$k, on ETTh1, ETTh2 and ETTm2, under full fine-tuning and under two
layer-unfreezing regimes (top~3 of~6, and all~6), in seven
(dataset, run-set, regime) groups.

\textbf{Measured on the representations the paper's CKA column describes.} Each
run's record stores \texttt{final\_cka}, computed during training on the first
300 validation evaluation windows with the encoder output mean-pooled. The
battery (\texttt{scripts/drift\_metric\_battery.py}) rebuilds exactly those
windows (extended lookback $96{+}96$, raw values, the model normalising
internally), and recomputes linear CKA as a self-check: it agrees with the
stored value to four decimal places on all 59 encoders, so every other column is
known to describe the same representations rather than a similar-looking set.
The checkpoint load asserts that \texttt{load\_state\_dict(strict=False)}
reports no missing and no unexpected keys, because a namespace mismatch loads
nothing silently and returns CKA ${\approx}1$, which reads as ``no drift''.

\textbf{The more invariant the measure, the less drift there is to see.} Linear
CKA spans $0.194$ to $0.915$ across these encoders. SVCCA (invariant to any
invertible linear map, so it asks whether the same information survives in a
possibly rotated and rescaled basis) spans only $0.726$ to $0.939$, and
orthogonal-Procrustes similarity $0.715$ to $0.947$. The cell that CKA scores at
$0.194$ is at $0.73$ on SVCCA. This is the concrete form of the objection
limitation~(7) concedes: a large part of what CKA reports as drift is a change of
basis under which the subspace, and with it the linearly decodable content, is
largely preserved. It is also why we do not claim CKA is invalid (it measures
what it measures), only that a paper cannot infer damage from it.

\textbf{The measures do not order the encoders alike, so ``drift'' is not one
quantity.} Rank correlations among them run from $+0.073$ (linear CKA against
mean cosine, the least invariant measure in the battery) through $+0.412$ (linear
CKA against RBF CKA) to $+0.845$ (RBF CKA against Procrustes). Choosing a
similarity measure chooses an ordering of these encoders. We had expected the
opposite (a family of near-clones), and report the disagreement because it makes
the next paragraph the load-bearing one.

\textbf{No measure orders the outcome either.} Against selection-split
forgetting, the pooled rank correlations run from $+0.350$ (linear CKA) to
$-0.287$ (mean cosine), and the pooled figures are the flattering ones: within
groups, where dataset identity is held fixed, every similarity measure changes
sign across the six groups (linear CKA $[-0.19,{+}0.60]$, RBF CKA
$[-0.36,{+}0.90]$, SVCCA $[-0.70,{+}0.35]$, Procrustes $[-0.52,{+}0.80]$, mean
cosine $[-0.67,{+}0.60]$). Six measures spanning no invariance to full
linear-map invariance therefore reproduce the body's reading rather than
overturning it: the disagreement among them does not resolve into any one of them
ordering the outcome. The single exception is not a similarity measure at all.
The effective-rank ratio (how many directions the representation uses after
fine-tuning against before) is above $1$ on 55 of the 59 encoders
($0.969$--$1.250$, so fine-tuning generally \emph{expands} the spectrum) and is
the one column with no negative group value ($[+0.00,{+}0.65]$). At six groups,
one backbone and one sample size we record that as a lead for future work, not a
diagnostic; it is exactly the kind of pattern this paper's own prospective test
(\S\ref{sec:analysis:prospective}) was built to keep us honest about.

measured pre-trained advantage are the Moirai/ETTh2 cells, and three of those
five are the cells where full fine-tuning beats the frozen encoder by the most
(B$-$D $-20.2$ to $-22.3$~pp), while the cells with the most negative
gates (Weather and ETTh1) are where freezing wins by tens of points, up to
$+62.1$~pp. A practitioner following ``preserve where the gate fires'' would
therefore freeze precisely where adapting the encoder pays best. We report this
at the strength it has: a within-Moirai point estimate on 22 retrospective cells
from six series, with a clustered interval that spans zero. \textbf{The negative
sign is a description of these cells, not an interval-supported claim, and the
magnitude is not estimated to any useful precision.} The mechanism above is
legible cell by cell in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} and does not depend on the
correlation surviving; that is why we still report the direction.

gives $\rho{=}{+}0.800$, CI $[+0.333, +1.000]$, forg$_\text{B}$ gives

gives $\rho{=}{+}0.500$, CI $[-0.667, +1.000]$, and forg$_\text{B}$ gives
$-0.100$. These are reported for completeness and are uninformative at four and
five cells: a bootstrap over four cells resamples only a few distinct rank
patterns, so even the TimesFM gate interval, which nominally excludes zero, is
an artefact of that granularity and we read nothing from it. Chronos's
forg$_\text{B}$ carries a further defect and is flagged
\texttt{forg\_confounded} in the code: a head/decoder mismatch means its B-side
number is not a clean per-condition forgetting measurement, so it is retained
only to show where the forg$_\text{B}$ rule breaks and is never quoted as a
forgetting value.

\textbf{Nothing here is a replacement diagnostic.} The honest summary of this
appendix and of Appendix~\ref{app:crosscell} together is that no observational
quantity we measured orders the intervention in a usable direction: CKA and
$\ell_2$ drift do not order it, the gate orders it \emph{backwards}, and the one
predictor that tracks it does so by identity and only after the run that would
have told you the answer anyway. On the evidence in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all},
the only reliable way to know whether encoder adaptation helped a given cell is
to run both conditions on that cell.

\section{Cells Are Not Independent: Clustered Intervals for Every Cross-Cell Statistic}
\label{app:clustered}
% ============================================================

Every interval in Appendices~\ref{app:crosscell} and~\ref{app:predictor} was
previously computed by resampling \emph{cells}, which treats each cell as an
independent observation. They are not. A cell is a (backbone, size, dataset,
horizon) combination, so the 31 cells draw on 3 backbones and 7 series: the
Moirai-Small, -Base and -Large runs on ETTh2 at $h{=}96$ and $h{=}192$ share the
series, the chronological split, the normalisation constants and (at fixed
size) the pretrained checkpoint. Resampling cells therefore counts shared facts
repeatedly and produces intervals that are too narrow, and a $p$-value computed
from that scheme is not conventional inferential evidence. This is a fair
objection to how the paper reported these numbers and it is answerable without
new runs.

\textbf{The resampling unit.} We take the cluster to be the
$(\text{backbone}, \text{dataset})$ pair: the level at which the pretraining
corpus, the series, the split and the checkpoint family are shared. Clusters are
drawn with replacement and all of a drawn cluster's cells enter together
(\texttt{scripts/cluster\_keys.py}, one definition shared by
\texttt{cell\_matrix.py} and \texttt{cka\_fixed\_effects.py} so the two cannot
drift apart). A draw spanning fewer than three distinct predictor values admits no
rank correlation and is discarded, which is why the draws kept are reported
alongside every interval; on the 31-cell and 22-cell analyses the discard rate is
under $0.1\%$, and it is material only on the four- and five-cell arms, which
carry no information anyway. The effective sample size falls sharply: 31 cells are
15 clusters, the 22 Moirai cells are 6, and the 25 cells with a weight-drift
record are 9.

\textbf{What changes, in full.} Of the eleven cross-cell correlations reported in
this paper, \textbf{exactly one changes verdict} when the interval is taken over
clusters instead of cells, and it changes against us. Both levels appear side by
side in Table~\ref{tab:crosscell} and in the two tables of
Appendices~\ref{app:crosscell} and~\ref{app:predictor}; the summary is:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Unchanged, and these carry the body's claims.} CKA does not order
  B$-$D within Moirai at either level ($\rho{=}{+}0.168$; cells
  $[-0.328, +0.598]$, clusters $[-0.469, +0.717]$). $\ell_2$ weight drift does not
  order it at either level, pooled ($\rho{=}{+}0.118$) or within Moirai
  ($\rho{=}{+}0.318$). forg$_\text{B}$ still orders it with an interval far from
  zero ($\rho{=}{+}0.894$; clusters $[+0.594, +0.965]$), which is expected,
  since that relationship is close to an identity
  (Appendix~\ref{app:predictor}). The unlicensed pooled CKA correlation still
  excludes zero, and we still decline to read it.
\item \textbf{Changed: the gate's anti-predictive interval.} On the retrospective
  test-side gate, $\rho{=}{-}0.518$ between the gate and B$-$D moves from
  $[-0.840, -0.040]$ over cells to $[-0.810, +0.500]$ over the six Moirai
  clusters. \textbf{We withdraw the ``excludes zero'' reading} and report the
  direction as a description of these 22 cells. On the primary selection-split
  gate the correlation is $\rho{=}{-}0.293$ and no level excludes zero, so the
  withdrawal is not what the split flip rests on. The mechanism (the highest-gate
  cells are the ETTh2 and ETTm2 cells where adaptation pays best) remains visible
  cell by cell in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all} and does not rest on the interval.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Backbone fixed effects.} The cluster bootstrap also lets us report the
adjustment the previous version of this appendix declined to fit
(Table~\ref{tab:clustered_fe}).

Most of the unadjusted slope survives adjustment ($+47.7$ falls only to

\section{Does CKA Add Predictive Information Beyond Backbone Identity? A Pre-Specified
Out-of-Sample Test}
\label{app:loco}
% ============================================================

A wide interval on a within-backbone slope shows that a design cannot resolve a
sign; it does not show that the predictor is uninformative, and the previous
appendix says so in those words.  That leaves the reader with a genuine question
we had not answered: \emph{if you already know which backbone you are on, the
horizon and the training-set size, does measuring CKA improve your prediction of
the value of encoder adaptation?}  This appendix answers the decision-relevant
version of that question out of sample.

\textbf{The analysis was fixed before it was run, and the guarantee is weaker than
the prospective arm's.}  The model ladder, the covariate construction, the
deciding statistic and the sentence this paper would print under each of three
possible outcomes were all written to
\texttt{results/preregister\_loco.json} and committed before any of the models was
fitted.  That is a real guarantee against specification search, and it is
\emph{not} the guarantee of \S\ref{sec:analysis:prospective}: there the outcome runs did not
yet exist, whereas here the outcome (held-out B$-$D) was already computed and
already published in Table~\ref{tab:heldout_all}.  What is frozen is the
\emph{analysis}, not a prediction about unseen data, and the registration file says
so on its first page.  The registered prediction was
$\Delta R^2 \leq 0$.

\textbf{The ladder, and why dataset identity is absent from it.}
Table~\ref{tab:cka_loco} fits five nested models to $\denc$ over all 31 scored
cells, validated by leaving out one $(\text{backbone}, \text{dataset})$ cluster at
a time.  The review that prompted this analysis asked for dataset fixed effects
\emph{and} leave-one-cluster-out validation; those two requests are jointly
unsatisfiable, and satisfying them naively would have produced a table that looks
right and means nothing.  A cluster \emph{is} a (backbone, dataset) pair, so
holding one out removes the only cells that identify that dataset's fixed effect,
its column is all-zero in the training fold, and the fold silently scores a model
nobody specified.  Three of the seven series (Electricity, Electricity-7, ILI) are
covered by a single backbone each, so no other fold could identify them even in
principle.  We therefore run two schemes and label each for what it is.  The
leave-one-cluster-out ladder excludes dataset fixed effects and is genuinely
out-of-sample; it is where $\Delta R^2$ is computed.  The variant that
\emph{carries} dataset fixed effects is reported as an in-sample fit ($R^2$ rising
from $+0.642$ to $+0.711$, an incremental $+0.069$ from CKA, on 7 series and 31
cells with 12 parameters) and is explicitly not called validation.

\textbf{The result.}  The registered prediction fires.  Knowing the backbone buys
$R^2_\text{LOCO}{=}{+}0.137$; adding horizon and $\log n$ takes it to $+0.201$;
adding CKA \emph{lowers} it to $+0.176$.  $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.025$, the median
bootstrap draw is $-0.083$, and adding CKA fails to help in $69\%$ of
cluster-bootstrap draws.  Two conventions in that sentence are worth stating.
First, out-of-sample $R^2$ is scored against the \textbf{training-fold mean},
never the held-out fold's own mean: against its own mean a fold with little
internal spread looks catastrophic and one with a lot looks good, so the aggregate
would report fold composition rather than model quality.  The intercept-only model
scores exactly $+0.000$, which is the check that this convention is implemented as
described.  Second, mean absolute error is reported beside $R^2$ because the folds
are unbalanced (cluster sizes run from 1 to 5 cells), and MAE moves in the same
direction ($+0.59$~pp worse with CKA in the model).

\textbf{One disclosed deviation from the registration, and it is a dispersion
measure, not the deciding statistic.}  The registration said the cluster bootstrap
would be reported on $\Delta R^2$ itself.  It cannot usefully be: an out-of-sample
$R^2$ is unbounded below, so a draw that duplicates clusters and leaves a one-cell
fold can score an arbitrarily large negative value, and the $2.5$th percentile of
$\Delta R^2$ came out at $-3.3$, $-1.7$ and $-32.5$ on bootstrap seeds 0, 1 and 7
while the point estimate, the median and the share of non-helping draws moved in
the third decimal.  Quoting that percentile would have put a number in this paper
that a reader re-running the script with another seed cannot reproduce; $\Delta$MAE's
upper bound is unstable for the same reason.  Both therefore carry a median and an
interquartile range, which are seed-stable, and the $95\%$ interval is quoted on the
one \emph{linear} coefficient in the analysis, CKA's slope in M3, $+43$~pp per unit
CKA with a cluster-bootstrap interval of $[-31, +107]$, whose bounds move by under
$5$~pp across seeds.  The registered \emph{deciding statistic}, the sign of
$\Delta R^2$, is unchanged.

% tables/cka_loco.tex carries its own table environment, caption and label.
\input{tables/cka_loco}

\textbf{What this does and does not establish.}  It does not establish that CKA is
uninformative about fine-tuning in general; it establishes that on these 31 cells,
CKA carries no incremental out-of-sample information about the value of encoder
adaptation once backbone identity, horizon and training-set size are known, and
that the part of $\denc$ that \emph{is} predictable is predictable from protocol
identity rather than from the representation-similarity measurement.  That is a
sharper statement than a wide slope interval, and it is the sense in which a
post-treatment similarity statistic fails to substitute for running the
intervention.  It is also worth naming what the ladder shows about the alternative:
the interaction model M4 collapses to $+0.019$, barely better than the intercept,
so ``CKA predicts $\denc$ but with a backbone-specific slope'' is the worst
performing account of these data, not a rescue of the pooled correlation.

\subsection{The gate's linear baseline was measured wrongly}
\label{app:gatecorrection}

\textbf{Two estimators, one name.} The value gate is
$\Vb{\text{Linear}} = 1 - \text{MSE}_\text{ZS}/\text{MSE}_\text{Linear}$
with threshold $0.20$, and \S\ref{sec:background} defines
$\text{MSE}_\text{Linear}$ as a lookback-96 linear regression \emph{fit on that
same set}: a single ridge-OLS map from $96{\times}D$ inputs to $h{\times}D$
outputs, estimated once on the cell's training windows and applied unchanged
out of sample. That estimator is
\texttt{gate\_linear\_baseline.fit\_linear\_map} (applied by
\texttt{apply\_linear\_map}). The gate was not computed with it. Through
26~Aug~2026 the denominator came from
\texttt{gate\_linear\_baseline.linear\_forecast}, which fits a slope and
intercept to the last 96 steps \emph{inside each evaluation window} and
extrapolates them forward, using no training data at all. These are two
different estimators sharing one label, not two implementations of one
estimator, and the paper defines the first while reporting the second.

\textbf{The estimator the gate used is weaker than a constant.}
Table~\ref{tab:gate_baselines} measures four denominators on the same 300
matched test windows on the train-normalised scale the stored zero-shot MSE is
measured on. The load-bearing row is the third: on ETTh1 $h{=}96$, simply
predicting the training mean scores $0.9937$ against the trend
extrapolation's $1.2207$. A baseline that a constant predictor beats cannot
support the inference the gate exists to license, namely that \emph{there is a
valuable pre-trained capability here worth preserving}, which is the sole
premise the degradation reading rests on. The defined estimator is stronger by
a factor of about three on ETTh1 and $9.4$ on Weather. Two independent checks
say the correction runs in the right direction: the fitted ETTh1 $h{=}96$ MSE
of $0.4145$ is in line with published multivariate Linear/DLinear results
\cite{zeng2023transformers}, and Moirai zero-shot losing to a supervised
linear model on ETTh1 is a known result, whereas $1.2207$ is not a physically
plausible score for any linear baseline on this cell. Note also an asymmetry
the definition imposes and which we retain: Moirai sees $\text{lookback}+h$
steps of context while the linear baseline sees $96$, so the corrected gate is
still a lower bound on the linear model's strength.

\subsection{Retraction: the probe-sign asymmetry axis}
\label{sec:retraction}

An earlier version of this work reported a second diagnostic axis alongside
CKA drift: a \emph{probe-sign asymmetry}, in which fine-tuning raised linear
decodability of the trained objective ($\Delta R^2 {>} 0$) while leaving
task-orthogonal probes flat or lower. We reported it as replicating across
every gate-passing cell. \textbf{We retract it.} It does not survive a
protocol with every choice fixed in advance, and nothing in this paper's
remaining claims depends on it.

\textbf{What fails.} The statistic reproduces numerically: the headline
$\Delta R^2{=}{+}0.67{\pm}0.24$ on Moirai-Small/ETTh2 at $n{=}10$k is
recoverable to four significant figures, but only in a regime where the
probe does not generalise. Under a temporal split the pre-trained probe
scores $R^2(\text{PT})$ between $-2.7$ and $-164.1$: it is not decoding the
target at all, so $\Delta R^2$ measures relative movement between two failed
fits. Re-running the identical probes, encoders and windows under a
randomised split, where all seven probes generalise
($R^2(\text{PT}) \in [0.678, 0.994]$), no $|\Delta R^2|$ exceeds $0.010$.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
Temporal (as originally reported) & $-164.1$ to $-2.7$ & $21.4$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The effect is therefore an artifact of probe failure, not evidence about the
encoder. It also fails our own published criterion for discarding a probe: we
stated that a probe with negative $R^2$ should not be read as evidence, and
then read one.

\textbf{What this costs, and what it does not.} Every claim resting on probe
asymmetry is withdrawn: the ``dissociation'' bullet in
\S\ref{sec:intro}, criterion~(i) of \S\ref{sec:background}, and the
per-cell $\Delta R^2$ readings in \S\ref{sec:exp3} and the appendix
tables, which we retain only as a record of the retracted computation.
Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} carried three such columns.  They no longer appear
there, and we transcribe them here instead, so that the withdrawal is legible
against what was printed rather than quietly absorbed into a regenerated table.
Unlike every other table in this paper these values are \textbf{not}
re-derivable by \texttt{scripts/rederive\_all.sh}: the probe records behind them
are scattered across result directories at one to three seeds per $n$, and we
are not going to write an emitter whose output is numbers we are telling the
reader not to use.  They are a transcript, they carry no dispersion where
$k{=}1$, and they support no claim.

\section{The Pre-Registered Positive Control, in Full}
\label{app:poscontrol}
% ============================================================
% The complete record of the arm S7.3 states in three paragraphs. Written so the arm can be read as a
% null without a reader having to trust us: the registration fixed all three outcome sentences and the
% destruction threshold before the first run, scripts/emit_positive_control.py quotes the sentence that
% fired rather than composing one, and Table \ref{tab:poscontrol} is generated from the 21 run records.

\textbf{What was registered, and when.}
% \allowbreak after the slash: the path is 45 characters of \texttt and TeX will not hyphenate it, so
% without a break point it overran \linewidth by 6.4pt and tripped the Overfull gate.
\texttt{results/positive\_control/\allowbreak preregistration.json} was committed at a named
revision before any run of this arm existed, and it fixed: task A, chosen from the
run records as $\arg\max\Vb{\text{ridge}}$ over the seven gate-passing cells
(Moirai-Small/ETTh2, $h{=}192$, $\Vb{\text{ridge}}{=}{+}0.932$); task B by
SHA-256, with its generator (\texttt{scripts/make\_conflicting\_series.py}); the
grid; the primary readout; \emph{which checkpoint is primary}; \emph{where CKA is
measured}; two falsifiable validity conditions; and all three outcome sentences.

\textbf{Task B, and why its construction matters.} $x_j(t) = a_j\,(-1)^{\lfloor
t/P\rfloor}\,s_j(t \bmod P) + \sigma_j\varepsilon$, with $s_j$ a three-harmonic
unit-variance periodic shape and $P{=}192{=}h$.  The correct forecast is
therefore the \emph{negation} of the previous period, so the seasonal-naive
prior (which is task A's own strongest admissible baseline rung) is maximally
wrong on task B: seasonal-naive loses to its own negation by $200.9\times$.  This
is what makes learning B a genuine conflict with A rather than the addition of an
unrelated skill, and it is the reason a null here is informative.

\textbf{Where CKA is measured, and why it differs from the main matrix.} On
\textbf{task A's} inputs, not task B's.  In the main matrix the two coincide,
because a cell trains and is evaluated on one series; here they cannot, and
measuring drift on the series being trained \emph{on} would answer a different
question than ``did the representation supporting A change''.  This was the
design choice we expected to be probed, so it is registered and stated rather
than buried.

\textbf{Both checkpoints, and the noise floor that makes them readable.} The
early-stopped state is primary, as registered; the final-epoch state is reported
beside it as the maximal-destruction bound, because the flag selects on task B's
validation loss and so can return a checkpoint from before damage to A has finished
developing.  \textbf{The reason the registration gave for that choice
(comparability with the main matrix, ``which early-stops the same way'') was
wrong}: the matrix passes no \texttt{-{}-early-stopping} flag and is read at its
final epoch (\S\ref{sec:method}).  We leave the registered choice and the
registration's wording as they are and report both states, so the error is in a
stated rationale and not in a result; the arm's own runs do restore the best
selection-split epoch, which is what the primary column is.  That epoch was
often the last one, so the two states are frequently \emph{identical}, and then the gap
between their two retention numbers is not redundancy but a direct read of the
evaluator's own sampling noise, since Moirai's forecast is the median of 20
samples.  Over the $17$ identical-state pairs that gap is at most $0.48$~pp and
$0.20$~pp on average.  \textbf{Every retention number in this arm should be read
against that floor}, which is why the emitter computes it across all such pairs
rather than asserting a tolerance.

\textbf{The validity conditions, both of which hold.} (i) Task B must actually be
learned; destruction without learning is weight thrashing, not forgetting. It
was, in \textbf{21 of 21} runs, by at least $+56.6\%$ against a registered $20\%$
threshold. (ii) The frozen encoder should preserve task A; if it does not, the
damage is not in the encoder. It does: retention stays inside $+5\%$ at all three
completed rungs ($+1.3\pm0.4$, $+4.2\pm2.2$, $+3.4\pm9.4$), against a destruction
threshold of $+50\%$.

\textbf{The outcome, quoted from the registration.} Destruction was not achieved:
the registered rule was retention$_A\!\geq\!50\%$ in a majority of seeds at some
rung under condition B, and no rung meets it: the per-rung maxima are $-7.1$,
$+26.7$, $+4.9$ and $-20.1$.  The registered sentence for this outcome reads:
``the positive control failed to construct\ldots deliberately adversarial
fine-tuning did not destroy the capability -- itself a finding about TSFM
robustness, and a reason the main matrix's null is unsurprising.''  Across all 21
runs Spearman $\rho$ between CKA on task A's inputs and retention is $+0.466$
($p{=}0.03$, $n{=}21$): a positive coefficient is the wrong sign for a
preservation heuristic, so the ordering fails here as it does retrospectively.

\textbf{The declared extension, and the three runs that diverged.} The stopping
rule permitted one extension to $\text{lr}{=}10^{-1}$ if destruction was not
achieved at $10^{-2}$, and then a stop.  Condition B completed all three seeds
and supplies the ladder's sharpest row.  Condition D diverged in 3 of 3: with the
encoder frozen every gradient lands on the decoder, and its likelihood head
leaves the domain its distribution requires: two seeds on the negative
binomial's \texttt{total\_count}, one on a categorical's \texttt{logits}.
\textbf{We do not re-run them.}  Guarding the evaluation call and retrying would
be re-running the declared extension after seeing its outcome, which is the thing
a pre-registration exists to prevent; the divergence is reported as the result
for that rung.  It is also not load-bearing: condition D's role is to localise
damage in the encoder, and there is no damage anywhere in the ladder to localise.

\textbf{What this arm cannot show}, as registered: that the capability at stake
was valuable against a strong baseline (task A beats seasonal-naive by only
$+0.081$, and does not clear the full admissible ladder, so this tests
\emph{detection} of a retention loss rather than the value of what is lost); that
anything here generalises beyond one backbone, one series and one synthetic task
B; or that a sign-inverting target task resembles a deployment scenario.  It also
cannot show that a retention loss is impossible on these benchmarks, only that
we could not construct one, having tried four learning-rate rungs spanning three
orders of magnitude with a task built to conflict.
