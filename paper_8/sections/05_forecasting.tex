% ============================================================
\section{Representation Drift Diagnosis: ETT Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecasting}
% ============================================================

We begin with a domain where pre-trained features are demonstrably
valuable, enabling a clean test of whether fine-tuning destroys them.
ETT time-series forecasting benchmarks~\cite{zhou2021informer} provide
this setting: zero-shot Moirai outperforms Linear baselines by 28--45\%
(Section~\ref{sec:forecasting:gate}).
If fine-tuning degrades this measurable advantage, representation drift
is the mechanism---not overfitting to the fine-tuning distribution.
The frozen-encoder control (same data, same epochs, CKA$=$1.000)
confirms this: overfitting would not be eliminated by freezing.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Domain Selection and Pre-Training Value Gate}
\label{sec:forecasting:gate}
% ============================================================

We evaluate on ETTh2~\cite{woo2024moirai}
(7 features, 17{,}420 timesteps, 12/4/4 month split).
Moirai-Small (13.8M parameters) zero-shot outperforms Linear by
28\% ($h{=}96$: 0.269 vs.\ 0.373) and 45\% ($h{=}192$: 0.299 vs.\ 0.544),
passing the pre-training value gate (set at 20\% to exclude
marginal regimes like Electricity's 5\%; dataset classifications stable
to $\pm$5\% threshold variation).

% ============================================================
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:forecasting:setup}
% ============================================================

We test: \textbf{B}~(NLL-only fine-tuning),
\textbf{C}~(NLL$+$SupCon with temporal clusters),
\textbf{D}~(frozen encoder), plus mitigations:
\textbf{E}~(LoRA, $r{=}8$)~\cite{hu2022lora},
\textbf{F}~(L2-SP)~\cite{li2018l2sp},
\textbf{G}~(EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc}.
Training: 500 samples, 20 epochs, AdamW (lr$=$1e-4);
B, C, D use 10 seeds, E/F/G use 5 seeds (Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum}, $n$ column).
Diagnosis: CKA and weight drift ($\ell_2$) between pre-trained
and fine-tuned encoders.

\subsection{Representation Drift Degrades High-Value Features}
\label{sec:forecasting:results}
% ============================================================

\input{tables/forecasting_forgetting}

Table~\ref{tab:forecasting_forgetting} shows the central result:
fine-tuning degrades ETTh2 performance where pre-trained features
are valuable.
B and C degrade by 10--16\% MSE ($p{<}0.001$, $d{=}1.23$;
paired bootstrap, 10{,}000 resamples) while training loss
\emph{decreases}---this is not overfitting (which would cause
validation loss to rise), but representation drift.
The frozen-encoder control (D, CKA$=$1.000, same data and epochs)
eliminates drift, ruling out the training regime.
Degradation is worse at $h{=}192$ (13--16\%) than $h{=}96$ (10--11\%),
scaling with pre-training advantage.
Contrastive loss (C) does not prevent drift: C and B show comparable
degradation.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Mitigation Spectrum}
\label{sec:forecasting:mitigation}
% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum} presents the mitigation spectrum on ETTh2.
Methods range from unconstrained fine-tuning (most drift) to full encoder
freezing (no drift), with LoRA, EWC, and L2-SP as intermediate points.

\input{tables/mitigation_spectrum}

\textbf{LoRA achieves the best trade-off on Moirai-Small.}
LoRA ($r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$) \emph{improves} over zero-shot
at $h{=}96$ while maintaining high CKA and zero weight drift
(low-rank updates do not modify base parameters).
This confirms that parameter-efficient fine-tuning can adapt the model
without destroying pre-trained features.
A rank sweep ($r \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$) shows consistent results across
ranks (CKA$\geq$0.979, MSE within $\pm$0.003; Appendix~\ref{app:lora_rank}).

\textbf{LoRA-Large needs a 10$\times$ smaller LR to rescue.}
The same LoRA recipe that succeeds on Moirai-Small fails on Moirai-Large
at the default $\mathrm{LR}{=}10^{-4}$ ($r{=}8$: forg.\ $+$25.7$\pm$3.2\%,
3 seeds); rank escalation ($r{\in}\{16,32,64\}$, seed 42) does
\emph{not} rescue ($+$22.5, $+$19.0, $+$29.9\%), nor do $\alpha$ or
target-module scope (Appendix~\ref{app:lora_rank}).
Reducing the LR by $10\times$ to $10^{-5}$ does rescue:
forg.$={-8.5}{\pm}0.7$\% at CKA$=$0.989${\pm}0.003$ (5~seeds on ETTh2).
The rescue transfers cross-dataset: on ETTh1
(forg.$={-16.7}{\pm}0.2$\%, 3~seeds) and ETTm2
(forg.$={-28.3}{\pm}0.4$\%, 3~seeds) at $n{=}500$,
confirming the LR recipe is not ETTh2-specific.
Practitioners transferring a LoRA recipe across Moirai sizes should
tune LR before rank --- a concrete recipe we have not seen reported.

\textbf{EWC and L2-SP reduce but do not eliminate drift.}
At stronger penalties, both EWC ($\lambda{=}1000$, CKA$=$0.972$\pm$0.004)
and L2-SP ($\lambda{=}0.1$, CKA$=$0.965$\pm$0.006) improve CKA over
unconstrained fine-tuning (B, CKA$=$0.937), but MSE remains
18--19\% above zero-shot.
Neither method matches LoRA's ability to adapt without drift.

\textbf{Encoder freezing eliminates drift at the cost of adaptation.}
Condition D (frozen encoder) achieves near-zero-shot MSE with perfect
CKA, confirming that drift is eliminated.
However, it cannot adapt to the fine-tuning distribution --- a
limitation that matters when pre-trained features are insufficient
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}, IoT negative control).

% ============================================================
\subsection{Replication Across Datasets and Model Sizes}
\label{sec:forecasting:replication}
% ============================================================

Representation drift replicates across datasets and model sizes
(Table~\ref{tab:cross_domain}).  On ETTh1 at $n{=}500$, fine-tuning
causes drift (CKA$=$0.807$\pm$0.048) and modest degradation
($+$7.0$\pm$3.2\%); at $n{=}2{,}000$ (3 seeds),
ETTh1 still does \emph{not} resolve --- forg.$=+$9.2$\pm$5.0\%
(CKA$=$0.752$\pm$0.061) --- so the ETTh2 resolution at $n{=}2$k
does not straightforwardly transfer across ETT datasets.
On ETTm2 at $n{=}500$, drift (CKA$=$0.851$\pm$0.083) is accompanied
by \emph{improvement} ($-$13.3$\pm$6.1\%); at $n{=}2{,}000$ (3 seeds)
the improvement deepens to forg.$=-$20.1$\pm$5.1\%
(CKA$=$0.818$\pm$0.019).  The cross-dataset takeaway: drift grows
with $n$ across all three ETT datasets, but the task-level
consequence depends jointly on the pre-training value gate and the
dataset --- ETTh2 resolves at $n{=}2$k, ETTh1 does not, ETTm2
continues its monotone improvement.
Moirai-Base on ETTh2 shows aggressive drift (CKA$=$0.460$\pm$0.200)
with large improvement ($-$44.1$\pm$5.7\%), mirroring the IoT pattern.
Moirai-Large (311M) on ETTh2 at 500 samples exhibits substantial
drift (CKA$=$0.626$\pm$0.165) and forgetting ($+$10.5$\pm$9.5\%),
comparable to Moirai-Small; frozen-Large reduces forgetting to
$+$3.2$\pm$0.6\% (CKA$=$0.994), confirming the pattern holds
across model sizes (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
An extended sample-size sweep (Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep},
$n{\in}\{500,1\mathrm{k},2\mathrm{k},5\mathrm{k},10\mathrm{k}\}$)
reveals that \emph{drift and forgetting are dissociable}.
Drift grows monotonically with $n$ (CKA from 0.949 at 500
to 0.407 at 10{,}000; weight drift from 3.7 to 17.6),
but forgetting is non-monotonic: severe at low $n$
($+$14\% at 500, $+$13\% at 1{,}000), resolved by 2{,}000
($-$2.9\%), bimodal at 5{,}000 ($+$6.6$\pm$10.0\%, 7 seeds;
per-seed breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:probing}), and at
10{,}000 \emph{predominantly negative} under the deterministic
early-stopped protocol: $-5.3{\pm}6.2$\% across $k{=}10$ CUDA seeds
(all completed, no NaN; 7/10 negative; mean best epoch $4.2{\pm}1.4$;
Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep}).
Bootstrap 95\% CI on the $k{=}10$ forgetting: [$-9.6$\%, $-1.0$\%];
7/10 seeds negative; the $\Delta R^2{>}0$ majority is more
robust (\textbf{10/10 seeds positive}).
The three positive-forgetting seeds (123: $+$1.8\%, 456: $+$4.4\%,
789: $+$0.4\%) are all near-zero and follow the $n{=}5$k overshoot pattern
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:trajectory}).
A final-epoch $k{=}5$ run gave $+$7.5$\pm$8.2\%,
dominated by seed~101's late-epoch overshoot ($+$21.9\%;
val-MSE minimum at epoch~10 rising to epoch~20), which collapsed
to $-$20.7\% when restored to the best-val-MSE checkpoint at
epoch~4 --- exactly as the $n{=}5$k trajectory recipe predicts
(\S\ref{sec:forecasting:trajectory}).
Linear probing (Ridge on frozen encoder representations, held-out
ETTh2; Appendix~\ref{app:probing}) shows $\Delta R^2$ (FT$-$PT)
\emph{increasing} with $n$: $+$0.12 at $n{=}500$ to
$+$0.67$\pm$0.23 at $n{=}10$k on 10-seed CUDA deterministic encoders
(\textbf{10/10} seeds positive; independently replicated on AWS g5.xlarge,
all 10 seeds exit~0, results in Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
Geometric dissimilarity (CKA) and \emph{relative
linear-decodability} ($\Delta R^2$) move in opposite directions.
Absolute $R^2(\text{FT})$ remains \emph{negative} across every
sampled $n$ ($R^2_{\text{ZS}}{=}-6.90$), so $\Delta R^2$ is a
relative-improvement signal; the primary task-outcome signal
remains forg.$<0$ at $n{\geq}2$k.
An MLP probe ($\mathrm{hidden}{=}64$ per layer, $\alpha{=}10^{-3}$,
early stopping) on the CUDA 10-seed early-stopped encoders at $n{=}10$k
gives $\Delta R^2 {=} +0.70{\pm}0.32$ at $k{=}5$ hidden layers
(\textbf{10/10} positive) ---
the MLP result is consistent with and independently confirms the Ridge
result ($+$0.67$\pm$0.23).  For comparison, an earlier MPS $k{=}5$
hidden-layer run on 4 seeds gave $+0.81{\pm}0.18$.
Final-epoch MLP ($k{=}2$, 2 seeds) gave $+1.54{\pm}0.24$;
at $n{=}500$, $k{=}1$ MLP is $-0.26$,
consistent with probe noise at the smallest sample size
(Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
This reframes our headline: representation drift is harmful
specifically in the \emph{low-data fine-tuning regime}, which
is precisely where practitioners most need pre-trained features.
At sufficient scale, the replacement features are \emph{less
linearly misaligned} with the forecasting target than the
pre-trained ones, despite both remaining absolutely
linearly-insufficient.
The $n{=}5{,}000$ variance is bimodal; we unpack the mechanism
in \S\ref{sec:forecasting:trajectory}.

\textbf{Probe head with positive absolute floor.}
\label{sec:forecasting:probehead}
On the 96-step-ahead head both $R^2(\text{PT})$ and $R^2(\text{FT})$
sit below zero at every $n$, so $\Delta R^2$ there is a
\emph{relative} signal.  We re-probe the four saved early-stopped
encoders at $n{=}10$k using \texttt{scripts/reprobe\_saved\_encoders.py}
with a \emph{gradient-boosted probe} (GBM: 150 trees, HistGBM)
on three heads: the 96-step-ahead head (paper's primary), a
\emph{next-step-delta head} ($x_{t+1}-x_t$), and a
\emph{forecast48 head} (first 48 steps of the 96-step target,
a sub-horizon the encoder was not directly optimised for).
On the 96-step head, GBM gives $R^2_{\text{PT}}{=}{-}5.94$
(still negative).  On the delta1 head, GBM gives
$R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059{>}0$, establishing a positive-floor
head.  However, $\Delta R^2$ on delta1 is \emph{negative}
($-0.056{\pm}0.03$, 4 seeds): fine-tuning loses the pre-trained
next-step signal.
\textbf{GBM depth sensitivity.}  To confirm this is not a
capacity-overfitting artifact, we re-run the GBM probe at
depths 4 and 8 (same 4 seeds, \texttt{--gbm-depth} flag):
depth~4: $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.060{\pm}0.006$ (4/4 negative,
$R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.062$); depth~8: $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.052{\pm}0.006$
(4/4 negative, $R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.054$).
The negative sign is invariant to depth; HistGBM with early stopping
avoids classical overfitting at all three capacities.
The original depth-6 result ($\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.056$) is robust.
Note: $R^2(\text{PT})$ varies slightly across depths
($+$0.062 at depth~4, $+$0.059 at depth~6, $+$0.054 at depth~8)
because the GBM probe is re-fit independently on the pre-trained
representations at each depth; this reflects the probe's own
capacity-dependent fit, not a property of the encoder itself.
All three depths establish a positive PT floor.

\textbf{Horizon-sweep confirms head-specificity.}
Ridge $\Delta R^2$ across the same four early-stopped encoders:
\emph{forecast96} (trained head): $+$0.116$\pm$0.153;
\emph{forecast48} (untrained sub-horizon): $-$0.001$\pm$0.163;
\emph{delta1} (untrained; Ridge baseline $R^2_{\text{PT}}{=}{-}2.3$,
GBM baseline ${>}0$): $+$1.044$\pm$0.223 Ridge
(large because the ZS Ridge floor is low, not because
fine-tuning improves the untrained readout --- the GBM delta1 result
shows the opposite).
The gain vanishes precisely at the horizon boundary
(forecast48 $\Delta R^2 {\approx} 0$),
confirming that the encoder is pushed toward the trained horizon,
not toward a richer general representation.
Full per-seed table in Appendix~\ref{app:probing}.

\textbf{Task-specific decodability gain, not general representation improvement.}
This pattern is best interpreted as the encoder drifting
\emph{toward the fine-tuning objective} rather than developing
better general representations.  Fine-tuning on the 96-step NLL
objective imposes a \emph{geometric cost} (CKA$\downarrow$) while
yielding a \emph{task-specific benefit} ($\Delta R^2{>}0$ on the
trained head, $\Delta R^2{\leq}0$ on untrained heads).  The
untrained heads (delta1 GBM: $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.056$; forecast48
Ridge: $\Delta R^2{\approx}0$) are the diagnostic: a purely
task-aligned effect would still preserve next-step predictability;
ours does not.  The frozen-encoder control (condition D, 5 seeds, CKA$=$0.9993$\pm$0.0001)
gives $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.048{\pm}0.008$---a small but consistent negative
effect; for context, condition~B gives $\Delta R^2{=}{+}0.668{\pm}0.226$
on the same data, so the frozen-encoder $\Delta R^2$ is $14{\times}$ smaller
in magnitude, confirming the decodability gain is caused by encoder weight
updates specifically, not by the training regime or data distribution.
A top-3-layer partial-unfreeze already reproduces $\Delta R^2{>}0$ (3/3 seeds,
Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}), localising the gain to weight updates in the
top encoder layers specifically.
The \emph{parameterisation} of the update also matters: LoRA achieves
CKA$\approx$0.98 with task improvement, while top-3 full-layer unfreeze
achieves CKA$\approx$0.70 with slight forgetting ($+$1.3\%), suggesting
that low-rank weight changes preserve geometric similarity far more
effectively than full-rank updates to a subset of layers
(Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}).
Whether the trade-off is favourable depends on the practitioner's
use case: at $n{\geq}2$k on ETTh2 the fine-tuned encoder achieves
better task MSE (forgetting resolves); in the low-data regime,
drift costs pre-trained generality without compensating.
Per-seed probe$\times$head matrix in Appendix~\ref{app:probing}.

\textbf{Robustness to checkpointing protocol.}
Early-stopped CKA at $n{=}10$k is $0.461{\pm}0.232$ versus
$0.407{\pm}0.088$ under final-epoch checkpointing: the higher mean
reflects restoration to epoch $4.2{\pm}2.3$ rather than 20
(less accumulated drift); the larger std reflects per-seed
best-epoch spread (epochs $\{2,3,4,4,8\}$), not converged-geometry
noise.  Both protocols remain firmly drifted (CKA $\ll 0.98$,
the LoRA-Small ceiling) and give positive Ridge $\Delta R^2$ in
every seed individually.  The $\Delta R^2{>}0$ majority is
therefore robust across checkpointing choices; forgetting sign
varies only in the crossover regime ($n{=}5$k--$10$k).

\textbf{Second cell: ETTm2 at $n{=}10$k confirms per-seed invariant.}
On ETTm2 ($h{=}96$, $n{=}10$k, condition B), the cost--benefit gap
holds in a second cell with a different outcome: fine-tuning
\emph{improves} task MSE (forg.$=-28.2{\pm}2.2$\%, 10/10 negative)
while CKA falls to $0.491{\pm}0.143$ --- drift occurs even when
forgetting is monotonically beneficial.
Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}5.43{\pm}2.57$ (\textbf{10/10} positive); the large magnitude reflects the deeper ZS
Ridge floor on ETTm2 ($R^2(\text{ZS}){=}-24.52$, vs.\ $-6.90$ on
ETTh2 --- a 3.5$\times$ deeper floor that proportionally inflates raw
$\Delta R^2$).  The cross-dataset comparable statistic is the
\emph{sign test}, which gives \textbf{10/10} positive in both cells.
Absolute R$^2$ per seed: R$^2$(ZS)$= -24.52$ (constant, pre-trained
encoder), R$^2$(FT) ranging from $-20.83$ to $-14.13$ across the
five available seeds (full CUDA k=10 absolute values in
Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
This rules out that the CKA-probe gap is an artefact of the
specific forgetting direction: the CKA$\downarrow$,
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ per-seed pattern holds on both a forgetting
dataset (ETTh2) and an improving dataset (ETTm2).
ETTh1 at $n{=}10$k (CUDA $k{=}10$) gives a third
pattern: forgetting resolves ($-6.1{\pm}3.6$\%, 10/10 negative) but
Ridge $\Delta R^2$ is mostly negative ($-4.23{\pm}4.47$, 2/10 positive)
--- task improves without a linear decodability gain.
This narrows the per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ invariant to ETTh2 and
ETTm2; ETTh1 represents a task-improvement-without-decodability-gain
regime and is discussed in \S\ref{sec:analysis}.

\textbf{Non-ETT and second-backbone gate checks.}
Autoformer Weather gate-\emph{fails} (ZS 0.410 vs.\ Linear 0.218);
Autoformer Electricity is \emph{marginal} (ZS 0.102 vs.\ 0.097, 5\%);
with little to forget, fine-tuning replaces features (forg.\ $-$8.5\%
at $n{=}500$, $-$24.4\% at $n{=}2$k) --- a regime delimiter, not a
dissociation replication.
Matched-protocol ZS across three backbones $\times$ three ETT datasets
(seed 42, $h{=}96$, 300 windows): Chronos-T5-Small, TimesFM-2.5-200M,
and MOMENT-1-base all gate-fail ($-$14\% to $-$148\% vs.\ Linear).
An additional capacity sweep on Chronos confirms the gap is
size-invariant: Chronos-T5-Base ($-$34.3\%) and Chronos-T5-Large
($-$35.2\%) both gate-fail on ETTh2 at $h{=}96$.
Nine non-Moirai backbone${\times}$ETT cells tested (seven original
plus two additional Chronos capacity cells), all failing;
the CKA-probe gap is established on Moirai only
(full table Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
A gate-passing non-Moirai backbone on Traffic/ILI and MOMENT-1-large
are future-work priorities (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Trajectory Signature of Drift}
\label{sec:forecasting:trajectory}
% ============================================================

The $n{=}5{,}000$ crossover variance (Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep},
$+$6.6$\pm$10.0\% across 7 seeds) is not uniform noise but a
\emph{bimodal} split with a clean trajectory signature.
Seeds $\{42,101,123,202\}$ cluster at forg.\ $-$0.1$\pm$3.7\% with
val-MSE epoch-of-minimum $6.5\pm2.1$; seeds $\{303,456,789\}$ cluster
at $+$15.5$\pm$7.3\% with epoch-of-minimum $0.7\pm0.5$ ---
high-forg.\ seeds overshoot in epoch~1 and never recover (per-seed
trajectories in Appendix~\ref{app:n5k_trajectories},
Fig.~\ref{fig:n5k_trajectories}).
An LR/2 ablation preserves bimodality ($+$5.8$\pm$15.7\%, 7 seeds),
so the effect is not a pure learning-rate artefact.
\textbf{Recipe.} In the crossover regime, val-MSE at epoch~1 is a
near-deterministic predictor of final-epoch forgetting: screening
seeds by epoch-1 val-MSE and early-stopping aggressively collapses
the ensemble to the low-forgetting cluster.  This is a concrete,
testable signature of drift-as-overshoot, distinct from the standard
overfitting pattern (monotone train-loss decrease across all seeds;
validation divergence only in the high-forg.\ cluster).
\textbf{Held-out validation.}
We LOO-validate the recipe: a threshold-free predicate
(\texttt{ep1 val-MSE $>$ ZS val-MSE}) gives 5/7 correct cluster assignments;
a trajectory-aware predicate (argmin epoch-of-val-MSE $<$ 3) gives 7/7
at $n{=}5$k but is sufficient-not-necessary at $n{=}10$k (where
best-val-MSE early-stopping alone achieves negative forgetting in all seeds).
Sample-dependent threshold calibration is future work.
\textbf{Mechanistic conjecture.}
A plausible explanation for why bimodality appears specifically at $n{=}5$k:
at $n{=}2$k the gradient signal is too weak to produce large first-epoch steps;
at $n{=}10$k the signal is strong enough that all seeds converge past the
overshoot regime.
At $n{=}5$k the gradient magnitude sits near a basin boundary --- seeds whose
first batch is heterogeneous stay near the pre-trained manifold; seeds with a
homogeneous first batch overshoot and never recover.
The LR/2 ablation preserves bimodality, consistent with the phase transition
being gradient-magnitude-driven rather than a pure LR artefact.
Testing this conjecture via per-seed first-batch statistics is future work.
