\section{Representation Drift Diagnosis: ETT Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecasting}
% ============================================================

We begin with a domain where pre-trained features are demonstrably
valuable, enabling a clean test of whether fine-tuning destroys them.
ETT time-series forecasting benchmarks~\cite{zhou2021informer} provide
this setting: zero-shot Moirai outperforms Linear baselines by 28--45\%
(Section~\ref{sec:forecasting:gate}).
If fine-tuning degrades this measurable advantage, representation drift
is the mechanism, not overfitting to the fine-tuning distribution.
The frozen-encoder control (same data, same epochs, CKA$=$1.000)
confirms this: overfitting would not be eliminated by freezing.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Value Gate}
\label{sec:forecasting:setup}
\label{sec:forecasting:gate}
% ============================================================

We evaluate on ETTh2 (7 features, 17k timesteps, 12/4/4 month split).
Moirai-Small (13.8M) zero-shot outperforms Linear by 28--45\%
(ZS MSE: 0.126 at $h{=}96$, 0.158 at $h{=}192$; un-normalised gate-pass
scale: 0.269 vs.\ 0.373 at $h{=}96$, 0.299 vs.\ 0.544 at $h{=}192$),
passing the value gate (20\% threshold).
The 20\% threshold is a practical screen, not a phase boundary: at
10--20\%, drift diagnosis applies but marginal features reduce both
harm from drift and benefit from mitigation.
Conditions: \textbf{B}~(NLL-only), \textbf{C}~(NLL$+$SupCon),
\textbf{D}~(frozen encoder), \textbf{E}~(LoRA $r{=}8$),
\textbf{F}~(L2-SP), \textbf{G}~(EWC).
Training: $n{=}500$ samples, 20 epochs, AdamW (lr$=$1e-4), 10 seeds
(B/C/D) or 5 seeds (E/F/G).
Diagnosis: CKA and weight drift ($\ell_2$) between pre-trained/fine-tuned encoders.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Representation Drift Degrades High-Value Features}
\label{sec:forecasting:results}
% ============================================================

Fine-tuning degrades ETTh2 performance where pre-trained features are
valuable (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:forecasting_forgetting}).
Conditions B (NLL-only) and C (NLL+SupCon) degrade by 10--16\% MSE
(10/10 seeds positive, $p{=}0.002$ sign test; $d{=}1.23$, 95\%~CI $[0.37,\,2.01]$;
bootstrap $p{<}10^{-4}$) while
training loss \emph{decreases}, not overfitting (which raises validation
loss), but representation drift.
The frozen-encoder control (D, CKA$=$1.000, same data/epochs) eliminates
drift, ruling out the training regime.
Degradation scales with horizon: worse at $h{=}192$ (13--16\%) than $h{=}96$
(10--11\%).
Contrastive loss (C) does not prevent drift: C ($+$10.1\%) and B ($+$11.1\%) differ by
1.0\%, within seed standard deviation; a null result for the contrastive objective.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Mitigation Spectrum}
\label{sec:forecasting:mitigation}
% ============================================================

We evaluate five mitigation strategies spanning unconstrained fine-tuning
(most drift) to full freezing (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum}).

\textbf{LoRA achieves the best trade-off.}
LoRA ($r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$) \emph{improves} over zero-shot at $h{=}96$
while maintaining high CKA (0.979) and zero weight drift.
On Moirai-Large the same recipe fails at default LR ($+$25.7\%, rank
escalation does not rescue) but a $10\times$ LR reduction does
(forg.$={-}8.5$\%, CKA$=$0.989), transferring to ETTh1/ETTm2 at $n{=}500$:
\textbf{tune LR before rank} across model sizes.
By contrast, EWC and L2-SP reduce but do not eliminate drift, and buy no
reliable utility: matched at $n{=}1000$ and $h{=}96$, each measured against
its own zero-shot, they land at $+13.4$ to $+16.0\%$ above ZS against full
fine-tuning's $+13.4\%$---one worse, one a dead heat at markedly higher CKA
($.965$ vs $.936$), so anchoring is \emph{not} uniformly harmful, merely
uninformative. (An earlier version quoted $18$--$19\%$ here, from a table
that divides by a single shared zero-shot and mixes $n{=}500$ and $n{=}1000$
rows; see the caption of Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum}.) The frozen
encoder eliminates drift (CKA$=$1.000) but cannot adapt when pre-trained
features are insufficient.

\textbf{Why run the diagnostic if LoRA is the best mitigation?}
Because the diagnostic decides \emph{whether preservation is worth doing at
all}, and reveals when LoRA itself fails.  (i)~On gate-fail cells (IoT,
ETTm2) there is nothing to preserve; full fine-tuning is fine and applying
LoRA/EWC to protect worthless features is wasted effort---the value-gate is
what tells the practitioner to skip preservation entirely.  (ii)~LoRA is
\emph{not} uniformly safe: LoRA-Large fails at the default LR ($+25.7\%$) and
only the capacity/step-size diagnosis surfaced the $10\times$ LR fix.  So
the diagnostic and the mitigation are not competitors---the diagnostic is
the \emph{decision procedure} (preserve? which regime? which LR?) and LoRA is
the recommended \emph{action} once the diagnostic says preservation is
warranted.

\subsection{Sample-Size Sweep: Drift--Forgetting Dynamics}
\label{sec:forecasting:replication}
% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} ($n{\in}\{500,\ldots,10\mathrm{k}\}$, ETTh2)
reveals that \emph{drift and forgetting are dissociable}, a finding best
illustrated by comparing Moirai-Base (protocol-robust improvement of
50--57\%, 10/10 seeds, despite CKA$\approx$0.25; \S\ref{sec:analysis}) with Small, where the same
mechanism produces capacity-dependent outcomes.
On Small, drift grows monotonically with $n$ (CKA from 0.949 at 500
to 0.518 at 10k), but forgetting is non-monotonic: severe at low $n$
($+$14\% at 500), resolved by 2k ($-$2.9\%), predominantly negative
at 10k ($-5.3{\pm}6.2$\%, 7/10 negative, 10-seed CUDA deterministic).
The $n{=}5$k variance ($+$6.6$\pm$10.0\%, 7 seeds) is bimodal: 4 seeds
cluster at $-$0.1\%, 3 at $+$15.5\% with epoch-1 overshoot (full
trajectory analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:n5k_trajectories}).
Drift resolution at high $n$ is protocol-dependent for Small (final-epoch
$-0.6$\%, n.s.) but protocol-robust for Base (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).

\textbf{Headline:} drift is capacity-dependent: harmful on Small at
$n{\leq}1$k (where practitioners most need pre-trained features),
resolved by larger capacity (Base improves at all $n$, protocol-robust).

\textbf{The insight in Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep}} is not any single row
but the \emph{divergence of two columns}: CKA falls monotonically as $n$
grows (drift strictly increases), yet forgetting is non-monotonic and
reverses sign (harm at low $n$, resolution at high $n$).  If drift
magnitude predicted harm, these columns would track; they do not.
This is the drift--utility dissociation in a single table, and it is why a
practitioner cannot read task risk off a CKA number---the decision needs
the value-gate and the sample-size regime, not the drift magnitude alone.

\textbf{Task-native head: the task score that survives.}
\label{sec:forecasting:probehead}
Using Moirai's own forecasting head gives a well-defined positive-floor task
score, $R^2_{\text{task}}(\text{PT}) = 1 - \text{MSE}_{\text{ZS}}/\text{MSE}_{\text{Linear}}
= 1 - 0.269/0.373 = +0.279$ at $h{=}96$ ($+0.450$ at $h{=}192$).
After fine-tuning ($n{=}10$k, ES) it improves to
$\text{MSE}_{\text{FT}}{=}0.122{\pm}0.006$ vs.\ $0.126$ ZS
($\Delta R^2_{\text{task}}{>}0$ in 7/10 seeds; Table~\ref{tab:r2task}):
the geometry shift is functionally beneficial at high $n$, harmful at low
$n$ (forgetting $+$14\% at $n{=}500$). This quantity is a forecast-MSE ratio,
not a linear-probe score, and is unaffected by the retraction below.
