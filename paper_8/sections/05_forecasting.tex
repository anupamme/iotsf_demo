% ============================================================
\section{Representation Drift Diagnosis: ETT Forecasting}
\label{sec:forecasting}
% ============================================================

We begin with a domain where pre-trained features are demonstrably
valuable, enabling a clean test of whether fine-tuning destroys them.
ETT time-series forecasting benchmarks~\cite{zhou2021informer} provide
this setting: zero-shot Moirai outperforms Linear baselines by 28--45\%
(Section~\ref{sec:forecasting:gate}).
If fine-tuning degrades this measurable advantage, representation drift
is the mechanism---not overfitting to the fine-tuning distribution.
The frozen-encoder control (same data, same epochs, CKA$=$1.000)
confirms this: overfitting would not be eliminated by freezing.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:forecasting:setup}
% ============================================================

We evaluate on ETTh2 (7 features, 17k timesteps, 12/4/4 month split).
Moirai-Small (13.8M) zero-shot outperforms Linear by 28--45\%
(ZS MSE: 0.126 at $h{=}96$, 0.158 at $h{=}192$), passing the
value gate (20\% threshold).
Conditions: \textbf{B}~(NLL-only), \textbf{C}~(NLL$+$SupCon),
\textbf{D}~(frozen encoder), \textbf{E}~(LoRA $r{=}8$),
\textbf{F}~(L2-SP), \textbf{G}~(EWC).
Training: $n{=}500$ samples, 20 epochs, AdamW (lr$=$1e-4), 10 seeds
(B/C/D) or 5 seeds (E/F/G).
Diagnosis: CKA and weight drift ($\ell_2$) between pre-trained/fine-tuned encoders.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Representation Drift Degrades High-Value Features}
\label{sec:forecasting:results}
% ============================================================

Fine-tuning degrades ETTh2 performance where pre-trained features are
valuable (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:forecasting_forgetting}).
Conditions B (NLL-only) and C (NLL+SupCon) degrade by 10--16\% MSE
($p{<}0.001$, $d{=}1.23$; paired bootstrap, 10{,}000 resamples) while
training loss \emph{decreases}---not overfitting (which raises validation
loss), but representation drift.
The frozen-encoder control (D, CKA$=$1.000, same data/epochs) eliminates
drift, ruling out the training regime.
Degradation scales with horizon: worse at $h{=}192$ (13--16\%) than $h{=}96$
(10--11\%).
Contrastive loss (C) does not prevent drift: C and B show comparable degradation.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Mitigation Spectrum}
\label{sec:forecasting:mitigation}
% ============================================================

We evaluate five mitigation strategies (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:mitigation_spectrum}):
LoRA (E), frozen encoder (D), EWC (G), L2-SP (F), and contrastive loss (C).
Methods range from unconstrained fine-tuning (most drift) to full encoder
freezing (no drift).

\textbf{LoRA achieves the best trade-off.}
LoRA ($r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$) \emph{improves} over zero-shot at $h{=}96$
while maintaining high CKA (0.979) and zero weight drift.
On Moirai-Large, the same recipe fails at default $\mathrm{LR}{=}10^{-4}$
($+$25.7$\pm$3.2\%, 3 seeds); rank escalation does \emph{not} rescue.
Reducing LR by $10\times$ to $10^{-5}$ does rescue: forg.$={-8.5}{\pm}0.7$\%
(CKA$=$0.989, 5 seeds), transferring cross-dataset to ETTh1/ETTm2 at $n{=}500$.
\textbf{Practitioner recipe:} tune LR before rank when adapting LoRA across
model sizes.

\textbf{EWC and L2-SP reduce but do not eliminate drift.}
Both improve CKA over unconstrained fine-tuning but MSE remains 18--19\%
above zero-shot.
Frozen encoder (D) eliminates drift (CKA$=$1.000) but cannot adapt---a
limitation when pre-trained features are insufficient.

% ============================================================
\subsection{Sample-Size Sweep: Drift-Forgetting Dissociation}
\label{sec:forecasting:replication}
% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:sample_sweep} ($n{\in}\{500,1\mathrm{k},2\mathrm{k},5\mathrm{k},10\mathrm{k}\}$, ETTh2)
reveals that \emph{drift and forgetting are dissociable}.
Drift grows monotonically with $n$ (CKA from 0.949 at 500 to 0.518
at 10k), but forgetting is non-monotonic: severe at low $n$ ($+$14\%
at 500), resolved by 2k ($-$2.9\%), predominantly negative at 10k
($-5.3{\pm}6.2$\%, 7/10 negative, 10-seed CUDA deterministic).
Linear probing shows $\Delta R^2$ \emph{increasing} with $n$:
$+$0.12 at 500 to $+$0.67$\pm$0.23 at 10k (\textbf{10/10} seeds positive).
Geometric dissimilarity (CKA$\downarrow$) and relative
linear-decodability ($\Delta R^2\uparrow$) move in opposite directions.

Cross-dataset (Table~\ref{tab:cross_domain}): ETTh1 at $n{=}500$
shows drift (CKA$=$0.807) and modest forgetting ($+$7.0\%); ETTm2 shows
drift (CKA$=$0.851) with \emph{improvement} ($-$13.3\%).
Moirai-Base/Large at $n{=}500$ exhibit drift comparable to Small
(detailed in \S\ref{sec:analysis}).
Absolute $R^2(\text{FT})$ remains negative across all $n$
($R^2_{\text{ZS}}{=}-6.90$), so $\Delta R^2$ is a relative-improvement
signal (Appendix~\ref{app:probing} for full probe analysis).
MLP probe ($k{=}5$ hidden layers) on CUDA 10-seed encoders at $n{=}10$k
gives $\Delta R^2 {=} +0.70{\pm}0.32$ (\textbf{10/10} positive),
confirming Ridge result ($+$0.67$\pm$0.23).
\textbf{Headline:} drift is harmful in the low-data regime
($n{\leq}1$k), where practitioners most need pre-trained features.

\textbf{Head-specificity.}
\label{sec:forecasting:probehead}
On 96-step-ahead (trained head), $R^2(\text{PT/FT}) < 0$ at all $n$;
$\Delta R^2$ is a relative signal.
Gradient-boosted probe (GBM, 150 trees) on delta1 head ($x_{t+1}-x_t$)
gives $R^2(\text{PT}){=}{+}0.059$ (positive floor) but
$\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.056$ (4 seeds): fine-tuning loses pre-trained
next-step signal, confirming benefit is task-specific.
Ridge horizon-sweep: forecast96 (trained) $\Delta R^2{=}{+}0.116$,
forecast48 (untrained) ${\approx}0$.
Gain vanishes at horizon boundary (full details in
Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).

\textbf{Task-specific shift, not general improvement.}
Fine-tuning imposes geometric cost (CKA$\downarrow$) with task-specific
benefit ($\Delta R^2{>}0$ on trained head, ${\leq}0$ on untrained).
Three controls confirm this is encoder-specific restructuring:
(1)~frozen-encoder (D, CKA$=$0.9993) gives $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.048{\pm}0.008$,
14$\times$ smaller than condition B ($+0.668{\pm}0.226$);
(2)~random-init encoder gives $\Delta R^2{=}{-}0.199{\pm}0.205$
(4/5 negative, Appendix~\ref{app:randominit}), confirming pre-trained
structure is required; (3)~top-3-layer unfreeze reproduces $\Delta R^2{>}0$
(3/3 seeds), localising gain to top layers (Appendix~\ref{app:layerunfreeze}).
LoRA achieves CKA$\approx$0.98 with task improvement; top-3 unfreeze gives
CKA$\approx$0.70 with slight forgetting, showing low-rank updates preserve
geometry more effectively.

The $\Delta R^2{>}0$ signal is protocol-robust (holds under
early-stopped and final-epoch checkpointing); forgetting sign varies
in the crossover regime ($n{=}5$k--$10$k).
ETTm2 at $n{=}10$k confirms per-seed invariant: CKA$=$0.491,
Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}5.43{\pm}2.57$ (10/10 positive),
forg.$=-28.2{\pm}2.2$\% (10/10 negative)
while CKA falls to $0.491{\pm}0.143$ --- drift occurs even when
forgetting is monotonically beneficial.
Ridge $\Delta R^2{=}{+}5.43{\pm}2.57$ (\textbf{10/10} positive); the large magnitude reflects the deeper ZS
Ridge floor on ETTm2 ($R^2(\text{ZS}){=}-24.52$, vs.\ $-6.90$ on
ETTh2 --- a 3.5$\times$ deeper floor that proportionally inflates raw
$\Delta R^2$).  The cross-dataset comparable statistic is the
\emph{sign test}, which gives \textbf{10/10} positive in both cells.
Absolute R$^2$ per seed: R$^2$(ZS)$= -24.52$ (constant, pre-trained
encoder), R$^2$(FT) ranging from $-20.83$ to $-14.13$ across the
five available seeds (full CUDA k=10 absolute values in
Appendix~\ref{app:probing}).
This rules out that the CKA-probe gap is an artefact of the
specific forgetting direction: the CKA$\downarrow$,
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ per-seed pattern holds on both a forgetting
dataset (ETTh2) and an improving dataset (ETTm2).
ETTh1 at $n{=}10$k (CUDA $k{=}10$) gives a third
pattern: forgetting resolves ($-6.1{\pm}3.6$\%, 10/10 negative) but
Ridge $\Delta R^2$ is mostly negative ($-4.23{\pm}4.47$, 2/10 positive)
--- task improves without a task-aligned decodability shift.
This narrows the per-seed $\Delta R^2{>}0$ invariant to ETTh2 and
ETTm2; ETTh1 represents a task-improvement-without-decodability-gain
regime and is discussed in \S\ref{sec:analysis}.

\textbf{Non-ETT and second-backbone gate checks.}
Autoformer Weather gate-\emph{fails} (ZS 0.410 vs.\ Linear 0.218);
Autoformer Electricity is \emph{marginal} (ZS 0.102 vs.\ 0.097, 5\%);
with little to forget, fine-tuning replaces features (forg.\ $-$8.5\%
at $n{=}500$, $-$24.4\% at $n{=}2$k) --- a regime delimiter, not a
dissociation replication.
Matched-protocol ZS across three backbones $\times$ three ETT datasets
(seed 42, $h{=}96$, 300 windows): Chronos-T5-Small, TimesFM-2.5-200M,
and MOMENT-1-base all gate-fail ($-$14\% to $-$148\% vs.\ Linear).
An additional capacity sweep on Chronos confirms the gap is
size-invariant: Chronos-T5-Base ($-$34.3\%) and Chronos-T5-Large
($-$35.2\%) both gate-fail on ETTh2 at $h{=}96$.
Nine non-Moirai backbone${\times}$ETT cells tested (seven original
plus two additional Chronos capacity cells), all failing;
the CKA-probe gap is established on Moirai only
(full table Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
A gate-passing non-Moirai backbone on Traffic/ILI and MOMENT-1-large
are future-work priorities (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).

The $n{=}5$k variance ($+$6.6$\pm$10.0\%, 7 seeds) is bimodal: 4 seeds
cluster at $-$0.1\%, 3 at $+$15.5\% with epoch-1 overshoot (full
trajectory analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:n5k_trajectories}).
