\begin{abstract}
We contribute a \textbf{reusable diagnostic methodology} for assessing
representation drift during fine-tuning of time-series foundation models:
a value-gate screening criterion, controlled-comparison design with
frozen-encoder/random-init/layer-wise-unfreeze controls, task-orthogonal
probe protocol, LoRA-Large LR recipe, uni2ts library patch, and
CUDA-deterministic reproducibility protocol.
We demonstrate the methodology on a \textbf{Moirai-ETT case study}
(the one backbone$\times$dataset family where the value-gate passes).
The load-bearing empirical signals are: (1) the \textbf{task-orthogonal
probe asymmetry} ($\Delta R^2{<}0$ on positive-floor probes,
$\Delta R^2{>}0$ on the trained head; 10/10 seeds on ETTh2 and ETTm2,
where ETTm2 is a gate-failing cross-domain comparison confirming the
mechanism generalises), operationalising ``task-specific shift''; and (2) \textbf{layer-wise
localisation} (top-3 layers suffice for $\Delta R^2{>}0$ but lower
layers required for task recovery; 10/10 seeds).
Fine-tuning imposes geometric cost (CKA $0.949{\to}0.52$ as
$n{:}500{\to}10$k) with protocol-dependent task outcome---harmful at
$n{\leq}1$k ($+$14\% forgetting), improving at $n{=}10$k under
early-stopped checkpointing.
Five non-Moirai backbones gate-fail on all nine ETT cells tested;
Moirai itself gate-fails on Traffic, Exchange, and Electricity,
narrowing the gate-pass to ETTh2 and confirming that corpus
composition drives coverage (\S\ref{sec:forecasting:nonmoirai}).
\textbf{Practitioner takeaway:} Use LoRA with $10{\times}$ LR reduction
on larger Moirai variants before escalating rank; apply the value-gate
before investing in drift diagnosis.
\end{abstract}
