\begin{abstract}
Do synthetic hard negatives improve contrastive anomaly detection for IoT
intrusion detection?  We investigate using \ours{} (\textbf{H}ard-\textbf{N}egative
\textbf{IDS}), a framework that synthesises stealth-controlled IoT attacks via
closed-form perturbations with protocol compliance (Modbus, MQTT, CoAP), and
fine-tunes Moirai~\cite{woo2024moirai} with combined NLL and supervised
contrastive loss.  A five-condition ablation at three stealth levels with
benign-calibrated thresholds isolates the contributions of hard negatives,
contrastive learning, and fine-tuning against nine IDS baselines.

\textbf{Our central finding is catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained features.}
At increased training scale, performance is non-monotonic: 500 samples
improves over 200 (suggesting the smallest regime is under-trained), but
degrades from 500 to 1000 samples as catastrophic forgetting erases
pre-trained features.  Freezing the encoder and
training only the projection head recovers AUC substantially, confirming that
extended fine-tuning destroys the pre-trained representations that provide
discriminative power---collapsing performance to from-scratch CNN level.
Freezing is a mitigation, not a complete solution: AUC recovers but
operational F1 under calibrated thresholds remains low due to
projection-head initialisation variance.

At small scale, Gaussian-noise negatives outperform hard negatives; at
5$\times$ scale with NLL-only early stopping this gap collapses to
near-zero---the advantage of easy negatives diminishes with sufficient
training data.  Cross-dataset validation on N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018n}
(AUC$=$\nbaiotAUC{}) confirms framework generalisation.
We release code, datasets, and calibration scripts.
\end{abstract}
