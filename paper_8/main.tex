\begin{abstract}
We contribute a \textbf{reusable diagnostic methodology} for assessing
representation drift during fine-tuning of time-series foundation models,
comprising a value-gate screening criterion, controlled-comparison design,
and task-orthogonal probe protocol.
On Moirai/ETTh2 (gate-pass 28\%), task-orthogonal probe asymmetry
($\Delta R^2{>}0$ on the trained head, ${<}0$ on positive-floor probes;
10/10 seeds) establishes that fine-tuning restructures toward the
objective rather than improving generally.
Restructuring is generally beneficial for gate-passing cells;
harm occurs only in the narrow low-$n$/small-capacity regime
(1 of 7 cells tested).
Cross-backbone validation on Chronos-T5-Small/M4-Monthly (gate-pass
84.5\%) confirms the value-gate as a regime indicator: strong gate-pass
predicts encoder stability (CKA${\geq}$0.95), no probe signal, and no
forgetting.
On ILI (gate-pass 57\%), full probes show restructuring with beneficial
outcome (CKA$=$0.478, $\Delta R^2{>}0$ in 10/10 seeds, forgetting
$-$80.7\%), extending the probe-asymmetry regularity to a third
gate-passing cell and revising the predicted stability threshold upward.
\textbf{Practitioner takeaway:} apply the value-gate before investing
in drift diagnosis; use LoRA with reduced LR on larger variants.
\end{abstract}
