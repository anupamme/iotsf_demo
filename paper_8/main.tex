\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning a time-series foundation model reshapes its internal
representations, and that reshaping is routinely read as catastrophic
forgetting to be prevented.  Preventing it presupposes something worth
preserving.  \textbf{We test that presupposition, and on the standard
benchmarks it mostly fails.}  Screening \textbf{32 held-out cells} across three
backbones (Moirai-S/B/L, Chronos-T5, TimesFM-2.5) and six datasets against a
lookback-96 linear regression \emph{fit on the target data}, only
\textbf{five} show the pre-trained model beating that baseline, and all five
are ETTh2; all three backbones lose to it on ETTh1, ETTm2, Weather and
Electricity, as does Moirai on ILI.  Against an eight-baseline ladder,
\textbf{no cell beats every admissible rung}.  On the five that pass, fine-tuning
\emph{improves} MSE by 31--42\% in three, and the two with mean harm carry
4/10 and 5/10 seed agreement.  \textbf{No cell meets our own definition of
damaging a demonstrated pre-trained capability}, so the value-gate we
pre-registered has no positive instance to predict: TP~0, FP~8, and precision
$0.00$ at every threshold from $0.10$ to $0.60$.
Two measurement findings show how easily this is missed.  First, the
frozen-encoder comparison must be scored on held-out windows---4 of 31 cells
otherwise reverse sign, every one of them flattering the frozen encoder.
Second, an earlier version of this work drew the opposite conclusion because
its linear baseline was an unfitted per-window trend extrapolation that a
constant predictor beats; correcting the estimator moves 16 of 21 Moirai cells
from gate-pass to gate-fail.
Across the 31 intervention cells the outcome still spans $-51.8\%$ to
$+46.1\%$ against zero-shot, and the magnitude of CKA-measured representation
change is not sufficient to predict whether allowing encoder adaptation helps or
hurts (within-backbone Spearman $\rho{=}{+}0.168$ against the frozen-encoder
contrast, clustered CI $[-0.469,{+}0.717]$---too wide to establish a reliable
relationship in either direction).  We additionally retract a probe-based diagnostic reported in an
earlier version.
\end{abstract}
