\begin{abstract}
We propose a \textbf{diagnostic protocol} for representation drift during
fine-tuning of time-series foundation models.
The protocol first tests whether zero-shot features are valuable relative
to a linear baseline (value-gate), then uses controlled encoder comparisons
and task-orthogonal probes to distinguish harmful forgetting from useful
restructuring.
In a Moirai case study spanning three model sizes and multiple datasets,
fine-tuning often restructures representations toward the downstream
objective, but the utility consequence depends on sample size and model
capacity: Moirai-Small degrades in low-data ETTh2, while Moirai-Base and
ILI fine-tuning improve despite substantial drift.
Cross-backbone experiments (Chronos-T5-Small on M4-Monthly) confirm that
the value-gate serves as a screening heuristic for when preservation-focused
drift diagnosis is warranted.
These results suggest that drift alone is insufficient evidence of harm;
practitioners should first establish pre-training value, then assess
whether drift affects task-relevant structure.
\end{abstract}
